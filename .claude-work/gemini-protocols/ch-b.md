# Batch CH-B

## Protocol: acute-asthma-child
- **Title:** Acute asthma in children
- **Summary:** Severity triage, oxygen, salbutamol plus ipratropium, systemic steroids, and IV magnesium for paediatric acute asthma.
- **Category:** Respiratory
- **Keywords:** `paediatric asthma`, `wheeze`, `salbutamol`, `ipratropium`, `prednisolone`, `magnesium sulfate`, `PEF`, `spacer`

### Step: Severity Triage & Oxygen
- **Timing:** Immediate on arrival
- **Points:**
  - Acute severe: SpO2 <92%, unable to talk/feed, accessory muscles, HR >140 (2–5y) or >125 (>5y), PEF 33–50% ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - Life-threatening: SpO2 <92% plus silent chest, poor respiratory effort, agitation, confusion, cyanosis, or PEF <33% ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - Give facemask oxygen to maintain SpO2 94–98% (GINA 2025: ≥94% in ≤5 y); use O2-driven nebulisers in severe attacks ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/); [GINA 2025 — Oxygen](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GINA-2025-Update-25_11_08-WMS.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/), [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/), [GINA 2025 — Oxygen](https://ginasthma.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/GINA-2025-Update-25_11_08-WMS.pdf)

### Step: First-Line Bronchodilators
- **Timing:** Repeat every 20 minutes × 3 in first hour
- **Points:**
  - Moderate attack: salbutamol pMDI + spacer (100 mcg/puff), 2–10 puffs q20min × 3 in the first hour ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/); [GINA 2026 Review — SABA](https://all-imm.com/index.php/aei/article/view/1790/2589)).
  - Severe/life-threatening: oxygen-driven nebulised salbutamol 2.5 mg (2–5 y) or 5 mg (>5 y) every 20–30 min ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - Add nebulised ipratropium bromide 250 mcg every 20 min × 3 doses in severe, life-threatening, or poorly responsive attacks ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 1a–1b](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
- **Citations:** [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/), [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/), [WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 1a–1b](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/), [GINA 2026 Review — SABA](https://all-imm.com/index.php/aei/article/view/1790/2589)

### Step: Systemic Corticosteroids
- **Timing:** Give within first hour of presentation
- **Points:**
  - Give oral prednisolone 1–2 mg/kg daily (max 40 mg; 20 mg for 2–5 y) for 3–5 days ([GINA 2026 Review — Steroids](https://all-imm.com/index.php/aei/article/view/1790/2589); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - If vomiting or unable to swallow, give IV hydrocortisone 4 mg/kg every 4–6 hours (max 100 mg) ([BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/); [WHO Pocket Book — §4.5.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154448/)).
- **Dose:** Prednisolone — acute asthma in children | 1–2 mg/kg per day PO for 3–5 days | max 40 mg | Cap at 20 mg (<2 y), 30 mg (2–5 y), 40 mg (>5 y)
- **Dose:** Hydrocortisone — acute asthma (unable to take oral) | 4 mg/kg per dose IV every 6 h | max 100 mg | Switch to oral prednisolone once tolerating oral intake
- **Citations:** [GINA 2026 Review — Steroids](https://all-imm.com/index.php/aei/article/view/1790/2589), [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/), [WHO Pocket Book — §4.5.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154448/)

### Step: Second-Line IV Therapy (Refractory / Life-Threatening)
- **Timing:** Escalate immediately if poor response to 1st-hour nebulisers
- **Points:**
  - Preferred 2nd-line: IV magnesium sulfate 40–50 mg/kg (max 2 g) diluted in saline over 20–30 min with BP monitoring ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 2, Rec 5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - Preferred 3rd-line in PICU: IV salbutamol 15 mcg/kg bolus, then 1–5 mcg/kg/min infusion (monitor ECG, K+, lactate) ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 4–5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
  - Avoid IV aminophylline if MgSO4/salbutamol available (fallback: 5 mg/kg over 20 min, then 1 mg/kg/h) ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 3, Rec 5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
- **Dose:** Magnesium sulfate — refractory/life-threatening paediatric asthma | 40–50 mg/kg per dose IV over 20–30 min | max 2 g | Dilute in 0.9% NaCl; monitor BP closely during infusion
- **Citations:** [WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 2–5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/), [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age >5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)

### Note
- WHO 2026 and GINA 2025 discourage nebulised magnesium sulfate; BTS/SIGN 158 still allows considering 150 mg nebulised in life-threatening attacks ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Rec 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [BTS/SIGN 158 — Age 2–5 y](https://www.rightdecisions.scot.nhs.uk/asthma-pathway-bts-nice-sign-sign-244/managing-acute-asthma/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children/asthma-management-algorithms-for-children/management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/age-2-5-years-management-of-acute-asthma-in-children-in-hospital/)).
- Reserve IM adrenaline strictly for anaphylaxis; WHO 2026 does not recommend subcutaneous adrenaline for routine acute asthma ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Recs](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/)).

## Protocol: bronchiolitis
- **Title:** Bronchiolitis
- **Summary:** Risk stratification, admission thresholds, oxygen and fluid support, and therapies to avoid in infants under 2 years.
- **Category:** Respiratory
- **Keywords:** `bronchiolitis`, `RSV`, `wheeze`, `apnoea`, `HFNC`, `CPAP`, `infant`

### Step: Recognition & Risk Stratification
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose clinically (<2 years, peak 3–6 mo): coryzal prodrome followed by cough, tachypnoea/recessions, and wheeze or crackles ([NICE NG9 — §1.1.1–1.1.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Young infants (<6 weeks) may present with apnoea alone; suspect pneumonia if fever >39 °C or persistently focal crackles ([NICE NG9 — §1.1.4–1.1.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Identify high-risk infants early: age <3 months, prematurity <32 weeks, congenital heart disease, chronic lung disease, neuromuscular disorder, or immunodeficiency ([NICE NG9 — §1.2.3, §1.3.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG9 — §1.1.1–1.1.6, §1.2.3, §1.3.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Admission & Red-Flag Criteria
- **Points:**
  - Admit immediately for apnoea, central cyanosis, severe distress (grunting, marked recessions, RR >70/min), or intake <50–75% normal ([NICE NG9 — §1.2.1, §1.3.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
  - NICE admission SpO2 cutoff: persistently <90% (age ≥6 weeks) or <92% (<6 weeks or underlying comorbidities) ([NICE NG9 — §1.3.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Escalate to PICU for exhaustion, recurrent apnoea, or failure to maintain oxygen saturation despite supplemental oxygen ([NICE NG9 — §1.1.10](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG9 — §1.1.10, §1.2.1, §1.3.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Supportive Oxygen, Fluids & Airway Care
- **Points:**
  - Give low-flow nasal oxygen to maintain SpO2 ≥90% (≥92% if <6 weeks or comorbidities) ([NICE NG9 — §1.4.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/)).
  - Escalate to humidified high-flow nasal cannula (HHFNC) or CPAP for persistent hypoxaemia or impending respiratory failure ([WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/); [NICE NG9 — §1.4.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Give NG/OG feeds if oral intake inadequate; if IV isotonic fluids required, restrict to 2/3 maintenance (SIADH risk) ([NICE NG9 — §1.4.11–1.4.12](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations); [RCH IV Fluids — Bronchiolitis](https://www.rch.org.au/clinicalguide/guideline_index/Intravenous_fluids/)).
  - Perform gentle superficial nasal suctioning only if secretions impair feeding/breathing, or in all infants presenting with apnoea ([NICE NG9 — §1.4.6–1.4.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG9 — §1.4.4–1.4.8, §1.4.11–1.4.12](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations), [WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/), [RCH IV Fluids — Bronchiolitis](https://www.rch.org.au/clinicalguide/guideline_index/Intravenous_fluids/)

### Step: What NOT to Give
- **Points:**
  - Do not routinely give salbutamol, ipratropium, corticosteroids, antibiotics, nebulised adrenaline, or montelukast ([NICE NG9 — §1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/)).
  - Do not perform routine chest X-ray, blood tests, or chest physiotherapy (unless comorbidity such as neuromuscular disease) ([NICE NG9 — §1.3.6–1.3.7, §1.4.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Checklist:** Bronchiolitis Don'ts
  - Do NOT give bronchodilators (salbutamol or ipratropium)
  - Do NOT give systemic or inhaled corticosteroids
  - Do NOT give routine antibiotics or montelukast
  - Do NOT perform routine chest X-ray or chest physiotherapy
- **Citations:** [NICE NG9 — §1.3.6–1.3.7, §1.4.1, §1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations), [WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/)

### Note
- NICE NG9 advises against nebulised 3% hypertonic saline, whereas WHO 2026 conditionally suggests a trial in hospitalised infants <2 years (watch for bronchospasm) ([NICE NG9 — §1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Asthma 2026 — Bronchiolitis Rec 3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK622563/)).
- Discharge only when clinically stable, feeding adequately, and maintaining SpO2 >90% (>92% if <6 wk/comorbidities) in air for 4 hours including sleep ([NICE NG9 — §1.5.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng9/chapter/recommendations)).

## Protocol: croup
- **Title:** Croup (acute laryngotracheobronchitis)
- **Summary:** Minimal-handling assessment, single-dose dexamethasone for all severities, nebulised adrenaline rescue, and airway red flags in croup.
- **Category:** Respiratory
- **Keywords:** `croup`, `stridor`, `barking cough`, `dexamethasone`, `nebulised adrenaline`, `epiglottitis`, `tracheitis`

### Step: Recognition, Severity & Minimal Handling
- **Points:**
  - Classic triad (6 mo–3 y): sudden seal-like barking cough, hoarse voice, and inspiratory stridor worse at night ([CPS Croup — Presentation](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
  - Grade severity: mild (no stridor at rest), moderate (stridor + retractions at rest), severe (marked retractions + agitation/lethargy) ([CPS Croup — Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
  - Keep child calm on caregiver's lap; avoid throat examination, tongue depressor, unnecessary IV lines, or mist tents ([CPS Croup — General Care](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
- **Citations:** [CPS Croup — Presentation, Table 2, General Care](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)

### Step: Single-Dose Corticosteroid (All Severities)
- **Timing:** Give promptly to all children with mild, moderate, or severe croup
- **Points:**
  - Give single-dose oral dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg (CPS/WHO) or 0.15 mg/kg (UK/IAP mild–moderate); onset 2–3 hours, lasts 24–48 h ([CPS Croup — Corticosteroids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/); [IAP STG 10 — Croup](https://www.scribd.com/document/554593914/Ch-010-Croup-in-Children)).
  - If persistent vomiting or severe distress prevents oral intake, give dexamethasone IM (same dose) ([CPS Croup — Corticosteroids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
- **Dose:** Dexamethasone — croup (all severities) | 0.15–0.6 mg/kg single dose PO/IM | max 16 mg | CPS/WHO: 0.6 mg/kg; UK/IAP: 0.15 mg/kg in mild–moderate croup
- **Citations:** [CPS Croup — Corticosteroids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/), [IAP STG 10 — Croup](https://www.scribd.com/document/554593914/Ch-010-Croup-in-Children)

### Step: Nebulised Adrenaline (Moderate to Severe Croup)
- **Timing:** Immediate for stridor at rest with retractions or severe distress
- **Points:**
  - Give nebulised 1:1000 L-adrenaline 0.5 mL/kg (standard 5 mL undiluted, max 5 mL) via facemask ([CPS Croup — Epinephrine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
  - Clinical effect starts in 10–30 minutes and wears off by 2 hours; always co-administer dexamethasone ([CPS Croup — Epinephrine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
  - Observe for at least 2–4 hours after nebulised adrenaline before discharge to detect rebound stridor ([CPS Croup — Epinephrine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
- **Dose:** Adrenaline (1:1000) — nebulised for moderate–severe croup | 0.5 mL/kg per dose nebulised | max 5 mL | Undiluted 1:1000 L-epinephrine; observe 2–4 h post-dose for rebound stridor
- **Citations:** [CPS Croup — Epinephrine](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)

### Step: Red Flags & Airway Escalation
- **Points:**
  - Refer urgently to PICU/anaesthesia/ENT if poor or unsustained response to adrenaline, exhaustion, quiet stridor, or cyanosis ([CPS Croup — Treatment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
  - Do not give routine antibiotics or beta-2 bronchodilators in typical viral croup (<1:1000 bacterial) ([CPS Croup — Other Therapies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).
- **Checklist:** Red Flags for Alternative Life-Threatening Diagnoses
  - High fever, drooling, dysphagia, toxic look, sitting forward (suspect epiglottitis or abscess)
  - High fever, toxic look, and poor response to nebulised adrenaline (suspect bacterial tracheitis)
  - Sudden choking episode, biphasic stridor, or unilateral reduced air entry (foreign body)
  - Age <6 months or recurrent/prolonged croup (evaluate for structural airway narrowing)
- **Citations:** [CPS Croup — Table 1, Treatment, Other Therapies](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)

### Note
- Dexamethasone 0.6 mg/kg is standard in CPS trials and preferred in severe croup; 0.15 mg/kg is widely used in UK/Indian mild–moderate protocols ([CPS Croup — Corticosteroids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/); [IAP STG 10 — Croup](https://www.scribd.com/document/554593914/Ch-010-Croup-in-Children)).
- Adding nebulised budesonide to oral dexamethasone provides no extra benefit; mist tents and humidified air are ineffective ([CPS Croup — General Care, Corticosteroids](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5804741/)).

## Protocol: dehydration-diarrhoea-child
- **Title:** Acute diarrhoea and dehydration in children
- **Summary:** WHO Plans A, B, and C rehydration, low-osmolarity ORS, zinc, and targeted antibiotics for non-SAM children.
- **Category:** Metabolic
- **Keywords:** `diarrhoea`, `dehydration`, `ORS`, `Plan A`, `Plan B`, `Plan C`, `zinc`, `dysentery`, `cholera`, `dast`

### Step: Assess Dehydration & Screen for SAM
- **Timing:** Immediate on arrival
- **Points:**
  - Always screen for Severe Acute Malnutrition (SAM) first — if present, DO NOT use Plan C (see SAM with complications) ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.1, Table 11](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - Some dehydration (Plan B): ≥2 of restless/irritable, sunken eyes, drinks eagerly, skin pinch returns slowly ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2, Table 12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - Severe dehydration (Plan C): ≥2 of lethargic/unconscious, sunken eyes, unable to drink, skin pinch returns very slowly (≥2 s) ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2, Table 12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §5.1–5.2, Tables 11–12](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)

### Step: Plan A (No Dehydration) & Plan B (Some Dehydration)
- **Points:**
  - Plan A: low-osmolarity ORS (245 mOsm/L) after each loose stool (<2 y: 50–100 mL; ≥2 y: 100–200 mL); continue breastfeeding/feeding ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/); [MoHFW STOP Diarrhoea 2025 — p. 63](https://dhs.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/document-4-100.pdf)).
  - Plan B: give low-osmolarity ORS 75 mL/kg over 4 hours under supervision; if vomiting, wait 10 min then resume slowly ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/); [ICMR Diarrhoea STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_diarrhea.pdf)).
  - Reassess at 4 hours and shift to Plan A, repeat Plan B, or escalate to Plan C ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
- **Dose:** Low-osmolarity ORS — Plan B (some dehydration) | 75 mL/kg PO over 4 hours | max — | Continue breastfeeding; stop ORS if puffy eyelids (no diuretics)
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.2–5.2.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/), [MoHFW STOP Diarrhoea 2025 — p. 63](https://dhs.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/document-4-100.pdf), [ICMR Diarrhoea STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_diarrhea.pdf)

### Step: Plan C — Severe Dehydration (Non-SAM Only)
- **Timing:** Start IV Ringer's lactate immediately
- **Points:**
  - Give 100 mL/kg Ringer's lactate (or 0.9% NaCl if unavailable); NEVER use plain 5% dextrose ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.1, Table 13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - Infants <12 months: 30 mL/kg IV over 1 hour, then 70 mL/kg IV over 5 hours (reassess at 6 h) ([WHO Pocket Book — Table 13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - Children ≥12 months: 30 mL/kg IV over 30 min, then 70 mL/kg IV over 2.5 hours (reassess at 3 h) ([WHO Pocket Book — Table 13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - Repeat 30 mL/kg once if radial pulse remains weak; add ORS ~5 mL/kg/h once able to drink ([WHO Pocket Book — Table 13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).
  - No IV access: NG ORS 20 mL/kg/h × 6 h (120 mL/kg); transfer urgently if unimproved at 3 h ([WHO Diarrhoea 2005 — §4.4.3, p. 15](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/df59ceab-2498-4a64-bd93-ad7566addb4e/content)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.1, Table 13](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/), [WHO Diarrhoea 2005 — §4.4.3, p. 15](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/df59ceab-2498-4a64-bd93-ad7566addb4e/content)

### Step: Zinc, Targeted Antibiotics & Don'ts
- **Points:**
  - Give oral zinc for 14 days: 10 mg/day (2–6 months) or 20 mg/day (≥6 months); not recommended <2 months ([MoHFW STOP Diarrhoea 2025 — p. 6](https://dhs.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/document-4-100.pdf); [ICMR Diarrhoea STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_diarrhea.pdf)).
  - Do NOT give routine antibiotics for watery diarrhoea; treat bloody dysentery with ciprofloxacin 15 mg/kg PO BD × 3 days ([WHO Pocket Book — §5.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/); [ICMR Diarrhoea STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_diarrhea.pdf)).
  - Suspected cholera (<12 y): single-dose doxycycline 2–4 mg/kg PO, or azithromycin 20 mg/kg PO (max 1 g) if resistant ([WHO/GTFCC Cholera 2025 — Table 1, p. 4](https://www.gtfcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gtfcc-technical-note-on-use-of-antibiotics-for-the-treatment-of-cholera.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Ciprofloxacin — bloody diarrhoea / dysentery | 15 mg/kg per dose PO every 12 h for 3 days | max 500 mg | Separate from zinc by 2–4 h; reassess at 48 h
- **Dose:** Azithromycin — suspected cholera (alternative to doxycycline) | 20 mg/kg single dose PO | max 1000 mg | Or single-dose doxycycline 2–4 mg/kg PO if susceptible
- **Checklist:** Diarrhoea Strict Don'ts
  - NEVER give antidiarrhoeals (loperamide, diphenoxylate, codeine, adsorbents)
  - NEVER use plain 5% dextrose or 0.18% saline for Plan C IV rehydration
  - Do NOT give routine antibiotics for acute watery diarrhoea
  - Do NOT give extra zinc to SAM children already on F-75/F-100/RUTF
- **Citations:** [MoHFW STOP Diarrhoea 2025 — p. 6](https://dhs.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/document-4-100.pdf), [ICMR Diarrhoea STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_diarrhea.pdf), [WHO Pocket Book — §5.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/), [WHO/GTFCC Cholera 2025 — Table 1, p. 4](https://www.gtfcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gtfcc-technical-note-on-use-of-antibiotics-for-the-treatment-of-cholera.pdf)

### Note
- Separate oral fluoroquinolones and tetracyclines from zinc by 2 hours before or 4–6 hours after zinc to prevent chelation ([WHO/GTFCC Cholera 2025 — p. 4](https://www.gtfcc.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/gtfcc-technical-note-on-use-of-antibiotics-for-the-treatment-of-cholera.pdf)).
- Discard reconstituted ORS after 24 hours; observe Plan C children for ≥6 hours after full rehydration before discharge ([MoHFW STOP Diarrhoea 2025 — p. 63](https://dhs.kerala.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/document-4-100.pdf); [WHO Pocket Book — §5.2.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/sites/books/NBK154434/)).

## Protocol: sam-with-complications
- **Title:** Severe acute malnutrition (SAM) with complications
- **Summary:** Inpatient stabilisation for complicated SAM: hypoglycaemia, hypothermia, cautious ReSoMal/shock fluids, antibiotics, and F-75 to F-100 feeding.
- **Category:** Metabolic
- **Keywords:** `SAM`, `malnutrition`, `NRC`, `F-75`, `F-100`, `ReSoMal`, `kwashiorkor`, `marasmus`, `MUAC`

### Step: Recognition & Inpatient Admission Criteria
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose SAM (6–59 mo): WHZ <−3 SD, MUAC <115 mm, or bilateral pitting oedema (+, ++, or +++) ([WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/); [ICMR SAM STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952337_paediatrics_severe_acute_malnutrition.pdf)).
  - Admit to NRC/hospital immediately for IMCI danger signs, acute medical illness, dehydration, severe oedema (+++), or failed appetite test ([WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B2a](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B1, B2a](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/), [ICMR SAM STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952337_paediatrics_severe_acute_malnutrition.pdf)

### Step: Step 1 & 2 — Hypoglycaemia & Hypothermia
- **Timing:** Treat immediately on admission (first 30 minutes)
- **Points:**
  - Hypoglycaemia (<3.0 mmol/L [<54 mg/dL] or assumed if untested): conscious → 50 mL 10% glucose PO/NG, then F-75 q2h ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - Unconscious/convulsing: 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV immediately, then 50 mL 10% glucose NG; recheck at 30 min ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - Hypothermia (<35.0 °C axillary / <35.5 °C rectal): active rewarming (KMC skin-to-skin, warm blanket, cap), feed immediately, treat presumed infection ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
- **Dose:** 10% Dextrose — unconscious/convulsing SAM hypoglycaemia | 5 mL/kg per dose IV bolus | max — | Follow with 50 mL 10% glucose NG; recheck glucose at 30 min
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.1–7.4.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)

### Step: Step 3 — Cautious Rehydration & Shock in SAM
- **Points:**
  - Never use standard Plan C IV fluids; rehydrate non-shock dehydration orally/NG with ReSoMal (or low-osmolarity ORS if ReSoMal unavailable) ([WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/); [WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - ReSoMal rate: 5 mL/kg every 30 min for 2 h, then 5–10 mL/kg/h for 4–10 h alternating with F-75 ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - Stop ReSoMal immediately if respiratory rate rises ≥5/min or pulse rises ≥25/min (overhydration); use full-strength ORS in cholera ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - True shock ONLY: 15 mL/kg over 1 h (½-Darrow/5%D or RL/5%D); if unimproved, 10 mL/kg blood over 3 h ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1 §1.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)).
- **Dose:** IV shock fluid (RL/5%D or ½-Darrow/5%D) — SAM true shock ONLY | 15 mL/kg IV over 1 hour | max — | Repeat once ONLY if improving; if no improvement, give 10 mL/kg blood over 3 h
- **Citations:** [WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/), [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1 §1.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/), [WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)

### Step: Antibiotics, Micronutrients & F-75 to F-100 Feeding
- **Points:**
  - Routine IV/IM ampicillin 50 mg/kg q6h × 2 d (then PO amoxicillin) + gentamicin 7.5 mg/kg OD × 7 d ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - Stabilisation feeding: F-75 at 130 mL/kg/day (100 mL/kg/day if severe oedema +++) every 2–3 hours (NG if intake ≤80%) ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
  - Switch to F-100/RUTF once appetite returns and oedema resolves; start iron 3 mg/kg/day after 2 days on F-100 ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.6–7.4.8](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).
- **Dose:** Ampicillin — complicated SAM infection | 50 mg/kg per dose IV/IM every 6 h | max — | Give for 2 days then switch to oral amoxicillin; pair with gentamicin 7.5 mg/kg OD
- **Checklist:** SAM Fatal Pitfalls to Avoid
  - NEVER give diuretics for nutritional oedema
  - NEVER give iron during the stabilisation phase (increases infection/mortality)
  - NEVER use standard Plan C rapid IV boluses or hot-water bottles for rewarming
  - NEVER send ReSoMal home (facility use under supervision only)
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.4–7.4.8](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/), [WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/)

### Note
- WHO 2023 updated Rec B7: ReSoMal remains preferred in facilities, but standard low-osmolarity ORS can be used if ReSoMal is unavailable ([WHO Wasting 2023 — Rec B7](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK601655/)).
- Do not give iron if the child is receiving RUTF, and give Vitamin A only on day 1 (repeat days 2, 14) if corneal signs or measles ([WHO Pocket Book — §7.4.6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154454/)).

## Protocol: anaphylaxis-dengue-malaria-child
- **Title:** Anaphylaxis, severe dengue, and severe malaria in children
- **Summary:** Child-specific emergency drug doses and fluid rules for paediatric anaphylaxis, severe dengue shock, and severe malaria.
- **Category:** Infection
- **Keywords:** `paediatric anaphylaxis`, `adrenaline`, `dengue shock`, `DSS`, `severe malaria`, `artesunate`, `rectal artesunate`

### Step: Paediatric Anaphylaxis
- **Timing:** IM adrenaline immediately; repeat every 5 minutes
- **Points:**
  - Give IM adrenaline (1:1000) 10 mcg/kg (0.01 mL/kg, max 0.5 mg) into anterolateral mid-thigh every 5 minutes (see Anaphylaxis) ([RCUK 2025 Drug Chart — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Paediatric%20emergency%20drug%202025.pdf); [IAP Anaphylaxis STG — Dosing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357601848_Anaphylaxis_Indian_Academy_of_Pediatrics_Standard_Treatment_Guidelines_2022)).
  - IM adrenaline (1:1000) bands: <6m 100–150 mcg; 6m–6y 150 mcg; 6–12y 300 mcg; >12y 500 mcg ([RCUK 2025 Drug Chart — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Paediatric%20emergency%20drug%202025.pdf)).
  - Lie child flat (never stand/sit up suddenly), give high-flow oxygen, and infuse 10 mL/kg crystalloid bolus (IAP: 20 mL/kg) ([RCUK Anaphylaxis 2021 — §2.3, p. 19](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05/Emergency%20Treatment%20of%20Anaphylaxis%20May%202021_0.pdf); [RCUK 2025 Drug Chart — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Paediatric%20emergency%20drug%202025.pdf)).
  - Refractory (>2 IM doses): peripheral IV adrenaline infusion (1 mg/100 mL NS at 0.5–1 mL/kg/h) in PICU ([RCUK Refractory 2021 — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2021-04/Refractory%20anaphylaxis%20algorithm%202021.pdf)).
  - Do NOT use antihistamines or routine corticosteroids for acute life-threatening anaphylaxis or biphasic prevention ([RCUK Anaphylaxis 2021 — p. 9](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05/Emergency%20Treatment%20of%20Anaphylaxis%20May%202021_0.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Adrenaline (1:1000) — paediatric anaphylaxis | 10 mcg/kg per dose IM every 5 min | max 0.5 mg | 0.01 mL/kg of 1:1000 into anterolateral mid-thigh
- **Citations:** [RCUK 2025 Drug Chart — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2025-10/Paediatric%20emergency%20drug%202025.pdf), [RCUK Anaphylaxis 2021 — p. 9, §2.3](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2021-05/Emergency%20Treatment%20of%20Anaphylaxis%20May%202021_0.pdf), [RCUK Refractory 2021 — p. 1](https://www.resus.org.uk/sites/default/files/2021-04/Refractory%20anaphylaxis%20algorithm%202021.pdf), [IAP Anaphylaxis STG — Dosing](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/357601848_Anaphylaxis_Indian_Academy_of_Pediatrics_Standard_Treatment_Guidelines_2022)

### Step: Paediatric Severe Dengue — Compensated Shock
- **Timing:** Immediate isotonic crystalloid; reassess Hct and vitals hourly
- **Points:**
  - Warning signs: vomiting, abdominal pain, fluid accumulation, mucosal bleed, lethargy, liver >2 cm, rising Hct (see Severe dengue) ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Ch. 4.1, p. 15](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf)).
  - Compensated shock (pulse pressure ≤20 mmHg, CRT >2 s): give RL or 0.9% NaCl 10–20 mL/kg over 1 hour ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Ch. 7.3.2, p. 39](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf); [WHO ETAT 2016 — p. 33](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download)).
  - If improving, step down fluids: 7–10 → 5–7 → 3–5 → 2–3 mL/kg/h; stop IV fluids at 24–48 h ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Fig. 12, p. 40](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf)).
  - Use ideal body weight in overweight children; target urine output 0.5 mL/kg/h ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — pp. 35–36](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Ch. 4.1, Ch. 7.3.2, pp. 15, 35–40](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf), [WHO ETAT 2016 — p. 33](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download)

### Step: Paediatric Severe Dengue — Hypotensive Shock & Don'ts
- **Timing:** Rapid 20 mL/kg bolus over 15 minutes
- **Points:**
  - Hypotensive shock: 20 mL/kg crystalloid (or colloid) over 15 min, then step down 10 → 5–7 → 3–5 → 2–3 mL/kg/h ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Ch. 7.3.2, pp. 40–41](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf); [ICMR Dengue STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_dengue_fever.pdf)).
  - Unstable + rising Hct: 10–20 mL/kg colloid; falling Hct: 5 mL/kg PRBCs (or 10 mL/kg whole blood) ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — pp. 40–42](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf)).
  - Use paracetamol 10–15 mg/kg/dose for fever; avoid NSAIDs, aspirin, IM injections, corticosteroids, and routine prophylactic platelets ([NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — pp. 25, 28](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf); [WHO Arboviral 2025 — Ch. 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/who381804/ch1/)).
- **Checklist:** Severe Dengue Strict Don'ts
  - NEVER give aspirin, ibuprofen, or other NSAIDs
  - NEVER give IM injections or systemic corticosteroids
  - Do NOT give routine prophylactic platelet transfusions without active bleeding
  - Do NOT continue IV fluids past 48 h once plasma leakage resolves (pulmonary oedema risk)
- **Citations:** [NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — Ch. 5.4, Ch. 5.7, Ch. 7.3.2](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf), [ICMR Dengue STW — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952338_paediatrics_dengue_fever.pdf), [WHO Arboviral 2025 — Ch. 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/who381804/ch1/)

### Step: Paediatric Severe Malaria
- **Timing:** Start IV/IM artesunate immediately upon diagnosis
- **Points:**
  - IV/IM artesunate: 3.0 mg/kg (<20 kg) or 2.4 mg/kg (≥20 kg) at 0, 12, 24 h then OD → 3-day ACT (see Severe malaria) ([MMV/WHO Malaria 2025 — p. 3](https://www.severemalaria.org/sites/default/files/content/document/Review%20of%20treatment%20guidelines%20for%20severe%20malaria_2025_MMV.pdf); [NCVBDC Malaria — p. 10](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National-Drug-Policy-2013.pdf)).
  - At PHCs without IV/IM artesunate, give children <6 years single-dose rectal artesunate 10 mg/kg and refer immediately ([MMV/WHO Malaria 2025 — p. 3](https://www.severemalaria.org/sites/default/files/content/document/Review%20of%20treatment%20guidelines%20for%20severe%20malaria_2025_MMV.pdf)).
  - No artesunate: IM artemether (3.2 then 1.6 mg/kg OD) or IV quinine (20 mg/kg/4h then 10 mg/kg q8h); never arteether ([MMV/WHO Malaria 2025 — p. 3](https://www.severemalaria.org/sites/default/files/content/document/Review%20of%20treatment%20guidelines%20for%20severe%20malaria_2025_MMV.pdf); [NCVBDC Malaria — pp. 10–11](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National-Drug-Policy-2013.pdf)).
  - Avoid rapid IV fluid boluses without true shock (FEAST); treat hypoglycaemia (<45 mg/dL) with 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV ([WHO ETAT 2016 — Rec 2.1–2.2](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download); [WHO Pocket Book — §6.2.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/)).
  - Transfuse 10 mL/kg PRBCs (20 mL/kg whole blood) over 3–4 h if Hb ≤4 (or 4–5 + distress); no prophylactic phenobarbitone ([WHO Pocket Book — §6.2.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/)).
- **Dose:** Artesunate — severe malaria (children <20 kg) | 3 mg/kg per dose IV/IM at 0, 12, 24 h then daily | max — | Use 2.4 mg/kg for ≥20 kg; minimum 3 doses (24 h), then full 3-day oral ACT
- **Dose:** Rectal artesunate — PHC pre-referral severe malaria (<6 years ONLY) | 10 mg/kg single dose PR | max — | Refer immediately; do NOT use in children ≥6 years or adults
- **Citations:** [MMV/WHO Malaria 2025 — p. 3](https://www.severemalaria.org/sites/default/files/content/document/Review%20of%20treatment%20guidelines%20for%20severe%20malaria_2025_MMV.pdf), [NCVBDC Malaria — pp. 10–11](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National-Drug-Policy-2013.pdf), [WHO ETAT 2016 — Rec 2.1–2.5](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download), [WHO Pocket Book — §6.2.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/)

### Note
- WHO 2025 arboviral guidelines favour crystalloids over colloids and recommend against prophylactic platelets (<50,000/µL without bleeding); NCVBDC 2023 still permits colloids in refractory shock ([WHO Arboviral 2025 — Ch. 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/n/who381804/ch1/); [NCVBDC Dengue 2023 — p. 40](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/National%20Guidelines%20for%20Clinical%20Management%20of%20Dengue%20Fever%202023.pdf)).
- In severe malaria with shock AND severe anaemia (Hb <5 g/dL), transfuse blood urgently with maintenance fluids only — no pre-transfusion crystalloid bolus ([WHO ETAT 2016 — Rec 2.5, p. 40](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download)).
