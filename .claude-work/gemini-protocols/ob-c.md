# Batch OB-C

## Protocol: maternal-sepsis
- **Title:** Maternal sepsis
- **Summary:** Early recognition, 1-hour IV antibiotics, cautious fluids, source control, and fetal management in obstetric sepsis.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `maternal sepsis`, `puerperal sepsis`, `chorioamnionitis`, `endometritis`, `MEOWS`, `septic shock`, `GAS`, `necrotising fasciitis`

### Step: Recognition & Obstetric Red Flags
- **Timing:** Immediate assessment on suspicion
- **Points:**
  - Maternal sepsis is life-threatening organ dysfunction from infection during pregnancy, childbirth, post-abortion, or up to 42 days postpartum ([WHO Maternal Sepsis — Definition](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/0a290090-d59f-4937-ae06-f678ee638308/download)).
  - Suspect sepsis with unexplained organ dysfunction and possible infection regardless of fever; do not use NEWS2 in pregnancy ([SMFM #67 — Rec 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [RCOG GTG 64 — §5.1, §5.3](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - Use an obstetric early-warning chart (MEOWS); trigger immediate sepsis bundle for any red flag or lactate ≥2–4 mmol/L ([RCOG GTG 64 — Table 5–6, §5.4](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [MEOWS — Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5910060/)).
  - Obtain two blood culture sets and serum lactate urgently, without delaying IV antibiotics ([RCOG GTG 64 — §5.4, p. e67](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [ICMR AMR — Ch. 3, §3.4](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Maternal Sepsis Red Flags (MEOWS / Obstetric)
  - Altered mental state (GCS <15 or not alert on AVPU)
  - Respiratory rate ≥25/min (MEOWS red: >30 or <10/min) or SpO2 <94% on room air
  - Heart rate >130 bpm (MEOWS red: >120 or <40 bpm) or SBP <90 mmHg
  - Urine output <0.5 mL/kg/h or none for >12 hours, or lactate ≥4 mmol/L
  - Severe pain out of proportion, sunburn rash, or watery discharge (suspect GAS / necrotising fasciitis)
- **Citations:** [WHO Maternal Sepsis — Definition](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/0a290090-d59f-4937-ae06-f678ee638308/download), [SMFM #67 — Rec 1](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/), [RCOG GTG 64 — §5.1–5.4, Table 5–6](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf), [MEOWS — Table 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5910060/), [ICMR AMR — Ch. 3, §3.4](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)

### Step: Empirical IV Antibiotics
- **Timing:** Within 1 hour of recognition of sepsis or septic shock
- **Points:**
  - Give broad-spectrum IV antibiotics within 1 hour of recognising high-risk sepsis or septic shock ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, p. e71](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [SMFM #67 — Rec 8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/)).
  - Puerperal sepsis, septic abortion, or chorioamnionitis: piperacillin–tazobactam 4.5 g IV q6–8h (or clindamycin 600–900 mg q8h + gentamicin) ([ICMR AMR 2022 — Ch. 12 Table 1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/chapter12-table1.html); [ICMR AMR — Tables 3.1–3.2](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)).
  - In septic shock, use meropenem 1 g IV q8h (avoid pip–tazo until ESBL excluded) ± vancomycin 15 mg/kg q8–12h ([ICMR AMR — Ch. 3, Tables 3.1–3.2](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)).
  - Add clindamycin 600–900 mg IV q8h if Group A Streptococcus, toxic shock, or necrotising fasciitis is suspected ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, Table 9](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [ICMR AMR — Table 6.5](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)).
  - District alternative for chorioamnionitis/endometritis: ampicillin 2 g IV q6h + gentamicin 5 mg/kg IV q24h (+ metronidazole 500 mg q8h) ([WHO MCPC Brief — Table 5](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1)).
- **Dose:** Gentamicin — chorioamnionitis or endometritis (with beta-lactam or clindamycin) | 5 mg/kg per dose IV every 24 h | max — | Single daily IV dose; adjust in renal impairment
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, Table 9](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf), [SMFM #67 — Rec 8](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/), [ICMR AMR 2022 — Ch. 12 Table 1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/chapter12-table1.html), [ICMR AMR — Ch. 3, Tables 3.1–3.2, 6.5](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf), [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 5](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1)

### Step: Fluids, Vasopressors & ICU Support
- **Timing:** Initial crystalloid within first 3 hours; ICU if >2 L
- **Points:**
  - Use balanced crystalloids (Ringer's lactate) first-line; give 500 mL boluses (1–2 L total over 3 h) cautiously ([SMFM #67 — Rec 10–11](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, pp. e71–e72](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - Avoid hydroxyethyl starches and gelatins; involve ICU early if hypotension persists after 2 L crystalloid ([SMFM #67 — Rec 12](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, p. e72](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - Start noradrenaline (norepinephrine) first-line to maintain MAP ≥65 mmHg (may start peripherally while securing central access) ([SMFM #67 — Rec 14](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [SSC 2021 — Vasopressors](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-guidelines-2021)).
  - Refractory shock: IV hydrocortisone 50 mg q6h; target glucose 144–180 mg/dL; start LMWH (see Sepsis and septic shock) ([SMFM #67 — Rec 15–17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/)).
- **Citations:** [SMFM #67 — Rec 10–17](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/), [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, pp. e71–e72](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf), [SSC 2021 — Vasopressors](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-guidelines-2021)

### Step: Source Control
- **Timing:** As soon as medically and logistically practical
- **Points:**
  - Start IV antibiotics and perform prompt uterine evacuation for septic abortion or infected retained products (see Miscarriage and septic abortion) ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, p. e72](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [SMFM #67 — Rec 18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/)).
  - Deliver promptly regardless of gestational age if chorioamnionitis or intrauterine infection is the source ([SMFM #67 — Rec 18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/)).
  - Do not defer pelvic ultrasound, CT, or MRI solely for pregnancy; drain pelvic abscesses and remove infected catheters ([RCOG GTG 64 — §5.4, §6.1](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [SSC 2021 — Source Control](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-guidelines-2021)).
  - Perform urgent surgical debridement with general/plastic surgery for suspected necrotising fasciitis; isolate if GAS ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1.2, pp. e72–e73](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 64 — §5.4, §6.1, §6.1.2](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf), [SMFM #67 — Rec 9, 18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/), [SSC 2021 — Source Control](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-guidelines-2021)

### Step: Fetal Monitoring & Delivery Decisions
- **Points:**
  - Resuscitate the mother first — stabilising maternal oxygenation and perfusion is the best fetal resuscitation ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.2, p. e75](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - Start continuous CTG once viable; abnormal CTG may be an early sign of maternal deterioration ([RCOG GTG 64 — §6.2, pp. e74–e75](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - Sepsis alone is not an immediate indication for delivery unless an intrauterine source is suspected ([SMFM #67 — Rec 18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.2, p. e75](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
  - If deteriorating on first-line antibiotics, consider IV aciclovir 10 mg/kg q8h for disseminated HSV ([RCOG GTG 64 Corrigendum — §6.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418966/)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.2, pp. e74–e75](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf), [SMFM #67 — Rec 18](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/), [RCOG GTG 64 Corrigendum — §6.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13418966/)

### Note
- Fluid bolus differs: SMFM advises 1–2 L balanced crystalloid and RCOG advises 500 mL boluses (ICU if >2 L) due to pulmonary oedema risk, versus SSC's 30 mL/kg ([SMFM #67 — Rec 10](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37236495/); [RCOG GTG 64 — §6.1, p. e72](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf); [SSC 2021 — Resuscitation](https://www.sccm.org/clinical-resources/guidelines/guidelines/surviving-sepsis-guidelines-2021)).
- Avoid double anaerobic cover (metronidazole is redundant with pip–tazo or clindamycin); do not use clindamycin alone for GBS ([RCOG GTG 64 — Table 9, p. e73](https://livrepository.liverpool.ac.uk/3200578/1/W4405531035.pdf)).
- In Indian tertiary settings with high ESBL rates, ICMR advises carbapenems over piperacillin–tazobactam in septic shock ([ICMR AMR — Ch. 3, p. 30](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)).

## Protocol: vte-in-pregnancy
- **Title:** VTE in pregnancy
- **Summary:** Diagnostic imaging sequence, weight-based LMWH dosing, massive PE rescue, and neuraxial timing for pregnancy-associated DVT and PE.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `VTE`, `DVT`, `PE`, `pulmonary embolism`, `LMWH`, `enoxaparin`, `CTPA`, `YEARS`, `neuraxial`, `thrombolysis`

### Step: Diagnostic Imaging Sequence
- **Points:**
  - Suspected DVT: perform compression duplex ultrasound (CUS); if negative with high suspicion, stop LMWH and repeat days 3 and 7 ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4.1, p. 7](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
  - Suspected PE: perform 12-lead ECG and chest X-ray first; do bilateral leg CUS if DVT symptoms exist ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4.2, pp. 7–8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC PE 2019 — §9.5](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/54/3/1901647)).
  - If leg CUS confirms DVT in suspected PE, treat without chest imaging; otherwise proceed to V/Q or CTPA ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4.2, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
  - Use CTPA if CXR is abnormal or V/Q is unavailable (usual first-line in India); V/Q if CXR normal ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4.2, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [IJMR VTE — Diagnosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466491/)).
  - RCOG: do not use D-dimer; ESC: pregnancy-adapted YEARS + D-dimer (<1000 if 0 items, <500 if ≥1) ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4, p. 3](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — §11.4.1.2](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 37b — §4, §4.1–4.2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf), [ESC PE 2019 — §9.5](https://publications.ersnet.org/content/erj/54/3/1901647), [ESC CVD 2025 — §11.4.1.2](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf), [IJMR VTE — Diagnosis](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466491/)

### Step: Empirical & Therapeutic LMWH
- **Timing:** Start immediately on clinical suspicion while awaiting imaging
- **Points:**
  - Start therapeutic SC LMWH immediately on clinical suspicion unless strongly contraindicated (active bleeding or platelets <75 × 10⁹/L) ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.1, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ASH VTE — Rec 1–2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/)).
  - Dose by early-pregnancy booking weight: enoxaparin 1 mg/kg SC q12h (or 1.5 mg/kg OD) or RCOG weight bands ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.2, Table 1a](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [IJMR VTE — Table II](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466491/)).
  - Enoxaparin BD/OD: <50kg 40/60mg; 50–69kg 60/90mg; 70–89kg 80/120mg; 90–109kg 100/150mg; 110–125kg 120/180mg ([RCOG GTG 37b — Table 1a, p. 13](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
  - Avoid warfarin antenatally and avoid DOACs in pregnancy and breastfeeding; treat throughout pregnancy plus ≥6 weeks postpartum (min 3 months) ([RCOG GTG 37b — §8.1–8.3, §10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — Rec Table 4](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Enoxaparin — therapeutic VTE treatment (booking weight) | 1 mg/kg per dose SC every 12 h | max — | Use early-pregnancy booking weight; reduce or switch to UFH if CrCl <30 mL/min
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 37b — §6.1–6.2, §8.1–8.3, §10, Table 1a](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf), [ASH VTE — Rec 1–2, 13](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/), [ESC CVD 2025 — Rec Table 4](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf), [IJMR VTE — Table II](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10466491/)

### Step: Prophylactic LMWH by Weight Band
- **Points:**
  - RCOG 2026 prophylaxis (enoxaparin): <100kg 40mg OD; 100–129kg 60mg OD; 130–169kg 80mg OD; ≥170kg 0.6mg/kg/day ([RCOG 37a 2026 — Table 1, p. 1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/bzkdicre/position-statement-gtg37a-final-version.pdf)).
  - Prior RCOG 37a: <50kg 20mg OD; 50–90kg 40mg OD; 91–130kg 60mg OD; 131–170kg 80mg OD ([RCOG GTG 37a — Table 3, p. 24](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/m4mbpjwi/gtg-no37a-2015_amended-2023.pdf)).
  - Routine peak anti-Xa monitoring is not needed except extremes of weight (<50 or ≥90 kg), renal impairment, or recurrent VTE ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.3, p. 14](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ASH VTE — Rec 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG 37a 2026 — Table 1, p. 1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/bzkdicre/position-statement-gtg37a-final-version.pdf), [RCOG GTG 37a — Table 3, p. 24](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/m4mbpjwi/gtg-no37a-2015_amended-2023.pdf), [RCOG GTG 37b — §6.3, p. 14](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf), [ASH VTE — Rec 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/)

### Step: Massive / High-Risk PE
- **Timing:** Bedside echo or CTPA within 1 hour
- **Points:**
  - Call urgent MDT (obstetrician, physician, intensivist, radiologist, anaesthetist) and perform portable echo or CTPA within 1 hour ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.4, pp. 14–15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
  - Give IV UFH: 80 units/kg bolus (omit bolus if thrombolysed), then 18 units/kg/h infusion targeting APTT ratio 1.5–2.5 ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.4, Table 2, p. 15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
  - Systemic thrombolysis (e.g., alteplase) is NOT contraindicated in life-threatening haemodynamic instability despite bleeding risk ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.4, pp. 14–16](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ASH VTE — Rec 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/)).
  - Arrest at >20 weeks: CPR with left uterine displacement and perimortem CS by 5 min (see Maternal collapse) ([RCOG GTG 37b — §6.4, p. 15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 37b — §6.4, Table 2, pp. 14–16](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf), [ASH VTE — Rec 8](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6258928/)

### Step: Peripartum & Neuraxial Anaesthesia Timing
- **Points:**
  - Stop LMWH immediately at labour onset or vaginal bleeding, or 24 hours before planned induction/CS for therapeutic doses ([RCOG GTG 37b — §9.1, p. 19](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — §4.5.6.1](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
  - Neuraxial block intervals: wait ≥12 hours after prophylactic LMWH, ≥24 hours after therapeutic LMWH, or 4–6 hours after IV UFH ([RCOG GTG 37b — §9.1, p. 19](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — Rec Table 3](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
  - Wait ≥4 hours after spinal block or epidural catheter removal before giving the next LMWH dose (≥24 h if traumatic) ([RCOG GTG 37b — §9.1, pp. 19–20](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — Fig. 5, p. 27](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
  - Urgent delivery <12 h after therapeutic enoxaparin: protamine 1 mg/1 mg (<8 h) or 0.5 mg/1 mg (>8 h) ([ESC CVD 2025 — §4.5.6.2.2, p. 26](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Peripartum Anticoagulation Safety
  - Stop LMWH immediately if contractions or vaginal bleeding start
  - Confirm ≥12 h (prophylactic) or ≥24 h (therapeutic) since last LMWH before spinal/epidural
  - Confirm normal aPTT 4–6 h after stopping IV UFH before neuraxial block
  - Do NOT remove epidural catheter within 12 h of prophylactic or 24 h of therapeutic LMWH
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 37b — §9.1, pp. 19–20](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf), [ESC CVD 2025 — §4.5.6, Rec Table 3, Fig. 5](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)

### Note
- RCOG GTG 37b prohibits D-dimer in pregnancy, whereas ESC 2019/2025 and IJMR allow pregnancy-adapted YEARS plus D-dimer to avoid chest radiation ([RCOG GTG 37b — §4, p. 3](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/wj2lpco5/gtg-37b-1.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — §11.4.1.2](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).
- RCOG's September 2026 position statement removed the `<50 kg: 20 mg` enoxaparin prophylactic reduction (now `<100 kg: 40 mg OD`); ESC 2025 also favours fixed low-dose prophylaxis ([RCOG 37a 2026 — Table 1, p. 1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/bzkdicre/position-statement-gtg37a-final-version.pdf); [ESC CVD 2025 — Rec Table 11](https://www.unipi.it/wp-content/uploads/ESC-guidelines-Cardiovascular-disease-pregnancy-2025.pdf)).

## Protocol: dka-in-pregnancy
- **Title:** DKA in pregnancy
- **Summary:** Recognition of euglycaemic DKA, joint obstetric–medical resuscitation, weight-based insulin, potassium, and fetal monitoring in pregnancy.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `DKA`, `diabetic ketoacidosis`, `euglycaemic DKA`, `ketones`, `FRIII`, `insulin`, `pregnancy diabetes`, `GDM`

### Step: Diagnosis & Euglycaemic DKA Recognition
- **Timing:** Immediate VBG and capillary ketones on suspicion
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose DKA with ketones ≥3.0 mmol/L (or urine ≥2+), venous pH <7.30 and/or bicarbonate <18 mmol/L (<15 in JBDS) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 12 — §1.5.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
  - Pregnancy triggers DKA rapidly at lower or normal glucose (<200 mg/dL [11.1 mmol/L]); diagnose euglycaemic DKA regardless of glucose ([ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 2, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [ADA SOC 2026 — §15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690181/)).
  - Test capillary ketones urgently in any unwell pregnant woman with diabetes, hyperemesis, infection, or after antenatal corticosteroids ([NICE NG3 — Rec 1.3.23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3/chapter/recommendations); [ACOG PB 201 — p. e232](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/pregestational-diabetes-mell-number-201.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Pregnancy-Specific Precipitants & Don'ts
  - Infection, hyperemesis, missed insulin, insulin pump failure, or antenatal corticosteroids
  - NEVER use betamimetic tocolytics in women with diabetes (precipitates DKA)
  - Do NOT exclude DKA because blood glucose is normal or <200 mg/dL
- **Citations:** [ADA Consensus 2024 — §3, §6, Table 2, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/), [JBDS 12 — §1.5.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf), [ADA SOC 2026 — §15](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12690181/), [NICE NG3 — Rec 1.3.23, 1.3.43](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3/chapter/recommendations), [ACOG PB 201 — p. e232](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/pregestational-diabetes-mell-number-201.pdf)

### Step: Joint Care & Fetal Monitoring
- **Timing:** Immediate Level 2 / HDU admission
- **Points:**
  - Admit immediately for Level 2 critical care / HDU with joint senior obstetric and medical/diabetes teams ([NICE NG3 — Rec 1.3.24](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3/chapter/recommendations); [ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - Stabilise the mother FIRST; fetal heart rate abnormalities usually resolve once maternal acidosis and dehydration are corrected ([ACOG PB 201 — p. e232](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/pregestational-diabetes-mell-number-201.pdf); [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.3, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
  - Maintain continuous CTG once viable; avoid premature emergency caesarean section before maternal metabolic stabilisation ([ACOG PB 201 — p. e232](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/pregestational-diabetes-mell-number-201.pdf); [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.3, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG3 — Rec 1.3.24](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng3/chapter/recommendations), [ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/), [ACOG PB 201 — p. e232](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/pregestational-diabetes-mell-number-201.pdf), [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.3, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)

### Step: Pregnancy-Specific Fluid Resuscitation
- **Timing:** Start 1st litre over 1 hour immediately
- **Points:**
  - Infuse 0.9% NaCl or balanced crystalloid 1 L over 1 hour (500–1000 mL/h), then titrate cautiously for pulmonary oedema risk ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.2, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
  - Ongoing fluids: 1 L over 2 h × 2, then 1 L over 4 h; monitor lung bases ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.2, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf); [ICMR STW DKA — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1726567244_diabetic_ketoacidosis.pdf)).
  - When glucose <14 mmol/L (250 mg/dL), add 10% dextrose (50–125 mL/h) or 5% dextrose + saline alongside insulin ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.2, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf); [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - In euglycaemic DKA (glucose <14 mmol/L at presentation), start 5–10% dextrose immediately alongside saline so insulin clears ketones ([ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4, Table 4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/), [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.2, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf), [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf), [ICMR STW DKA — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1726567244_diabetic_ketoacidosis.pdf)

### Step: Fixed-Rate IV Insulin Infusion (FRIII)
- **Points:**
  - Start fixed-rate IV regular insulin at 0.1 units/kg/h using actual pregnant body weight (no routine bolus; max 14 units/h) ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf); [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - Continue usual long-acting basal SC insulin; disconnect insulin pumps (CSII) and stop prandial insulin ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf); [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - Target ketones falling ≥0.5 mmol/L/h, bicarbonate rising ≥3.0 mmol/L/h, glucose falling ~3.0 mmol/L/h; increase FRIII by 1 unit/h if unmet ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
  - Reduce FRIII to 0.05 units/kg/h once glucose falls <14 mmol/L (250 mg/dL) alongside dextrose to prevent hypoglycaemia ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Regular human insulin (FRIII) — DKA in pregnancy | 0.1 units/kg per hour IV infusion | max 14 units | Use actual pregnant weight; halve to 0.05 units/kg/h when glucose <14 mmol/L (250 mg/dL)
- **Citations:** [JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf), [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/), [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)

### Step: Potassium, Bicarbonate & Resolution
- **Points:**
  - Check K+ before insulin: if <3.5 mmol/L, hold insulin and replace K+ first (10 mmol/h until >3.5 mmol/L) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - If K+ 3.5–5.5 mmol/L (3.5–5.0 ADA) and passing urine, add 20–40 mmol KCl/L fluid; omit K+ if >5.5 mmol/L ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — Table 5, p. 12](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
  - Avoid routine sodium bicarbonate (consider only if pH <7.0: 100 mmol in 400 mL sterile water over 2 h) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/)).
  - Resolved: blood ketones <0.6 mmol/L and venous pH >7.3 (not urine ketones); overlap SC insulin 1–2 h (see DKA) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — §7.2, §7.4](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
  - Give prophylactic LMWH throughout hospital admission unless contraindicated (see VTE in pregnancy) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — §5.2, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [ADA Consensus 2024 — §4, Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/), [JBDS 02 — Table 5, §5.2, §7.2, §7.4](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)

### Note
- Acidosis bicarbonate cutoff differs: ADA 2024 raised it to <18 mmol/L, while JBDS uses <15 mmol/L (pregnancy baseline is lower) ([ADA Consensus 2024 — Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 12 — §1.5.1, p. 28](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf)).
- Dextrose infusion rate when glucose <14 mmol/L differs: JBDS 12 (pregnancy) uses 10% dextrose at 50 mL/h to limit fluid overload versus 125 mL/h in general adults ([JBDS 12 — §1.5.2.2, p. 29](https://diabetestimes.co.uk/wp-content/uploads/2022/01/JBDS-12-Managing-diabetes-and-hyperglycaemia-during-labour-and-birth-27.1.22.pdf); [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
- Where blood ketone meters are unavailable, use urine ketones ≥2+ for diagnosis and venous pH/bicarbonate rise for monitoring, never urine ketones for resolution ([ADA Consensus 2024 — §3–4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11272983/); [JBDS 02 — §5.3, p. 13](https://profketandhatariya.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/05/Diabetic-Medicine-2022.pdf)).
