# Batch OB-B

## Protocol: cord-prolapse
- **Title:** Umbilical cord prolapse
- **Summary:** Emergency decompression, positioning, and rapid delivery for overt or occult umbilical cord prolapse after membrane rupture.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `cord prolapse`, `UCP`, `occult cord`, `knee-chest`, `Sims`, `bladder filling`, `Category 1 CS`, `terbutaline`

### Step: Recognition & Immediate Call for Help
- **Timing:** Immediate on SROM/ARM or sudden FHR drop
- **Points:**
  - Suspect cord prolapse with sudden FHR bradycardia or variable decelerations after SROM or ARM ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.4, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Perform immediate speculum or digital vaginal examination to confirm prolapsed cord and check cord pulsation ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.4, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [WHO MCPC — Cord Prolapse](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf)).
  - Summon senior obstetrician, anaesthetist, theatre team, and neonatal resuscitation team immediately ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Avoid amniotomy if the presenting part is high or mobile, or if cord is felt below it ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.3, p. 7](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 50 — §4.3–4.5, pp. 7–8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf), [WHO MCPC — Cord Prolapse](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf)

### Step: Relieve Cord Compression
- **Timing:** Immediate — do not delay transfer to theatre
- **Points:**
  - Insert two gloved fingers vaginally and push the presenting part upwards off the cord continuously ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §3.2, p. 10](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)).
  - Place woman in knee–chest position or left lateral with head-down tilt and pillow under left hip ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - If delivery is delayed, instil 500–750 mL saline into bladder via Foley catheter and clamp it ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Empty the filled bladder immediately before attempting caesarean section or vaginal delivery ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Give terbutaline 0.25 mg SC if FHR abnormalities persist despite elevation while preparing for theatre ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf), [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §3.2, p. 10](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)

### Step: Cord Handling Rules
- **Points:**
  - Minimise handling of exposed cord loops to prevent arterial vasospasm and fetal acidosis ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Do not attempt manual replacement of the prolapsed cord above the presenting part ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Cord Handling Don'ts
  - Do NOT push the cord back into the uterus
  - Do NOT handle or repeatedly palpate exposed cord loops
  - Do NOT displace the presenting part excessively (can worsen prolapse)
  - Do NOT delay theatre transfer to perform bladder filling if manual elevation is underway
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)

### Step: Delivery Route, Timing & Neonatal Care
- **Timing:** Category 1 CS target ≤30 min
- **Points:**
  - Perform Category 1 caesarean section (target ≤30 min) for suspicious or pathological FHR; verbal consent suffices ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.6, pp. 9–10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [FOGSI GCPR 2024 — p. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Binder_Birth-after-Cesarean-Section.pdf)).
  - Consider Category 2 caesarean section with continuous CTG if FHR is normal; upgrade immediately if abnormal ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.6, p. 10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Attempt operative vaginal birth only at full dilatation when rapid, safe birth without cord impingement is expected ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.6, pp. 10–11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Ensure a skilled newborn resuscitator attends; take paired umbilical arterial and venous cord blood gases ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.6, p. 10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.11.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 50 — §4.6, pp. 9–11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf), [FOGSI GCPR 2024 — pp. 8–9](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/08/Binder_Birth-after-Cesarean-Section.pdf), [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.11.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)

### Step: PHC & Ambulance Transfer
- **Timing:** Category 1 emergency transfer
- **Points:**
  - Advise knee–chest face-down position while awaiting the ambulance at a PHC or home ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.7, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Switch to exaggerated Sims (left lateral with pillow under hip) during ambulance transit; knee–chest is unsafe moving ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.7, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
  - Maintain decompression via manual elevation or clamped 500–750 mL bladder filling and pre-alert receiving CEmOC teams ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.7, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §3.2, p. 10](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Pre-Transfer Actions
  - Confirm spontaneous vaginal birth is not imminent before transferring
  - Never transport in knee–chest position inside a moving ambulance
  - Alert receiving obstetric, anaesthetic, and neonatal teams before departure
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 50 — §4.7, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf), [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §3.2, p. 10](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)

### Note
- Wrapping exposed cord in warm saline swabs is sometimes practised, but has unproven benefit and must not cause excess handling ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.5, p. 9; §4.7, p. 12](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf)).
- Consider delayed cord clamping only if the baby is uncompromised; immediate resuscitation always takes priority over delayed clamping ([RCOG GTG 50 — §4.9, p. 12](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/3wykswng/gtg-50-umbilicalcordprolapse-2014.pdf); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.14](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).

## Protocol: shoulder-dystocia
- **Title:** Shoulder dystocia
- **Summary:** Stepwise manoeuvres, strict safety limits, and post-delivery care when fetal shoulders impact after head delivery.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `shoulder dystocia`, `turtle sign`, `McRoberts`, `suprapubic pressure`, `Rubin`, `Woods screw`, `posterior arm`, `Gaskin`, `BPI`

### Step: Recognition & Team Activation
- **Timing:** Immediate — note exact time of head delivery
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose when routine axial traction fails to deliver shoulders (head-to-body interval >60 s, turtle sign, no restitution) ([RCOG GTG 42 — §2, §6.2](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
  - Declare "shoulder dystocia" aloud and call senior obstetrician, anaesthetist, neonatal team, and extra nursing staff immediately ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e127](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
  - Stop oxytocin infusion, discourage maternal pushing, and exit birthing pool immediately if in water ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1, §6.4](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 42 — §2, §6.2, §6.3.1, §6.4](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42), [ACOG PB 178 — p. e127](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)

### Step: First-Line Manoeuvres
- **Timing:** Move immediately to next manoeuvre if unsuccessful
- **Points:**
  - Perform McRoberts' manoeuvre: lay flat, remove pillows, and hyperflex abducted thighs onto maternal abdomen ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e127](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
  - Apply downward, lateral suprapubic pressure from the fetal back side to adduct and rotate the anterior shoulder ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e127](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
  - Apply routine axial traction only; do not hold a failed manoeuvre for 30 seconds before escalating ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.2, §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
  - Perform episiotomy only if needed to gain vaginal access for internal manoeuvres — it does not relieve bony impaction ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 42 — §6.2, §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42), [ACOG PB 178 — pp. e127–e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)

### Step: Second-Line Manoeuvres
- **Points:**
  - Move buttocks to bed edge and insert the whole hand posteriorly into the sacral hollow ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
  - Perform internal rotation (Rubin II, Woods screw, or reverse Woods) to rotate shoulders into the oblique diameter ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
  - Deliver the posterior arm by grasping the fetal wrist and withdrawing it gently in a straight line ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — pp. e127–e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
  - Consider all-fours (Gaskin) position for mobile women without epidural analgesia, particularly when staff are limited ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42), [ACOG PB 178 — pp. e127–e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)

### Step: Third-Line & Rescue Manoeuvres
- **Points:**
  - Consider digital axillary traction of the posterior arm when standard manoeuvres fail; avoid axillary slings ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.3](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
  - Last-resort options include fetal cleidotomy, maternal symphysiotomy, abdominal rescue via hysterotomy, or Zavanelli cephalic replacement plus CS ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.2](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — pp. e124, e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Strict Don'ts
  - NEVER apply fundal pressure (increases brachial plexus injury and uterine rupture risk)
  - NEVER apply excessive downward or lateral traction on the fetal head or neck
  - Do NOT use axillary slings (risk of soft-tissue degloving injury)
  - Do NOT encourage maternal pushing while performing disimpaction manoeuvres
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 42 — §6.2, §6.3.1–6.3.3](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42), [ACOG PB 178 — pp. e124, e127–e128](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)

### Step: Post-Delivery Care & Documentation
- **Points:**
  - Anticipate PPH (11%) and 3rd/4th-degree tears (3.8%); perform systematic vaginal, perineal, and rectal examination (see Postpartum haemorrhage) ([RCOG GTG 42 — §2, §6.7](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)).
  - Sample paired umbilical cord gases; alert neonatologists for early volume replacement if resuscitation response is delayed ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.5](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
  - Examine neonate for brachial plexus injury, clavicle or humerus fracture, pneumothorax, and HIE; debrief parents ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.5, §6.7](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
- **Checklist:** Structured Documentation
  - Time of head delivery, time of body delivery, and which shoulder was anterior
  - Exact sequence and timing of every manoeuvre performed
  - Names and roles of all staff present and time they arrived
  - Paired cord blood gas results, Apgar scores, and neonatal examination findings
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 42 — §2, §6.5, §6.7, §7.2](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42), [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)

### Note
- RCOG 2026 removed the 30-second-per-manoeuvre rule and notes no published evidence supports a "rocking" suprapubic pressure technique ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.3.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
- Apparently normal paired umbilical cord gases can be falsely reassuring after shoulder dystocia; evaluate neonatal condition clinically ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.5](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42)).
- Prophylactic McRoberts' manoeuvre before head delivery is not recommended ([RCOG GTG 42 — §6.1](https://www.ovid.com/journals/bjogy/fulltext/10.1111/1471-0528.70258~shoulder-dystocia-green-top-guideline-no-42); [ACOG PB 178 — p. e129](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/shoulder-dystocia-number-178-may-2017.pdf)).

## Protocol: uterine-inversion
- **Title:** Acute uterine inversion
- **Summary:** Immediate manual uterine replacement, uterotonic timing, relaxation options, and shock resuscitation for acute puerperal uterine inversion.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `uterine inversion`, `Johnson manoeuvre`, `O'Sullivan`, `hydrostatic`, `Huntington`, `Haultain`, `neurogenic shock`, `PPH`

### Step: Recognition & Immediate Resuscitation
- **Timing:** Immediate — resuscitate in parallel with replacement
- **Points:**
  - Suspect with severe abdominal pain, shock disproportionate to blood loss, absent/dimpled fundus, and a fleshy vaginal mass ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf); [FOGSI PPH — Table 5, p. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/pph-prevention-and-management-updated-sept-2022.pdf)).
  - Call senior obstetric, anaesthetic, and OT teams; insert two large-bore IV cannulae (14–16G) and give high-flow oxygen ([ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf); [RCOG GTG 52 — §5.3.2, p. e121](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/prevention-and-management-of-postpartum-haemorrhage-green-top-guideline-no-52/)).
  - Infuse isotonic crystalloids (1 L over 15–20 min; up to 2 L awaiting blood) and cross-match 4 units PRBCs ([ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf); [RCOG GTG 52 — Table 2, p. e121](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/prevention-and-management-of-postpartum-haemorrhage-green-top-guideline-no-52/)).
- **Checklist:** Critical Pre-Replacement Don'ts
  - STOP all oxytocin and uterotonics immediately until the uterus is replaced
  - Do NOT detach or remove an attached placenta before replacing the uterus
  - Do NOT wait for full haemodynamic stabilisation before attempting manual replacement
- **Citations:** [ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf), [FOGSI PPH — Table 5, p. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/pph-prevention-and-management-updated-sept-2022.pdf), [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf), [RCOG GTG 52 — §5.3.2, Table 2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/prevention-and-management-of-postpartum-haemorrhage-green-top-guideline-no-52/), [SA Health PPG128 — pp. 4, 7–8](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)

### Step: Immediate Manual Replacement (Johnson Manoeuvre)
- **Timing:** Attempt immediately upon diagnosis before oedema tightens the ring
- **Points:**
  - Place gloved palm against the inverted fundus with fingertips at the uterocervical junction ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf); [SA Health PPG128 — p. 8](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
  - Push steadily upward through the cervical ring along the pelvic axis toward the umbilicus (last part out goes in first) ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf); [SA Health PPG128 — p. 8](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
  - Keep the fist inside the uterus after reduction until firm uterine contraction resumes following uterotonics ([SA Health PPG128 — pp. 8, 10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
- **Citations:** [ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf), [SA Health PPG128 — pp. 8, 10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)

### Step: Tocolysis & Anaesthesia if Cervical Ring Is Tight
- **Points:**
  - Give terbutaline 0.25 mg SC (or 50 mcg slow IV boluses up to 250 mcg; stop if HR >140 bpm) ([SA Health PPG128 — Table 1, p. 9](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
  - Alternatively give GTN 400 mcg sublingual spray (repeat once after 5 min) or 50–100 mcg IV bolus ([SA Health PPG128 — Table 1, p. 9](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines); [StatPearls Inversion — Treatment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK525971/)).
  - Magnesium sulfate 4 g IV is another relaxation option if beta-agonists or GTN are unsuitable ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf); [StatPearls Inversion — Treatment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK525971/)).
  - If reduction fails, transfer to theatre for general anaesthesia with volatile halogenated agent (sevoflurane or halothane) ([SA Health PPG128 — p. 10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines); [StatPearls Inversion — Treatment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK525971/)).
- **Citations:** [ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf), [SA Health PPG128 — Table 1, pp. 9–10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines), [StatPearls Inversion — Treatment](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK525971/)

### Step: Hydrostatic Reduction & Surgical Options
- **Points:**
  - Exclude uterine rupture, then attempt O'Sullivan hydrostatic reduction: infuse warmed 0.9% NaCl (up to 4 L) while sealing introitus ([SA Health PPG128 — pp. 9–10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines); [WHO MCPC — p. P-111](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565493)).
  - If manual and hydrostatic reduction fail, perform laparotomy for Huntington progressive upward traction on round ligaments/corpus ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)).
  - If Huntington fails, perform Haultain posterior longitudinal incision of the constriction ring, reduce uterus, and repair in layers ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)).
  - Use intrauterine balloon tamponade or compression sutures for recurrent inversion; reserve hysterectomy for intractable bleeding or necrosis ([ACOG PB 183 — pp. e175, e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [SA Health PPG128 — pp. 9–10](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines), [WHO MCPC — p. P-111](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565493), [ACOG PB 183 — pp. e175, e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)

### Step: Post-Replacement Care
- **Timing:** Start uterotonics immediately AFTER uterus is replaced
- **Points:**
  - Start oxytocin infusion: 20 IU in 500 mL RL/NS at 40–60 drops/min, or 40 IU over 4 h ([ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf); [RCOG GTG 52 — §5.6.1, p. e130](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/prevention-and-management-of-postpartum-haemorrhage-green-top-guideline-no-52/)).
  - Add second-line uterotonics and tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min for ongoing PPH (see Postpartum haemorrhage) ([ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf); [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 27](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/)).
  - Remove the placenta manually only after the uterus is replaced and firmly contracted ([SA Health PPG128 — p. 11](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
  - Give prophylactic IV antibiotic (single-dose ampicillin 2 g IV or cefazolin 1 g IV; add anaerobic cover if laparotomy) ([WHO MCPC Brief — Table 4, p. 7](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1); [SA Health PPG128 — p. 11](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
- **Citations:** [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf), [RCOG GTG 52 — §5.6.1, p. e130](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/prevention-and-management-of-postpartum-haemorrhage-green-top-guideline-no-52/), [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 27](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/), [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 4, p. 7](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1), [SA Health PPG128 — p. 11](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)

### Note
- No single tocolytic is proven superior for uterine inversion; use cautiously only if haemodynamically stable as uterine relaxation worsens bleeding ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e177](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf); [SA Health PPG128 — Table 1, p. 9](https://www.sahealth.sa.gov.au/wps/wcm/connect/public+content/sa+health+internet/resources/policies/uterine+inversion+-+sa+perinatal+practice+guidelines)).
- WHO 2017 updated intrauterine procedure prophylaxis to single-dose ampicillin 2 g or cefazolin 1 g IV without metronidazole; add broad-spectrum cover if laparotomy or contamination occurs ([WHO MCPC Brief — Table 4, p. 7](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1)).

## Protocol: retained-placenta
- **Title:** Retained placenta
- **Summary:** Time thresholds, initial management, manual removal under anaesthesia, and interventions to avoid for retained placenta.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `retained placenta`, `prolonged third stage`, `MROP`, `manual removal`, `CCT`, `accreta`, `PAS`, `PPH`

### Step: Time Definitions & Initial Stabilisation
- **Timing:** >30 min active management / >60 min physiological
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose retained placenta if not expelled within 30 minutes of birth with active management (60 min with physiological) ([WHO MCPC — p. S-43](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565493); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.20](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
  - Establish IV access immediately, empty the bladder (catheterise if unable to void), and monitor vitals and blood loss ([NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.21, 1.10.33](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations); [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)).
  - Trigger full PPH treatment if blood loss ≥500 mL, or ≥300 mL with abnormal vitals (see Postpartum haemorrhage) ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 22](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/)).
  - If bleeding is absent after 30 minutes, observe up to a further 30 minutes (60 min total) before MROP ([WHO Compendium 2025 — §2.1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615643/); [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 17](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [WHO MCPC — p. S-43](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789241565493), [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.20–1.10.21, 1.10.33](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations), [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 22](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/), [WHO Compendium 2025 — §2.1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615643/), [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 17](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf), [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)

### Step: Controlled Cord Traction & Uterotonic Rules
- **Points:**
  - Perform gentle controlled cord traction with suprapubic uterine counter-traction only during a uterine contraction ([MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 16](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf); [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 16–17](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/)).
  - If physiological management was used, switch to active management with oxytocin 10 IU IM/slow IV at 60 minutes ([NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.11, 1.10.17](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
  - If prophylactic oxytocin was already given and there is NO haemorrhage, do not give additional routine uterotonics ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
  - If excessive bleeding is present, start oxytocin infusion plus tranexamic acid 1 g IV and prepare urgent MROP ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 24, 27, 30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.24](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 16](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf), [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 16–17, 24, 27, 30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/), [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.11, 1.10.17, 1.10.23–1.10.24](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)

### Step: What NOT to Do
- **Points:**
  - Do not give ergometrine or methylergometrine — tetanic contractions can trap the placenta inside the uterus ([WHO Compendium 2025 — §2.1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615643/); [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 3, p. 6](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1)).
  - Do not use prostaglandin E2 alpha (dinoprostone or sulprostone) for retained placenta ([WHO Compendium 2025 — §2.1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615643/)).
  - Do not use umbilical vein oxytocin injection routinely (WHO: research only; NICE: do not use) ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 32](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.22](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
- **Checklist:** Interventions to Avoid
  - Do NOT give ergometrine/methylergometrine for retained placenta
  - Do NOT give prostaglandin E2 alpha (dinoprostone or sulprostone)
  - Do NOT give routine umbilical vein oxytocin injection
  - Do NOT give routine extra IV/IM oxytocin when there is no haemorrhage
  - Do NOT pull on the cord without uterine contraction and suprapubic counter-traction
- **Citations:** [WHO Compendium 2025 — §2.1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615643/), [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 3, p. 6](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1), [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 30, 32](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/), [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.22–1.10.23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)

### Step: Manual Removal of Placenta (MROP)
- **Timing:** Immediately if bleeding; at 60 min if retained without bleeding
- **Points:**
  - Transfer to an obstetric theatre; never perform uterine exploration or MROP without adequate regional or general anaesthesia ([NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.27–1.10.28](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations); [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)).
  - Provide analgesia before any preliminary vaginal assessment and stop immediately if analgesia is inadequate ([NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.25–1.10.26](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
  - Give routine single-dose prophylactic IV antibiotic: ampicillin 2 g IV or cefazolin 1 g IV ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 31](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 4, p. 7](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1)).
  - If no cleavage plane is found or placenta resists detachment, STOP — suspect placenta accreta spectrum and prepare hysterectomy ([ACOG PB 183 — p. e176](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.25–1.10.28](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations), [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf), [WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 31](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/), [WHO MCPC Brief — Table 4, p. 7](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/mca-documents/maternal-nb/mcpc-2017-brief.pdf?sfvrsn=a7daa312_1), [ACOG PB 183 — p. e176](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/postpartum-hemorrhage-number-183-october-2017.pdf)

### Step: Post-MROP Care
- **Points:**
  - Start oxytocin infusion (20 IU in 500 mL RL/NS at 40–60 drops/min) after placental removal and massage fundus ([ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)).
  - Inspect placenta and membranes for completeness and monitor closely for ongoing bleeding or uterine atony (see Postpartum haemorrhage) ([MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 17](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf); [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [ICMR STW PPH — p. 1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1768382528_updatedpostpartumhaemorrhage.pdf), [MoHFW LaQshya SOP — §5.4, p. 17](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/SOP%20for%20DH.pdf)

### Note
- WHO 2025 updated its guidance against routine additional uterotonics for retained placenta when there is no PPH, aligning with NICE NG235 ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 30](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.23](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
- Umbilical vein oxytocin is restricted to research settings by WHO and explicitly not recommended by NICE ([WHO PPH 2025 — Rec 32](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK619236/); [NICE NG235 — Rec 1.10.22](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng235/chapter/Recommendations)).
