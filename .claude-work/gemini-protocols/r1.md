# Batch R1

## Protocol: poisoning-general
- **Title:** Poisoned patient: first steps
- **Summary:** Resuscitation, toxidrome exam, bedside tests, charcoal, lavage don'ts, enhanced elimination, and psychosocial care for acute poisoning.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `poisoning`, `poisoned patient`, `overdose`, `OD`, `self-poisoning`, `deliberate self-harm`, `DSH`, `suicide attempt`, `zeher`, `zehar`, `unknown poison`, `toxidrome`, `activated charcoal`, `gastric lavage`, `stomach wash`, `ipecac`, `decontamination`, `whole bowel irrigation`, `urine alkalinisation`, `NPIC`, `1800 116 117`

### Step: Resuscitate first
- **Timing:** At first contact
- **Points:**
  - Prioritise airway, breathing, circulation, and staff safety; secure airway by rapid sequence intubation if reflexes lost or respiratory failure ([IJCCM Initial — Airway](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Check blood glucose immediately; if unmeasurable and consciousness is reduced, treat empirically as hypoglycaemia ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Keep unconscious children in the recovery position ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- **Citations:** [IJCCM Initial — Initial assessment; Airway](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: History and poison-centre advice
- **Points:**
  - Record agent, amount, and ingestion time; inspect containers, ask about traditional medicines, and check if other children were exposed ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, p. 26](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - After self-harm, establish the means used and discuss removing any remaining accessible lethal means ([NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225)).
  - Call AIIMS National Poisons Information Centre (24×7 toll-free 1800 116 117; landlines 011-26589391 / 26593677) for agent-specific guidance ([AIIMS NPIC — Home page](https://aiims.edu/index.php/en/npic_intro); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, p. 26](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, p. 26](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225), [AIIMS NPIC — Home page](https://aiims.edu/index.php/en/npic_intro)

### Step: Recognise the toxidrome
- **Points:**
  - Assess vital signs, mental status, pupils, mucous membranes, lungs (wheeze/crackles), and skin (burns/moisture/colour) ([NCDC Chemical — Toxidromes](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/glimpse_pdfs/1770271099_CE-3%20(1).pdf)).
  - Confirm unreliable history with toxidrome examination and tests; patients may not show every classic feature ([IJCCM Initial — Risk assessment; Table 1](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)).
- **Checklist:** Toxidromes
  - Cholinergic — confusion/coma, miosis, salivation, lacrimation, sweating, vomiting, diarrhoea, bronchoconstriction, fasciculations, weakness, seizures (OPs, carbamates)
  - Anticholinergic — delirium, hallucinations, mydriasis, hyperthermia, tachycardia, dry flushed skin/mouth, ileus, urinary retention (antihistamines, TCA, atropine)
  - Opioid — CNS depression/coma, miosis, slow breathing/apnoea, hyporeflexia, pulmonary oedema, needle marks (morphine, heroin, methadone)
  - Sympathomimetic — agitation, paranoia, hallucinations, mydriasis, hyperthermia, tachycardia, hypertension, sweating, tremor, hyperreflexia, seizures (cocaine, amphetamines)
  - Sedative-hypnotic — confusion/stupor/coma, variable pupils, hypothermia, bradycardia, hypotension, slow breathing (benzodiazepines, barbiturates, alcohols)
  - Serotonin syndrome — agitation, mydriasis, hyperthermia, tachycardia, tremor, myoclonus, clonus, hyperreflexia, diarrhoea (SSRIs, MAOIs, TCA)
- **Citations:** [IJCCM Initial — Risk assessment; Table 1](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307), [NCDC Chemical — Toxidromes](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/glimpse_pdfs/1770271099_CE-3%20(1).pdf)

### Step: Bedside and laboratory tests
- **Points:**
  - Check glucose, CBC, electrolytes, urea, creatinine, LFTs, lactate, ABG, 12-lead ECG (QRS/rate), and urine pregnancy test ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [IJCCM Initial — Diagnostic testing; Table 2–3](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)).
  - Measure serum paracetamol in all deliberate self-poisonings regardless of stated dose (nomogram applies to acute immediate-release ingestion only) ([ANZ Paracetamol — Initial approach](https://doi.org/10.5694/mja2.50428)).
  - Order targeted levels where management changes (salicylate, iron, lithium, digoxin, toxic alcohols, COHb, methaemoglobin); avoid routine urine drug screens ([IJCCM Initial — Diagnostic testing](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [IJCCM Initial — Diagnostic testing; Table 2–3](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307), [ANZ Paracetamol — Initial approach](https://doi.org/10.5694/mja2.50428)

### Step: Activated charcoal
- **Timing:** Within 1 h (AACT/EAPCCT); up to 6 h for many poisons (CTRC)
- **Points:**
  - Not routine; consider after risk assessment for charcoal-adsorbed poisons (AACT: ≤1 h; CTRC: up to 6 h, later if modified-release/bezoar) ([AACT SDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-200051867); [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807)).
  - Contraindicated without an intact or protected airway; do not intubate or insert an NG tube solely for charcoal ([AACT SDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-200051867); [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807)).
  - Single dose in 8–10 volumes water: ≤1 y 1 g/kg; 1–12 y 25–50 g; adolescents/adults 25–100 g ([WHO Pocket Book — Table 5, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- **Checklist:** Charcoal not indicated
  - Airway not intact or protected
  - Corrosives (acids, alkalis, bleach)
  - Kerosene, petrol, and other petroleum products
  - Iron, lithium, ethanol, methanol, ethylene glycol, arsenic, lead, copper, caesium, metformin, metals
- **Dose:** Activated charcoal — child ≤1 year | 1 g/kg per single dose PO | max — | Mix in 8–10 volumes water (e.g. 5 g in 40 mL); 1–12 y 25–50 g; ≥12 y 25–100 g
- **Citations:** [AACT SDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-200051867), [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, Table 5](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [IJCCM Initial — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)

### Step: Gastric lavage and induced vomiting
- **Points:**
  - Do not perform routine gastric lavage or give ipecac; consider lavage only for life-threatening ingestions <1 h by experienced staff ([AACT Lavage — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2013.770154); [AACT Ipecac — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2013.770153); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Never use salt-water emetics — they can be fatal ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- **Checklist:** Do not
  - Salt-water emetic (can be fatal)
  - Induce vomiting after corrosive or kerosene/petrol ingestion
  - Gastric lavage after corrosives or petroleum products
  - Lavage or charcoal in an unconscious patient without a protected airway
- **Citations:** [AACT Lavage — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2013.770154), [AACT Ipecac — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2013.770153), [IJCCM Initial — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, pp. 27–28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: Enhanced elimination
- **Points:**
  - Multi-dose charcoal (no cathartics): carbamazepine, dapsone, phenobarbital, theophylline (AACT adds quinine; CTRC adds cardiac glycosides, colchicine, phenytoin, thallium) ([AACT MDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-100102451); [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807)).
  - Consider whole-bowel irrigation for sustained-release/enteric-coated drugs, iron, lithium, potassium, or drug packets; avoid in obstruction, ileus, perforation, shock, unprotected airway ([AACT WBI — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2014.989326)).
  - Urine alkalinisation (salicylate, phenobarbital): NaHCO3 1–2 mEq/kg IV bolus, then 100 mEq/L D5W at 250 mL/h (urine pH 7.5–8.5) ([IJCCM Initial — Elimination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)).
  - Correct hypokalaemia before alkalinisation; contraindicated in fluid overload or renal impairment; dialyse toxins with Vd <1 L/kg ([IJCCM Initial — Elimination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)).
- **Dose:** Multiple-dose activated charcoal — repeat dose | 0.5 g/kg per repeat dose PO | max — | After initial standard dose; intact/protected airway only; no cathartics
- **Dose:** Sodium bicarbonate — urine alkalinisation bolus | 1–2 mEq/kg per IV bolus | max — | Then 100 mEq in 1 L D5W at 250 mL/h (±20 mEq KCl); check urine pH q15–30 min (target 7.5–8.5)
- **Citations:** [AACT MDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-100102451), [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807), [AACT WBI — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.3109/15563650.2014.989326), [IJCCM Initial — Elimination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23307)

### Step: Self-poisoning: psychosocial assessment and disposition
- **Timing:** Alongside medical treatment, not after it
- **Points:**
  - Refer to liaison psychiatry (crisis team for children) on arrival; assess alongside medical care without using suicide risk-prediction scales ([NICE NG225 — Rec 1.5.2, 1.6.1–1.6.4, 1.7.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225)).
  - Admit deliberate paediatric iron, pesticide, paracetamol, aspirin, narcotic, or antidepressant ingestions; observe 4–24 h depending on poison ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 28](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Under MHCA 2017 s.115, suicide attempt is not punishable; provide care and share Tele-MANAS 24×7 toll-free helpline 14416 ([MHCA 2017 — §115](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-01/THE%20MENTAL%20HEALTHCARE%20ACT%2C%202017.pdf); [Tele-MANAS — Press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1866498)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.5.2, 1.6.1–1.6.4, 1.7.3, 1.9.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, §1.6.1](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [MHCA 2017 — §115](https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-01/THE%20MENTAL%20HEALTHCARE%20ACT%2C%202017.pdf), [Tele-MANAS — Press release](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=1866498)

### Note
- Charcoal window and route differ: AACT limits to ≤1 h; CTRC allows up to 6 h and advises against unintubated NG placement (WHO allows NG with verified placement) ([AACT SDAC — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1081/CLT-200051867); [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- NCDC 2025 module mentions 1–4 h lavage, salt emetics, and charcoal for alcohol, contradicting WHO (salt emetic fatal) and CTRC (no charcoal for ethanol) ([NCDC Chemical — Fig. 16](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/glimpse_pdfs/1770271099_CE-3%20(1).pdf); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [CTRC Charcoal — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650.2025.2609807)).

## Protocol: corrosives
- **Title:** Corrosive ingestion (acids, alkalis, toilet cleaners)
- **Summary:** Airway protection, decontamination don'ts, perforation red flags, CT vs endoscopy grading, supportive care, and stricture follow-up.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `corrosive`, `caustic`, `acid`, `alkali`, `Harpic`, `toilet cleaner`, `bathroom cleaner`, `phenyl`, `lye`, `tezab`, `tezaab`, `caustic soda`, `sodium hydroxide`, `hydrochloric acid`, `sulphuric acid`, `battery acid`, `drain cleaner`, `bleach`, `oesophageal burn`, `oesophageal stricture`, `Zargar`

### Step: First contact: what not to do
- **Timing:** Pre-hospital and on arrival
- **Points:**
  - Identify agent/container (pH <2 or >10 high risk), intent, co-ingestions, time, volume (sip 30–50 mL; gulp 60–90 mL) ([WSES Caustic — Pre-hospital](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
  - Prevent vomiting and aspiration using antiemetics (metoclopramide) and 45° seated transport; supportive care is mainstay ([WSES Caustic — Pre-hospital; ED](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
- **Checklist:** Do not
  - Induce vomiting or perform gastric lavage
  - Neutralise with weak acid or alkali (exothermic reaction)
  - Give activated charcoal
  - Insert blind NG tube to prevent vomiting or stricture (perforation risk)
  - Give diluents (milk/water) — WSES advises against; WHO advises giving (see Note)
- **Citations:** [WSES Caustic — Pre-hospital; ED](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 29](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: Airway and resuscitation
- **Timing:** Immediate
- **Points:**
  - Secure airway and haemodynamics; prefer fibreoptic laryngoscopy over blind intubation, or perform tracheotomy if necessary ([WSES Caustic — ED management](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
  - Call an anaesthetist urgently for oral burns, stridor, or vapour aspiration (ammonia, formaldehyde); give adequate analgesia ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, p. 26](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [WSES Oesophageal — Etiologies](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [WSES Caustic — ED management](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
- **Citations:** [WSES Caustic — ED management](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, §1.6.1](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [WSES Oesophageal — Etiologies](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)

### Step: Laboratory assessment and perforation red flags
- **Timing:** Immediate surgical review if red flags present
- **Points:**
  - Send CBC, Na+/K+/Cl-/Mg2+/Ca2+, urea, creatinine, LFTs, pH, lactate, alcohol, β-hCG; call AIIMS NPIC 1800 116 117 ([WSES Oesophageal — Investigations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [AIIMS NPIC — Home page](https://aiims.edu/index.php/en/npic_intro)).
  - Acidosis, high lactate, deranged LFTs, leukocytosis, high CRP, AKI, or thrombocytopenia predict necrosis; watch hypocalcaemia (phosphoric/hydrofluoric acid) ([WSES Oesophageal — Investigations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)).
  - Absence of pain or oral burns does NOT exclude severe injury; operate immediately for peritonitis with shock or transmural necrosis ([WSES Caustic — Algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0); [WSES Oesophageal — Surgical treatment](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)).
  - Perforation can occur anytime in the first 2 weeks (peak days 3–12); repeat CT immediately if pain worsens ([IJCCM Corrosive — Clinical features](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305); [WSES Oesophageal — Non-operative](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)).
- **Checklist:** Perforation & Necrosis Red Flags
  - Peritonitis, rebound tenderness, or shock/haemodynamic instability
  - Increasing abdominal pain or new chest pain
  - Free air on neck, chest, or abdominal X-ray
  - Need for ventilatory support
  - Rising acidosis, renal failure, or leukocytosis
  - Dysphagia, odynophagia, drooling, or haemorrhage after deliberate ingestion
- **Citations:** [WSES Oesophageal — Investigations; Surgical treatment](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2), [WSES Caustic — Algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0), [IJCCM Corrosive — Clinical features](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305), [AIIMS NPIC — Home page](https://aiims.edu/index.php/en/npic_intro)

### Step: Contrast CT and endoscopy (Zargar grading)
- **Timing:** CT 3–6 h; endoscopy 3–6 h (or ≤48 h; avoid days 5–15)
- **Points:**
  - WSES: contrast CT neck/chest/abdomen at 3–6 h (no wall enhancement = grade III necrosis → surgery); ISG: endoscopy first ([WSES Oesophageal — Investigations; Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [ISG Caustic — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12664-024-01692-1)).
  - CT grade I: feed immediately, discharge 24–48 h; IIa stricture risk <20%; IIb >80%; III surgery ([WSES Oesophageal — Fig. 2; Non-operative](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)).
  - Perform endoscopy if CT unavailable/uncertain or in children; do within 48 h and avoid days 5–15 (perforation risk) ([WSES Oesophageal — Endoscopy](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [IJCCM Corrosive — Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305)).
  - Zargar ≤2a: early oral feeding and discharge; ≥2b: admit (71.4% of 2b and all grade 3 scar); 3b consider surgery ([ISG Caustic — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12664-024-01692-1); [Zargar — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0016-5107(91)70678-0); [WSES Caustic — Algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
- **Checklist:** Zargar Endoscopic Grades
  - I — erythema and oedema only
  - IIa — haemorrhage, erosions, blisters, ulcers with exudate
  - IIb — circumferential oesophageal ulceration
  - IIIa — scattered deep ulcers with brown, black, or grey discolouration
  - IIIb — extensive deep ulcers with brown, black, or grey discolouration
  - IV — perforation
- **Citations:** [WSES Oesophageal — Investigations; Endoscopy; Fig. 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2), [WSES Caustic — Algorithm](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0), [IJCCM Corrosive — Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305), [ISG Caustic — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12664-024-01692-1), [Zargar — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0016-5107(91)70678-0)

### Step: Supportive care, strictures and psychiatric review
- **Timing:** Dilation 3–6 weeks after ingestion
- **Points:**
  - Do not give routine steroids to prevent strictures (airway symptoms only) or routine antibiotics (only grade 3 with steroids/lung involvement) ([WSES Caustic — ED management](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
  - PPI benefit is unproven though often given in higher grades; resume oral intake early in low-grade injury ([WSES Caustic — ED; Nutrition](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0); [IJCCM Corrosive — Resuscitation](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305)).
  - For severe burns unable to eat, use jejunostomy or nasojejunal feeding (avoid gastrostomy due to gastric outlet obstruction) ([WSES Caustic — Nutrition](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
  - Strictures (≤4 mo): dilate <3 short (<5 cm) at 3–6 weeks; bypass ≥6 months; review grade II at 4–6 months ([WSES Oesophageal — Endoscopy](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [WSES Caustic — Stricture; Reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
  - Mandatory psychiatric review before discharge after self-harm (gastric outlet obstruction can appear days to 6 years later) ([WSES Oesophageal — Non-operative](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.13](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225); [WSES Caustic — GOO](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0)).
- **Citations:** [WSES Caustic — ED; Nutrition; Stricture; GOO; Reconstruction](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0), [WSES Oesophageal — Non-operative; Endoscopy](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2), [IJCCM Corrosive — Resuscitation](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305), [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.13](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225)

### Note
- Dilution differs: WHO advises milk or water promptly; WSES advises against diluents; IJCCM notes injury occurs within minutes ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 29](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [WSES Caustic — Pre-hospital](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0); [IJCCM Corrosive — Resuscitation](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23305)).
- Initial imaging differs: WSES relies on contrast CT at 3–6 h first (endoscopy in children); ISG prefers endoscopy first ([WSES Oesophageal — Investigations](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2); [ISG Caustic — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12664-024-01692-1)).
- NG tubes: WSES 2015 advises against prophylactic NG placement; WSES 2019 allows NG or jejunostomy for enteral feeding when unable to eat ([WSES Caustic — ED management](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-015-0039-0); [WSES Oesophageal — Non-operative](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13017-019-0245-2)).

## Protocol: carbon-monoxide
- **Title:** Carbon monoxide poisoning
- **Summary:** Immediate 100% oxygen, blood COHb confirmation, cardiac workup, hyperbaric oxygen criteria, pregnancy, fire-smoke cyanide, and delayed sequelae.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `carbon monoxide`, `CO`, `CO poisoning`, `angithi`, `sigri`, `coal stove`, `room heater`, `smoke inhalation`, `fire`, `generator`, `gas geyser`, `COHb`, `hyperbaric oxygen`, `HBO`, `HBOT`, `cyanide`, `hydroxocobalamin`, `Cyanokit`

### Step: Suspect CO and give 100% oxygen immediately
- **Timing:** Immediately, before lab confirmation
- **Points:**
  - Suspect CO with headache, dizziness, nausea, chest pain, or altered mentation without fever; test others exposed in same household ([CDC CO — Recognition](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html); [CDC HAN-00476 — Recommendations](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/han/2022/han00476.html)).
  - Give 100% high-flow oxygen immediately until symptom-free (usually 4–5 h; minor cases up to 12 h) or CO ruled out ([ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32); [CDC CO — Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - COHb half-life drops from ~5 h on air to 85 min on high-flow non-rebreather and 20 min with hyperbaric oxygen ([ACEP CO 2025 — Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005)).
- **Checklist:** Clues to CO Poisoning
  - No fever with flu-like or neurological symptoms
  - Exposure to generator, angithi, coal stove, gas geyser, or room heater
  - Multiple household members with similar complaints
- **Citations:** [CDC CO — Recognition; Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html), [CDC HAN-00476 — Recommendations](https://archive.cdc.gov/www_cdc_gov/han/2022/han00476.html), [ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32), [ACEP CO 2025 — Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: Confirm COHb and screen heart, brain, pregnancy
- **Points:**
  - Measure blood COHb by venous or arterial CO-oximetry; never rely on conventional pulse oximetry or noninvasive pulse CO-oximetry ([CDC CO — Diagnosis](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html); [ACEP CO 2017 — Critical Q1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2016.11.003); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - COHb >2% (non-smokers) or >9% (smokers) supports diagnosis, though levels fall over time and correlate poorly with clinical severity ([CDC CO — Confirmation](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
  - Obtain ECG and cardiac biomarkers in moderate–severe poisoning (myocardial injury increases 10-year mortality) and pregnancy test in childbearing women ([ACEP CO 2017 — Critical Q3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2016.11.003); [CDC CO — Diagnosis; Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
  - Perform serial neurological exams; obtain CXR and head CT/MRI in loss of consciousness or cardiopulmonary signs ([CDC CO — Diagnosis; Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
- **Citations:** [CDC CO — Diagnosis; Confirmation; Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html), [ACEP CO 2017 — Critical Q1, Q3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2016.11.003), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: Hyperbaric oxygen (HBOT) and pregnancy
- **Timing:** Weigh severity against transfer distance and time
- **Points:**
  - Consider HBOT with a toxicologist/hyperbaric centre for severe symptoms, weighing transport risks, barotrauma, and oxygen-toxicity seizures ([ACEP CO 2025 — Critical Q1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005); [CDC CO — Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
  - In pregnancy, HBOT is recommended regardless of COHb or milder symptoms ([ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32); [CDC CO — Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
  - Do not give HBOT to asymptomatic patients presenting >24 hours after exposure ended ([ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32)).
- **Checklist:** HBOT Indications
  - Altered or lost consciousness (transient or prolonged)
  - Neurological, respiratory, or psychological signs; abnormal neuropsychiatric testing
  - Cardiac involvement or severe acidosis
  - COHb >25–30%
  - Pregnancy (regardless of presentation or COHb)
- **Citations:** [ACEP CO 2025 — Critical Q1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005), [ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32), [CDC CO — Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)

### Step: Fire smoke: think cyanide
- **Timing:** Do not delay antidote awaiting lactate
- **Points:**
  - Suspect cyanide in enclosed-space fire with soot around mouth/nose/oropharynx and altered mentation; lactate ≥10 mmol/L strongly supports cyanide ([FDA Cyanokit — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d56fcc8d-bd64-46ab-b0c0-2124bd745a6b)).
  - Hydroxocobalamin 5 g IV/15 min (separate line); repeat 5 g over 15 min–2 h (max 10 g; child 70 mg/kg) ([FDA Cyanokit — §2.2, §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d56fcc8d-bd64-46ab-b0c0-2124bd745a6b)).
  - Monitor BP during infusion and renal function for 7 days; red colour interferes with colorimetric labs and dialysis alarms ([FDA Cyanokit — §5.3–5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d56fcc8d-bd64-46ab-b0c0-2124bd745a6b)).
  - Avoid sodium nitrite in smoke inhalation/CO poisoning — causes hypotension and methaemoglobinaemia, worsening hypoxia ([FDA Nithiodote — Boxed warning; §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ff4941b3-9901-4aab-adcf-c5327bede34e)).
- **Citations:** [FDA Cyanokit — §2.1–2.2, §5.3–5.5, §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=d56fcc8d-bd64-46ab-b0c0-2124bd745a6b), [FDA Nithiodote — Boxed warning; §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ff4941b3-9901-4aab-adcf-c5327bede34e)

### Step: Disposition and delayed neurological sequelae
- **Points:**
  - Delayed neuropsychiatric sequelae can emerge 2–40 days post-exposure (risks: age ≥36 y, COHb ≥25%, exposure ≥24 h, LOC, abnormal CT) ([ACEP CO 2025 — Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005)).
  - Warn every discharged patient and arrange repeat medical and neurological examination at 2 weeks ([CDC CO — Common questions](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
  - Notify fire/emergency services to inspect the source before return; forbid indoor generators, stoves, or charcoal heaters ([CDC CO — Common questions](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)).
- **Citations:** [ACEP CO 2025 — Introduction](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005), [CDC CO — Common questions](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html)

### Note
- HBOT strength differs: ACEP 2025 gives only a conditional option weighing transfer harms, whereas ECHM strongly recommends HBOT for neurological/cardiac signs or pregnancy ([ACEP CO 2025 — Critical Q1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annemergmed.2024.12.005); [ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32)).
- CDC lists COHb >25–30% as an HBOT consideration, while ECHM bases HBOT on clinical signs regardless of COHb ([CDC CO — Management](https://www.cdc.gov/carbon-monoxide/hcp/clinical-guidance/index.html); [ECHM 2016 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.28920/dhm47.1.24-32)).

## Protocol: hydrocarbons
- **Title:** Hydrocarbon ingestion (kerosene, petrol)
- **Summary:** Aspiration pneumonitis recognition, decontamination don'ts, respiratory support, chest X-ray timing, 6-hour observation, and avoiding routine steroids/antibiotics.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `kerosene`, `mitti ka tel`, `mitti da tel`, `petrol`, `diesel`, `gasoline`, `turpentine`, `thinner`, `paraffin oil`, `lamp oil`, `hydrocarbon`, `petroleum`, `aspiration pneumonitis`, `chemical pneumonitis`

### Step: Recognise aspiration risk and decontamination don'ts
- **Timing:** At presentation
- **Points:**
  - Aspiration of even <1 mL causes severe chemical pneumonitis and delayed pulmonary oedema; ingestion can also cause encephalopathy ([IJCCM Kerosene — Pathophysiology](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 30](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Remove soiled clothing and wash contaminated skin or eyes with soap and water ([IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.2, p. 29](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
  - Do NOT induce vomiting, lavage, or give charcoal (aspiration risk exceeds systemic absorption; same for petrol-solvent pesticides) ([IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, §1.6.4](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)).
- **Checklist:** Signs of Aspiration & Strict Don'ts
  - Signs: coughing/choking/gagging, vomiting, tachypnoea, retractions, grunting, wheeze, cyanosis, low SpO2, drowsiness/seizures
  - Do NOT induce vomiting or use salt-water emetic
  - Do NOT perform gastric lavage
  - Do NOT give activated charcoal
- **Citations:** [IJCCM Kerosene — Pathophysiology; Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1–1.6.2, §1.6.4](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3)

### Step: Oxygen, respiratory support and chest X-ray
- **Timing:** CXR changes usually appear within 6 h (range 20 min–24 h)
- **Points:**
  - Give humidified oxygen for respiratory distress/cyanosis, nebulised β2-agonists for bronchospasm, and restrict fluids to maintenance ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 30](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
  - Intubate early with a cuffed tube for refractory hypoxaemia or severe CNS depression; ventilate lung-protectively (6 mL/kg, plateau <30) ([IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
  - Obtain chest X-ray for all symptomatic cases (bibasal/perihilar infiltrates usually appear within 6 h; pneumatoceles at 2–3 weeks) ([IJCCM Kerosene — Investigations](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.4, p. 30](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment; Investigations](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)

### Step: Observe at least 6 hours
- **Timing:** Minimum 6 h observation before discharge
- **Points:**
  - Observe every child for at least 6 hours; symptoms almost always start within 6 h and peak at 24–48 h ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, pp. 26–27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [IJCCM Kerosene — Disposition](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
  - Discharge after 6–8 hours only if completely asymptomatic with normal saturation and return precautions; admit if any symptoms develop ([IJCCM Kerosene — Disposition](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316); [Anas 1981 — Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7253158/)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6, pp. 26–27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [IJCCM Kerosene — Disposition](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316), [Anas 1981 — Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7253158/)

### Step: No routine steroids or antibiotics; prevention
- **Points:**
  - Do not give routine corticosteroids or prophylactic antibiotics; early fever (≤30%) usually settles within 24 h ([IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
  - Suspect bacterial superinfection and start antibiotics only if fever persists beyond 48–72 h or definite infection signs appear ([IJCCM Kerosene — Clinical manifestations; Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).
  - Counsel against storing kerosene in beverage bottles; arrange psychosocial assessment for deliberate ingestions ([IJCCM Kerosene — Prevention](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316); [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.5, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225)).
- **Citations:** [IJCCM Kerosene — Clinical manifestations; Treatment; Prevention](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316), [WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.5, p. 33](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3), [NICE NG225 — Rec 1.7.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng225)

### Note
- WHO suggests giving water or milk orally after petroleum ingestion, whereas IJCCM omits oral dilution because vomiting increases aspiration risk ([WHO Pocket Book — §1.6.1, p. 27](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/978-92-4-154837-3); [IJCCM Kerosene — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23316)).

## Protocol: organophosphate
- **Title:** Organophosphate and carbamate poisoning
- **Summary:** Cholinergic toxidrome recognition, rapid doubling atropine titration, pralidoxime controversy, intermediate syndrome monitoring, and ventilation.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `OP`, `OPC`, `organophosphorus`, `insecticide`, `pesticide`, `carbamate`, `chlorpyrifos`, `monocrotophos`, `dichlorvos`, `DDVP`, `malathion`, `parathion`, `dimethoate`, `fenthion`, `profenofos`, `Baygon`, `propoxur`, `atropine`, `pralidoxime`, `PAM`, `2-PAM`, `intermediate syndrome`, `miosis`, `bronchorrhoea`, `SLUDGE`, `kitnashak`

### Step: Recognise cholinergic toxidrome & secure airway
- **Timing:** At first contact — before decontamination
- **Points:**
  - Key signs: pinpoint pupils, profuse sweating, bronchorrhoea/bronchospasm, bradycardia (may be normal/fast), and hypotension ([Eddleston 2004 — Recognition](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Atropine reverses muscarinic signs (secretions, bradycardia), not nicotinic muscle weakness; fat-soluble OPs (fenthion) cause delayed/recurrent crises ([Peter 2014 — Acute symptoms](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.144017); [Eddleston 2004 — Recognition](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Secure airway (intubate if unconscious), give high-flow oxygen, place left lateral head-down, record GCS/SpO2, and treat seizures with IV diazepam ([Eddleston 2004 — Initial assessment; Airway](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Never delay atropine even if oxygen is unavailable ([Eddleston 2004 — Care of airway](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
- **Checklist:** Cholinergic Signs
  - Symmetrical pinpoint pupils and drenched skin
  - Bronchorrhoea, wheeze, or lung crepitations
  - Bradycardia (may be absent) and hypotension
  - Salivation, vomiting, diarrhoea, incontinence
  - Fasciculations, muscle weakness, agitation, convulsions, or coma
  - Garlic or kerosene-like pesticide odour
- **Citations:** [Eddleston 2004 — Recognition; Initial assessment; Airway](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953), [Peter 2014 — Acute symptoms](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.144017)

### Step: Rapid atropine loading to atropinisation
- **Timing:** Reassess and double every 3–5 min
- **Points:**
  - Unconscious/severe: give atropine 1.8–3 mg (three to five 0.6 mg vials) rapid IV with 500–1000 mL saline ([Eddleston 2004 — Loading](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Unclear presentation: give 0.6–1 mg IV (HR rise >20–25/min with flushing suggests no significant cholinergic poisoning) ([Eddleston 2004 — Loading](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Reassess every 3–5 min; if no consistent improvement, DOUBLE the previous dose — never repeat the same dose ([Eddleston 2004 — Continuation](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Target clear chest, HR >80/min, SBP >80 mmHg, dry axillae, and non-pinpoint pupils (HR 120–140/min indicates toxicity) ([Eddleston 2004 — Table 2](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
- **Checklist:** Atropinisation End-Points
  - Chest clear, no wheeze
  - Heart rate >80 beats/min
  - Systolic BP >80 mmHg
  - Pupils no longer pinpoint (may dilate late)
  - Axillae dry
- **Citations:** [Eddleston 2004 — Loading; Table 2; Continuation](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)

### Step: Atropine infusion and toxicity
- **Timing:** Review every 15 min initially, then hourly for 6 h
- **Points:**
  - Start infusion at 10–20% of total loading dose per hour (less if >30 mg loaded; >3–5 mg/h rarely needed) ([Eddleston 2004 — After atropinization](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - If cholinergic signs recur, give bolus atropine until clear and increase the infusion rate ([Eddleston 2004 — After atropinization](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Atropine toxicity (confusion + fever + absent bowel sounds): stop infusion, recheck at 30 min, restart at 70–80% rate ([Eddleston 2004 — Atropine toxicity](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Cool fever early (fan/wet towels); treat agitation with diazepam 10 mg slow IV (max 30–40 mg/24 h) ([Eddleston 2004 — Active cooling](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
- **Citations:** [Eddleston 2004 — After atropinization; Toxicity; Cooling](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)

### Step: Pralidoxime and decontamination
- **Timing:** Decontaminate only after stabilisation
- **Points:**
  - WHO regimen (disputed): pralidoxime 30 mg/kg IV over 10–20 min, then 8–10 mg/kg/h until recovery/7 days; avoid in carbamates ([Eddleston 2004 — Oximes](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Avoid rapid pralidoxime (vomiting, tachycardia, hypertension); Indian open protocol: 2 g load then 1 g/h × 48 h ([Eddleston 2004 — Oximes](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953); [Pawar 2006 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(06)69862-0)).
  - Ipecac is contraindicated; consider lavage only for highly toxic pesticide <1–2 h in calm/intubated patient; no proven charcoal benefit ([Eddleston 2004 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Aspiration causes chemical pneumonitis (suspect infection if fever >48 h/focal consolidation); MgSO4 4 g IV lacks mortality benefit ([Eddleston 2004 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953); [Vijayakumar 2017 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.4103/0259-1162.194585)).
- **Dose:** Pralidoxime — WHO loading dose (disputed) | 30 mg/kg once IV over 10–20 min | max — | Not for carbamates; rapid infusion causes vomiting, tachycardia, hypertension
- **Dose:** Pralidoxime — WHO maintenance infusion (disputed) | 8–10 mg/kg per hour continuous IV | max — | Until clinical recovery (12–24 h off atropine/extubated) or 7 days
- **Citations:** [Eddleston 2004 — Oximes; Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953), [Cochrane Oximes — Conclusions](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005085.pub2), [Pawar 2006 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(06)69862-0), [Vijayakumar 2017 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.4103/0259-1162.194585)

### Step: Intermediate syndrome and late monitoring
- **Timing:** 24–96 h after poisoning (weakness up to 18 days)
- **Points:**
  - Watch from day 1 for intermediate syndrome (24–96 h; up to 114 h with methamidophos): neck-flexor, proximal, cranial-nerve, respiratory weakness ([Peter 2014 — Delayed symptoms](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.144017); [Eddleston 2004 — Respiratory failure](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Intubate and ventilate early for respiratory failure; monitor hourly for 6 h then every 2–3 h (watch recurrent fenthion crises) ([Eddleston 2004 — Observation; Care](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
  - Late encephalopathy (~4 d), cerebellar ataxia (8 d), extrapyramidal signs (5–15 d) occur; treat clinically without awaiting cholinesterase ([Peter 2014 — Delayed symptoms](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.144017); [Eddleston 2004 — Confirmation](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)).
- **Checklist:** Early Intermediate-Syndrome Signs
  - Cannot lift head off pillow (weak neck flexion)
  - Accessory muscle use, nasal flaring, or tachypnoea
  - Cranial nerve palsies or failed extubation (laryngeal paralysis)
  - Proximal limb weakness with preserved distal strength
- **Citations:** [Peter 2014 — Delayed symptoms](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.144017), [Eddleston 2004 — Observation; Care; Confirmation](https://doi.org/10.1186/cc2953)

### Note
- Pralidoxime is disputed: Cochrane 2011 and placebo-controlled trials found no mortality or ventilation benefit for WHO/standard regimens, while an Indian open trial reported benefit at 1 g/h ([Cochrane Oximes — Conclusions](https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD005085.pub2); [Eddleston 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1000104); [Pawar 2006 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(06)69862-0); [Kharel 2020 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.7759/cureus.7174)).

## Protocol: aluminium-phosphide
- **Title:** Aluminium phosphide poisoning
- **Summary:** Staff protection, refractory shock, metabolic acidosis, arrhythmia, and organ support for phosphine poisoning — no specific antidote exists.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `Celphos`, `ALP`, `AlP`, `aluminium phosphide`, `aluminum phosphide`, `wheat tablet`, `rice tablet`, `sulfas`, `sulphas`, `phosphine`, `Quickphos`, `Alphos`, `Phostoxin`, `metal phosphide`, `garlic odour`, `gehu ki goli`

### Step: Recognise, protect staff and initial workup
- **Timing:** Toxicity develops within minutes; hours 0–12 critical
- **Points:**
  - Confirm product (dark-grey 3 g tablets release phosphine on acid/moisture contact; some herbal "rice tablets" are not AlP) ([Gurjar 2011 — Properties; Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
  - Protect staff with full face mask and rubber gloves — phosphine in vomit/stool is hazardous and can rarely ignite ([Gurjar 2011 — Evaluation](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [D'Silva 2019 — Clinical Features](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Order ECG (tachycardia 3–6 h, ST-T/conduction changes 6–12 h), CXR, glucose, ABG, electrolytes + Mg2+, LFT, RFT, and echo ([Gurjar 2011 — Clinical features; Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
  - Treat on clinical suspicion without waiting for black silver-nitrate paper test on gastric aspirate ([Gurjar 2011 — Diagnosis](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
- **Checklist:** Features of Phosphide Toxicity
  - History of Celphos, wheat tablet, or rice tablet ingestion (especially freshly opened pack)
  - Garlic or decaying-fish breath or vomitus odour
  - Early vomiting and epigastric pain
  - Refractory hypotension (76–100%) and metabolic acidosis
  - ST-T changes, conduction defects, or arrhythmias
- **Citations:** [Gurjar 2011 — Properties; Clinical features; Diagnosis; Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868), [D'Silva 2019 — Clinical Features](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Step: Decontamination (within 1–2 h only)
- **Timing:** Potential benefit only within 1–2 h
- **Points:**
  - Never perform gut decontamination with an unprotected airway; no specific antidote exists ([Gurjar 2011 — Exposure](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [Proudfoot 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650802520675)).
  - Lavage controversial (speeds disintegration): Gurjar uses 1:10,000 KMnO4; Farahani favours vegetable oil/paraffin (warns KMnO4 causes haemolysis/methaemoglobinaemia) ([Gurjar 2011 — Exposure](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [D'Silva 2019 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [Farahani 2016 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712)).
- **Citations:** [Gurjar 2011 — Exposure](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868), [D'Silva 2019 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318), [Farahani 2016 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712), [Proudfoot 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650802520675)

### Step: Shock and haemodynamic support
- **Timing:** Continuous invasive ICU monitoring
- **Points:**
  - Start invasive monitoring (CVP/PAWP) and early vasoactives: norepinephrine or phenylephrine preferred (dopamine/dobutamine provoke arrhythmias) ([Gurjar 2011 — Hemodynamic support](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
  - Keep anti-arrhythmics, DC cardioversion, and temporary pacing at bedside; shock often resists large crystalloid volumes ([Gurjar 2011 — Hemodynamic support](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [Farahani 2016 — Hypotension](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712)).
  - Consider VA-ECMO or IABP where available for refractory cardiogenic shock (myocardial dysfunction reverses by day 5 in survivors) ([Farahani 2016 — ECMO](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712); [Gurjar 2011 — Hemodynamic; Prognostic](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
- **Citations:** [Gurjar 2011 — Hemodynamic support; Prognostic](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868), [Farahani 2016 — Hypotension; ECMO](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712)

### Step: Acidosis, methaemoglobinaemia and organ support
- **Points:**
  - Give IV sodium bicarbonate for severe acidosis (Farahani: pH <7 only); dialyse for AKI, severe acidosis, or fluid overload ([Gurjar 2011 — Organ failures](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [Farahani 2016 — Acidosis](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712)).
  - Oxygen-refractory cyanosis: confirm methaemoglobin (co-oximetry) and give 1% methylene blue 2 mg/kg IV over 5 min (avoid in G6PD deficiency) ([Gurjar 2011 — Organ failures](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [FDA Provayblue — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=4f6848e5-35ed-4046-b13c-3032b5ba3232)).
  - Correct hypoglycaemia; routine MgSO4 (3 g/3 h then 6 g/24 h × 3–5 d) and NAC lack mortality benefit ([Gurjar 2011 — Organ failures; Supportive](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [D'Silva 2019 — Supportive](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Most deaths ≤24 h (mortality 30–100%, >1.5 g usually fatal); check dysphagic survivors for oesophageal strictures and offer counselling ([Gurjar 2011 — Prognostic; Supportive](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
- **Dose:** Methylene blue 1% — symptomatic methaemoglobinaemia | 2 mg/kg per dose IV over 5 min | max — | Confirm by co-oximetry; contraindicated in G6PD deficiency (haemolysis risk); repeatable if cyanosis persists
- **Citations:** [Gurjar 2011 — Organ failures; Supportive; Prognostic](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868), [Farahani 2016 — Acidosis; Cardiac; Antioxidant](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712), [FDA Provayblue — Contraindications; Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=4f6848e5-35ed-4046-b13c-3032b5ba3232), [D'Silva 2019 — Supportive](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Note
- Decontamination conflicts: KMnO4 and coconut oil lavage are widely practised in India, but reviews report lacking evidence and potential KMnO4 haemolysis/methaemoglobinaemia; sources also disagree on charcoal ([D'Silva 2019 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [Farahani 2016 — Decontamination](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712); [Gurjar 2011 — Exposure](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868)).
- Evidence for MgSO4, NAC, trimetazidine, and high-dose insulin is conflicting or limited to small studies/case reports; no antidote exists ([Gurjar 2011 — Supportive](https://doi.org/10.4103/0974-2700.83868); [Farahani 2016 — Cardiac; Antioxidant](https://doi.org/10.4103/0972-5229.195712); [Proudfoot 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650802520675)).

## Protocol: zinc-phosphide
- **Title:** Zinc phosphide poisoning
- **Summary:** Delayed-onset metal phosphide rodenticide causing abdominal X-ray radiopacity, misleading symptom-free intervals, and acute liver failure at 3–6 days.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `zinc phosphide`, `ZnP`, `Zn3P2`, `Rat kill`, `ratkill`, `rat poison`, `rodenticide`, `rat powder`, `metal phosphide`, `phosphine`, `rat bait`

### Step: Identify product and protect staff
- **Points:**
  - Identify agent (zinc phosphide: grey powder; AlP: dark crystals; yellow phosphorus: waxy paste) and call AIIMS NPIC ([D'Silva 2019 — General Approach](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Protect staff from phosphine gas released in vomit and stool on contact with gastric acid ([D'Silva 2019 — Aluminium and Zinc Phosphide](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — General Approach; Aluminium and Zinc Phosphide](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Step: Expected delayed course and serial tests
- **Timing:** Phases over hours to 6 days — do not discharge early
- **Points:**
  - 30–60% are initially asymptomatic; GI burning/vomiting starts at 2–6 h, followed by a misleading symptom-free interval at 12–24 h ([Faress 2025 — Clinical manifestations](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
  - Acute liver failure appears at 3–6 days (plus cardiomyopathy, AKI, pulmonary oedema, rhabdomyolysis); observe in ICU for 48–72 h ([Faress 2025 — Clinical manifestations](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048); [D'Silva 2019 — General Approach](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Obtain abdominal X-ray for radiopaque stomach contents (negative film does not exclude toxicity) and serial ALT, AST, INR, ABG, glucose ([Faress 2025 — Diagnostic testing](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
- **Citations:** [Faress 2025 — Clinical manifestations; Diagnostic testing](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048), [D'Silva 2019 — General Approach](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Step: Decontamination cautions
- **Points:**
  - Consider gastric emptying only if early and X-ray shows abundant gastric toxin; avoid in delayed presentation ([Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
  - Exercise extreme caution with gastric lavage (liberates phosphine endangering staff); charcoal, coconut oil, and PEG-ELS lack proven outcome benefit ([D'Silva 2019 — General Approach](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
- **Citations:** [Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048), [D'Silva 2019 — General Approach](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Step: Supportive care and liver failure
- **Points:**
  - No specific antidote exists; manage shock and metabolic acidosis as in Aluminium phosphide poisoning ([Proudfoot 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650802520675); [Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
  - Monitor closely for acute liver failure, coagulopathy, and cardiotoxicity; refer early for liver transplant if liver failure progresses ([Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048); [Mohanka 2020 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Antioxidants (NAC, L-carnitine, vitamin E, CoQ10) lack randomised zinc-phosphide trials ([Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048)).
- **Citations:** [Proudfoot 2009 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1080/15563650802520675), [Faress 2025 — Management](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048), [Mohanka 2020 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)

### Note
- Zinc phosphide mortality varies widely across series (1.3–7% vs 37–100% extrapolated from aluminium phosphide); rigorous trials are lacking ([Faress 2025 — Introduction](https://doi.org/10.31729/jnma.9048); [D'Silva 2019 — Aluminium and Zinc Phosphide](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).

## Protocol: yellow-phosphorus
- **Title:** Yellow phosphorus rodenticide poisoning
- **Summary:** Rat-paste (Ratol) poisoning: 3-stage delayed hepatotoxicity over 72 h, no antidote, ICU liver-failure care, and early transplant evaluation.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `Ratol`, `rat paste`, `rodenticide paste`, `yellow phosphorus`, `white phosphorus`, `rat killer`, `phosphorus paste`, `firecracker poisoning`, `acute liver failure`, `luminescent vomitus`

### Step: Recognise exposure and 3-stage course
- **Timing:** Day 0–1 GI; day 1–3 silent; >72 h acute liver failure
- **Points:**
  - Pastes (e.g. Ratol) contain 2–5% yellow phosphorus; lethal dose ~1 mg/kg (fulminant >1–2 g); garlic odour, luminescent vomit/stool ([D'Silva 2019 — Yellow Phosphorus](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - 0–24 h: GI irritation; 24–72 h: silent LFT/bilirubin rise; >72 h: ALF, coagulopathy, shock, arrhythmias, AKI, coma ([D'Silva 2019 — Mechanism](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Serum phosphorus does not estimate ingested dose; check calcium (hypocalcaemia) and admit to ICU for daily LFTs and RFTs ([D'Silva 2019 — Yellow Phosphorus; Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — Yellow Phosphorus; Mechanism; Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Step: Early decontamination and N-acetylcysteine
- **Timing:** Within 24 h (best within a few hours)
- **Points:**
  - No specific antidote exists; Mohanka uses plain saline lavage if ≤24 h (D'Silva describes 1:1000 KMnO4 lavage) ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Indian liver-ICU protocol gives IV NAC 100 mg/kg over 24 h for 5 days (observational evidence only) ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010); [Jubin 2024 — Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11886211/)).
  - Avoid corticosteroids (not useful, possibly harmful) ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318), [Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010), [Jubin 2024 — Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11886211/)

### Step: Acute liver failure care
- **Timing:** Monitor closely days 3–5 post-ingestion
- **Points:**
  - Nurse 30° head-up; check encephalopathy/pupils hourly; electively intubate for severe hepatic encephalopathy (grade ≥2–3) ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1; Management](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - INR >2: give vitamin K 10 mg IV × 3 days; reserve FFP, cryoprecipitate, platelets, tranexamic acid for active bleeding ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Use CVVHDF for AKI, severe lactic acidosis, or ammonia >150 µmol/L twice (>200 once); give lactulose and rifaximin ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Monitor glucose, electrolytes, lactate, ammonia, and daily SOFA score; consider plasmapheresis for systemic inflammation ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
- **Citations:** [Mohanka 2020 — Table 1; Management](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)

### Step: Prognosis and transplant referral
- **Timing:** Evaluate for liver transplant from admission
- **Points:**
  - Refer early to a liver-transplant centre; list super-urgent if King's College or NHSBT criteria met and worsening despite medical care ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1; Results](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Spontaneous survival associated with <17.5 g ingested, no cardiotoxicity, HE <grade 3, peak lactate <5.8, and peak SOFA <14.5 ([Mohanka 2020 — Results; Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
  - Transplant requires recovery from marrow suppression and cardiotoxicity, without irreversible brain injury or systemic infection ([Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).
- **Citations:** [Mohanka 2020 — Results; Table 1; Table 3](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)

### Note
- Lavage additives differ: D'Silva describes 1:1000 KMnO4, whereas Mohanka advises against KMnO4, charcoal, and copper sulfate (plain saline only) ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [Mohanka 2020 — Table 1](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jceh.2020.09.010)).

## Protocol: superwarfarin
- **Title:** Superwarfarin (bromadiolone, brodifacoum) poisoning
- **Summary:** Delayed coagulopathy from long-acting anticoagulant rodenticides requiring prolonged high-dose vitamin K1 and plasma/PCC for active bleeding.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `superwarfarin`, `bromadiolone`, `brodifacoum`, `difenacoum`, `flocoumafen`, `coumarin`, `hydroxycoumarin`, `anticoagulant rodenticide`, `LAAR`, `rat poison`, `vitamin K`, `high INR`, `prolonged PT`

### Step: Recognise delayed bleeding and test coagulation
- **Timing:** INR rises ~48 h post-exposure; may be delayed days to weeks
- **Points:**
  - Coagulopathy appears 24–48 h post-ingestion once clotting factors fall <30% (brodifacoum half-life 16–34 days; persists 16–270 days) ([D'Silva 2019 — Coumarins; Table 2](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Suspect in unexplained prolonged PT/INR and aPTT with multisite bleeding (haematuria, epistaxis, ecchymoses, intracranial) without DIC ([D'Silva 2019 — Coumarins](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002)).
  - Monitor daily PT/INR and aPTT after exposure; factor assays (II, VII, IX, X, V) support diagnosis ([D'Silva 2019 — Diagnosis](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Checklist:** Clues to Superwarfarin Poisoning
  - Unexplained prolonged PT/INR and aPTT
  - Multisite bleeding without DIC
  - No clotting-factor inhibitor present
  - Rodenticide access (even if initially asymptomatic)
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — Coumarins; Diagnosis; Table 2](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318), [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002)

### Step: Vitamin K1 therapy
- **Timing:** Factors rise at 6–8 h; maintain weeks to months (28–730 days)
- **Points:**
  - Clotting factors rise 6–8 h after vitamin K1; check INR at 6–8 h and repeat if prolonged ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [FDA Phytonadione — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=226c0a5c-3e04-65a9-01ac-e67aad9a430a)).
  - No consensus dose: 0.1–3 mg/kg/d (max 500–800 mg/d); bleeding 50–100 mg IV, then ~100 mg PO/d (mean 168 d) ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318); [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002)).
  - Avoid IV/IM vitamin K1 unless SC/PO unfeasible due to fatal anaphylaxis risk; never infuse IV faster than 1 mg/min ([FDA Phytonadione — Boxed warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=226c0a5c-3e04-65a9-01ac-e67aad9a430a)).
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318), [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002), [FDA Phytonadione — Boxed warning; Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=226c0a5c-3e04-65a9-01ac-e67aad9a430a)

### Step: Active bleeding: plasma and PCC
- **Points:**
  - Give fresh frozen plasma (FFP) or prothrombin complex concentrate (PCC) immediately for active or life-threatening bleeding ([D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Watch for paradoxical thrombosis with haemostatic products (PCC/rFVIIa); consider plasma exchange if vitamin K anaphylaxis occurs ([King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002); [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Citations:** [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318), [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002)

### Step: Follow-up and rebound coagulopathy
- **Points:**
  - Continue long-term high-dose oral vitamin K1 and serial INR checks because rebound coagulopathy occurs as toxin persists in liver ([King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002); [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
  - Phenobarbital has been used as an adjunct to speed metabolism; monitor closely for anaphylaxis with vitamin K1 ([King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002); [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)).
- **Citations:** [King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002), [D'Silva 2019 — Treatment](https://doi.org/10.5005/jp-journals-10071-23318)

### Note
- Case-report superwarfarin doses (50–100 mg IV acutely; 100 mg PO daily) exceed the FDA warfarin-reversal label limit (2.5–10 mg, up to 25–50 mg single dose) ([King 2015 — Abstract](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tmrv.2015.06.002); [FDA Phytonadione — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=226c0a5c-3e04-65a9-01ac-e67aad9a430a)).

## Protocol: snakebite
- **Title:** Snakebite
- **Summary:** First aid, 20WBCT, neurotoxic vs haemotoxic syndromes, polyvalent ASV, anaphylaxis, neostigmine, ventilation, AKI, and referral.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `snake`, `snakebite`, `saanp`, `krait`, `cobra`, `naag`, `Russell's viper`, `saw-scaled viper`, `Echis`, `phoorsa`, `ASV`, `anti-snake venom`, `20WBCT`, `neurotoxic`, `haemotoxic`, `ptosis`, `neostigmine`

### Step: First aid, arrival and observation
- **Timing:** Immediate; admit and observe ≥24 h
- **Points:**
  - Reassure (~70% non-venomous), splint limb like a fracture, left-lateral recovery position, NPO, remove rings/tight items, transport via 102/108 ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 7–9](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Do not remove tourniquets in the field; release slowly (proximal last) only after ASV starts with atropine–neostigmine ready ([NAPSE 2024 — pp. 65–66](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf); [MoHFW Snakebite — p. 10](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Resuscitate ABCs, start large-bore IV saline, type/cross-match first blood sample (venom/ASV interfere later), and observe ≥24 h ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 10, 18–22](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Re-examine asymptomatic bites at 2, 6, 12, 24 h; GCS and fixed pupils cannot diagnose brain death in neurotoxic paralysis ([NAPSE 2024 — p. 71](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf); [MoHFW Snakebite — p. 19](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.4.2.3](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
- **Checklist:** First-Aid Don'ts
  - Tourniquet or tight ligature
  - Cutting, suction, rubbing, tattooing, massage, or vigorous wound washing
  - Herbs, chemicals, ice, cautery, electric shock, or "black stone"
  - Injecting or applying ASV at the bite site
  - Delay for traditional healers or trying to catch/kill the snake
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 7–10, 18–22](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [NAPSE 2024 — Ch. 11, pp. 65–74](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.2–6.4](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)

### Step: 20-minute whole blood clotting test (20WBCT)
- **Timing:** Baseline, hourly × 3 h, then 6-hourly to 24 h
- **Points:**
  - Place 2 mL fresh venous blood in a new clean dry glass tube undisturbed for 20 min, then tilt once ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 21](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.6.1, pp. 123–125](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Liquid blood = "not clotted" (coagulopathy); report clotted/not clotted, never positive/negative; plastic/wet tubes give false non-clotting ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 21](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.6.1, p. 124](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Do not delay ASV for spontaneous systemic bleeding (20WBCT sensitivity ~84%); repeat 6 h after ASV loading dose ([NAPSE 2024 — p. 68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf); [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 19, 21](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 19, 21, 68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.6.1, pp. 123–125](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547), [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 68, 76–77](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf)

### Step: Recognise envenoming syndrome
- **Timing:** Cobra 30 min–6 h; krait 6–12 h (up to 16–36 h)
- **Points:**
  - Neuroparalytic (cobra/krait): 5 Ds + 2 Ps (dyspnoea, dysphonia, dysarthria, diplopia, dysphagia; ptosis, descending paralysis); krait often lacks bite marks ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 6, 10–17](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Vasculotoxic (Russell's/saw-scaled viper): painful swelling, blisters, bleeding, non-clotting 20WBCT, shock, AKI (Russell's: capillary leak, parotid swelling, chemosis) ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 10–17](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Anxiety starts within minutes; true envenoming usually ≥30 min; intubate early for impending respiratory failure ([NAPSE 2024 — pp. 67–68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf); [ICMR Paralysis STW — Algorithm](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952346_neurology_acute_paralysis.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Impending Respiratory Failure
  - Single breath count ≤30 (normal >30) or breath-holding time ≤45 s
  - Cannot complete one sentence in one breath
  - Cannot lift head ("broken neck" sign)
  - Pooling of saliva or husky/weak cry in a child
  - Paradoxical (abdominal) breathing or SpO2 <90%
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 6, 10–17, 51](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 67–68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf), [ICMR Paralysis STW — Algorithm](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952346_neurology_acute_paralysis.pdf)

### Step: Anti-snake venom (ASV): indications and dose
- **Timing:** Full dose immediately once envenoming evident
- **Points:**
  - Start polyvalent ASV 10 vials IV over 30 min (NAPSE: 30–90 min); never IM, local, or test dose ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 27–28](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 83–84](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf)).
  - Neurotoxic: give second 10 vials after 1 h if unimproved (max 20 vials) ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 31](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Vasculotoxic: repeat q6h until clotted — high-dose 10→6 vials (low-dose 10 [6 Echis]→2 vials × 3 d; reconsider >30 vials) ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 28, 30–31](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 83–84](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf)).
  - Children and pregnant women receive the exact same vial dose as adults (dilute in 5–10 mL/kg saline for children) ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 30](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.7.8](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
- **Checklist:** ASV Indications
  - Spontaneous systemic bleeding
  - Non-clotting 20WBCT, INR >1.2, or platelets <100,000/mm³
  - Neurotoxic signs: ptosis, ophthalmoplegia, or paralysis
  - Shock, hypotension, arrhythmia, or abnormal ECG
  - Oliguria/anuria, rising creatinine, or dark brown urine
  - Swelling >half limb within 48 h, rapidly spreading swelling, digital bite, or tender regional nodes
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 27–31](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 71, 83–84](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf), [NCDC PHC — ASV box](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.7.2, §6.7.7–6.7.10](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)

### Step: ASV reactions
- **Timing:** Monitor q5min × 30 min, then q15min × 2 h
- **Points:**
  - High risk (past equine ASV, anti-tetanus or anti-rabies serum; severe atopy or asthma): give ASV only for systemic envenoming, with premedication — MoHFW: hydrocortisone 200 mg; WHO: adrenaline 0.25 mg SC, then IV chlorphenamine ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 31–33](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO 2016 — §6.7.5.2](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Keep doctor at bedside with two adrenaline syringes ready; give prophylactic adrenaline 0.25 mg SC unless BP >140/90 or CVD/stroke ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 31–32](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.7.5.3, p. 134](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - At first sign (itch, urticaria, cough, vomiting, hypotension): stop ASV and give IM adrenaline 0.5 mg (1:1000) every 5–10 min ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 32](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.7.5.4, p. 135](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Add IV chlorphenamine 10 mg and hydrocortisone 100 mg (acts late); restart ASV slowly once recovered to finish dose ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 32–33](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.7.5.4](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Serum sickness (1–12 d): chlorphenamine 2 mg q6h×5 d; add prednisolone 5 mg q6h×5 d if unimproved at 24–48 h ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 33](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Adrenaline for ASV reaction — child | 0.01 mg/kg per dose IM (1:1000) | max 0.5 mg | Adults 0.5 mg IM; repeat every 5–10 min if reaction persists
- **Dose:** Prophylactic adrenaline before ASV — child | 0.005 mg/kg once SC just before ASV | max 0.25 mg | Adults 0.25 mg SC; avoid if BP >140/90 or cardiovascular/cerebrovascular disease
- **Dose:** Chlorphenamine — child | 0.2 mg/kg per dose IV | max 10 mg | Adults 10 mg IV
- **Dose:** Hydrocortisone — child | 2 mg/kg per dose IV | max 100 mg | Adults 100 mg IV; acts after several hours
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 29, 31–33](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.7.5, pp. 132–135](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547), [NCDC PHC — Adverse reaction](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf)

### Step: Neurotoxic envenoming: neostigmine and ventilation
- **Timing:** Give 1 atropine–neostigmine dose before transfer
- **Points:**
  - Support ventilation early (intubation/LMA/ventilator) — never rely on ASV alone in bulbar or respiratory paralysis ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 35](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.1, p. 144](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - India: atropine 0.6 mg + neostigmine 1.5 mg IV, then 0.5 mg q30min×5 (taper 1/2/6/12 h); stop if krait/no response×3 ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 35–36](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - WHO: trial in all neurotoxic bites — atropine 0.6 mg IV + neostigmine 0.02 mg/kg IM (max 10 mg/24 h) ([WHO SEARO — §6.9.3, pp. 152–153](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - No response after 3 doses (krait): calcium gluconate 10 mL IV (children 1–2 mL/kg, 1:1 dilution) over 5–10 min q6h ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 36](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - MoHFW: avoid sedatives; WHO: sedate ventilated patients (fentanyl 2–40 µg/h+midazolam 2–4 mg/h, or morphine 1–5+lorazepam 1–2 mg/h) ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 45](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.2, p. 152](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
- **Dose:** Atropine before neostigmine — child | 0.05 mg/kg IV before each neostigmine dose | max 0.6 mg | Adults 0.6 mg IV
- **Dose:** Neostigmine first dose — child | 0.04 mg/kg once IV/IM after atropine | max 1.5 mg | Adults: 1.5 mg IV (MoHFW) or 0.02 mg/kg IM (WHO)
- **Dose:** Neostigmine repeat — child (MoHFW) | 0.01 mg/kg IV with atropine q30min × 5 | max 0.5 mg | Adults 0.5 mg; stop if no ≥50% ptosis recovery after 3 doses
- **Dose:** Neostigmine trial — adult (WHO) | 0.02 mg/kg once IM after atropine 0.6 mg IV | max — | Observe 30–60 min; responders 0.5–2.5 mg q1–3h (max 10 mg/24 h)
- **Dose:** Neostigmine maintenance — child (WHO) | 0.01–0.04 mg/kg every 2–4 h IM/IV/SC with atropine | max 2.5 mg | Only after convincing trial response
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 35–36, 45, 51](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.9.1–6.9.3, pp. 144, 152–153](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547), [NCDC PHC — ASV box](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf)

### Step: Haemotoxic complications: bleeding, shock, AKI
- **Timing:** Forced alkaline diuresis within 24 h only
- **Points:**
  - Strict bed rest, no IM injections or arterial/central punctures, target urine output 1 mL/kg/h; never give heparin or antifibrinolytics ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 37–40](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.4](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Persistent bleeding after ASV or PCV <30%: transfuse blood or FFP 10–15 mL/kg (aim INR <2.0 at 6 h) ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 37–38](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Shock: crystalloid boluses up to 20 mL/kg; start dopamine/dobutamine if crepitations or hepatomegaly develop ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 40](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
  - Oliguria <24 h: forced alkaline diuresis (MoHFW) or furosemide 1–1.5 mg/kg IV (WHO; <100 mL/h over 2 h predicts AKI) ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 38](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.5, pp. 156–159](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Dialyse for creatinine >4 mg/dL, urea >130 mg/dL, K+ >7 mmol/L or ECG changes, fluid overload, encephalopathy, or pericarditis ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 39](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.5](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
- **Dose:** Fresh frozen plasma — persistent coagulopathy after ASV | 10–15 mL/kg IV over 30–60 min | max — | Give within 4 h of ASV; target INR <2.0 at 6 h
- **Dose:** Crystalloid bolus — snakebite shock | 20 mL/kg per bolus IV | max — | Over 5–10 min (children 30 min); stop if crepitations/hepatomegaly
- **Dose:** Furosemide stress test — snakebite oliguria | 1–1.5 mg/kg once slow IV (4–5 mg/min) | max — | After adequate fluids; <100 mL/h over 2 h predicts AKI progression
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 37–40](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.9.4–6.9.5, §6.10](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547), [NCDC PHC — Fluid bolus](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf)

### Step: Wound care, tetanus, discharge and referral
- **Timing:** Discharge ≥24 h (no envenoming) or ≥48 h (after ASV)
- **Points:**
  - Clean bite with povidone-iodine (no dressing, leave blisters); give deltoid tetanus booster ONLY after coagulation normalises ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 45–47](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [NCDC PHC — Tetanus](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf)).
  - Analgesia: paracetamol (adults 500–1000 mg q4–6h, max 4 g/d; tramadol 50 mg if severe); NEVER aspirin or NSAIDs ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 45, 68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.15B](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Fasciotomy ONLY for confirmed compartment syndrome (pressure >40 mmHg) after coagulopathy is corrected; avoid steroids for local swelling ([MoHFW Snakebite — p. 47](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.11–6.13](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
  - Refer (phone ahead) if no ASV, non-clotting post-10 vials, bleeding, AKI, or progressive paralysis; warn serum sickness (5–10 d) ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 49, 51–53](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Paracetamol — child analgesia | 10–15 mg/kg per dose PO every 4–6 h | max 1000 mg | Max 100 mg/kg/day (adults max 4 g/24 h); avoid aspirin/NSAIDs
- **Citations:** [MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 45–53, 68](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf), [WHO SEARO — §6.11–6.13, §6.15B](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547), [NCDC PHC — Tetanus; Referral](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/PEP-Protocol.pdf)

### Note
- Indian polyvalent ASV covers only the "big four" (cobra, krait, Russell's, saw-scaled viper) — not hump-nosed, Malabar, or green pit vipers, or sea snakes ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 28, 30](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.7.8, p. 140](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).
- First aid differs: WHO advises pressure-pad immobilisation unless elapid excluded; MoHFW restricts crepe bandage to trained staff (transfer 30 min–3 h); NAPSE advises splinting ([WHO SEARO — §6.2.3](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547); [MoHFW Snakebite — p. 9](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [NAPSE 2024 — pp. 73–74](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/NATIONAL-ACTION-PLAN-FOR-PREVENTION-AND-CONTROL-OF-SNAKEBITE-ENVENOMING-NAPSE.pdf)).
- Neostigmine, sedation, and AKI differ: MoHFW uses IV neostigmine (none in krait), avoids sedation, and uses forced alkaline diuresis; WHO uses IM neostigmine, sedates ventilated patients, and uses furosemide stress ([MoHFW Snakebite — pp. 36, 38, 45](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/Standard-Treatment-Guidelines-for-Management-of-Snake-Bite.pdf); [WHO SEARO — §6.9.2–6.9.5](https://iris.who.int/handle/10665/249547)).

## Protocol: scorpion-sting
- **Title:** Scorpion sting
- **Summary:** Grading Indian red scorpion envenoming, oral prazosin, scorpion antivenom, pulmonary oedema/myocarditis ICU care, and drugs to avoid.
- **Category:** Poisoning
- **Keywords:** `scorpion`, `scorpion sting`, `Mesobuthus tamulus`, `Hottentotta tamulus`, `red scorpion`, `bichhu`, `thuhan`, `prazosin`, `Minipress`, `Prazopress`, `SAV`, `scorpion antivenom`, `AScVS`, `autonomic storm`, `priapism`

### Step: Recognise and grade
- **Timing:** Grade on arrival; delay >4–6 h carries higher mortality
- **Points:**
  - Grade 1: severe local pain radiating along dermatomes, mild oedema, no systemic signs; observe 24 h ([Bawaskar 2011 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9)).
  - Grade 2 (autonomic storm): vomiting, profuse sweating, ropy salivation, priapism, HR >120 or bradycardia, BP >140/90 or hypotension, cold extremities ([Bawaskar 2011 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136)).
  - Grade 3: pulmonary oedema (RR >24, crackles), myocardial dysfunction (inotrope ≥5 µg/kg/min), cold shock; Grade 4: warm shock/MODS ([Bawaskar 2011 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [JIPMER 2018 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7)).
  - For Grade 1 pain, use local anaesthetic (digital block for digits) or topical 5% lidocaine plus paracetamol ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9); [Kumar 2022 — Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_2300_21); [Rodrigo 2017 — Pain relief](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-017-0469-8)).
- **Checklist:** Ominous Early Signs
  - Vomiting
  - Profuse sweating
  - Cold extremities
  - Priapism in males
- **Citations:** [Bawaskar 2011 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136), [JIPMER 2015 — Methods; Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9), [JIPMER 2018 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7), [Kumar 2022 — Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_2300_21), [Rodrigo 2017 — Pain relief](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-017-0469-8)

### Step: Prazosin (grade 2 and above)
- **Timing:** Start at first autonomic sign; repeat every 3 h
- **Points:**
  - Start oral prazosin immediately: child 30 µg/kg (JIPMER) or 250 µg (<18 y)/500 µg (adult) q3h until extremities warm ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9); [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136)).
  - Keep patient lying flat to prevent first-dose postural syncope (peaks 30–90 min; worsened by beta-blockers) ([FDA Prazosin — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=08114092-fa12-4f58-b08e-03cadf9f726b)).
  - Re-dose promptly if vomited; recovery on oral prazosin takes 12–48 h with ICU monitoring ([Bawaskar 2011 — Results](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [Bawaskar 2015 — p. 289](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Prazosin — child (JIPMER protocol) | 30 mcg/kg per dose PO every 3 h | max 1000 mcg | Continue until extremities are warm; lay flat (first-dose syncope); adults 500 mcg q3h
- **Citations:** [JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9), [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments; Results](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136), [Bawaskar 2015 — p. 289](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf), [FDA Prazosin — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=08114092-fa12-4f58-b08e-03cadf9f726b)

### Step: Scorpion antivenom (SAV)
- **Timing:** Most effective within 4–6 h of sting
- **Points:**
  - Give Haffkine monovalent SAV 30 mL in 100 mL saline IV over 30–60 min regardless of age alongside prazosin ([Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [JIPMER 2018 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7)).
  - SAV plus prazosin hastens recovery (8 vs 17.7 h) in early Grade 2, but does not reverse established severe envenoming ([Bawaskar 2011 — Results](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [Bawaskar 2015 — pp. 289–290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf)).
  - Repeat 30 mL SAV if autonomic storm persists/worsens ≤6 h; stop SAV and give adrenaline for allergic reactions (8%) ([JIPMER 2018 — Methods; Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7)).
- **Citations:** [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments; Results](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136), [JIPMER 2018 — Methods; Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7), [Bawaskar 2015 — pp. 289–290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf)

### Step: Pulmonary oedema, myocardial dysfunction and shock
- **Timing:** Grades 3–4: ICU support for 24–96 h
- **Points:**
  - Myocardial dysfunction or warm shock: oxygen, non-invasive/mechanical ventilation, and IV dobutamine 6–20 µg/kg/min ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9); [Bawaskar 2015 — pp. 289–290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf)).
  - Pulmonary oedema: oxygen, ventilation, and IV nitroglycerine 2–6 µg/kg/min (light-protected) or sodium nitroprusside 0.3–5 µg/kg/min ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9)).
  - Correct dehydration cautiously with IV crystalloid (avoid fluid overload); NEVER give diuretics ([Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [JIPMER 2018 — Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7); [Bawaskar 2015 — p. 290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Dobutamine — scorpion myocardial dysfunction (child) | 6–20 mcg/kg per minute IV infusion | max — | Give with oxygen in ICU
- **Dose:** Nitroglycerine — scorpion pulmonary oedema (child) | 2–6 mcg/kg per minute IV infusion | max — | Freshly prepared, protected from sunlight
- **Dose:** Sodium nitroprusside — scorpion pulmonary oedema (child) | 0.3–5 mcg/kg per minute IV infusion | max — | Alternative to nitroglycerine
- **Citations:** [JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9), [Bawaskar 2015 — pp. 289–290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf), [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136), [JIPMER 2018 — Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7)

### Step: Drugs to avoid, monitoring and discharge
- **Timing:** Vitals at 0, 30 min, 1, 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, 14, 18, 24 h
- **Points:**
  - Record BP, HR, RR, SpO2 on schedule; obtain ECG at 0 and 6 h, echo and CPK-MB within 24 h ([Bawaskar 2011 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9)).
  - Give first prazosin dose at peripheral centres before referring promptly to an ICU with oxygen and SAV ([Kumar 2022 — Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_2300_21)).
  - Observe ≥24 h after SAV (or ≥24 h stable off drugs after complications); give tetanus immunisation before discharge ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9); [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136)).
- **Checklist:** Drugs to Avoid in Scorpion Envenoming
  - Corticosteroids (worsen catecholamine myocardial injury; no benefit)
  - Antihistamines as envenoming treatment (QT prolongation; sudden death)
  - Calcium channel blockers (e.g. nifedipine — depress myocardium)
  - Diuretics (worsen pulmonary oedema and shock)
  - Atropine, beta-blockers, and lytic cocktail (pethidine + promethazine + chlorpromazine)
- **Citations:** [Bawaskar 2011 — Introduction; Methods; Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136), [Bawaskar 2015 — pp. 289–290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf), [JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9), [Kumar 2022 — Management](https://doi.org/10.4103/jfmpc.jfmpc_2300_21)

### Note
- Prazosin dosing differs: JIPMER uses 30 µg/kg (capped at 1 mg) in children; Bawaskar uses fixed 250 µg (<18 y) or 500 µg (adults); off-label for scorpion sting ([JIPMER 2015 — Methods](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-015-0627-9); [Bawaskar 2011 — Study treatments](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj.c7136); [FDA Prazosin — Indications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=08114092-fa12-4f58-b08e-03cadf9f726b)).
- Antihistamines conflict: Bawaskar warns against them (QT prolongation), while JIPMER 2018 premedicated SAV with IV chlorphenamine and ranitidine ([Bawaskar 2015 — p. 290](https://indianpediatrics.net/apr2015/289.pdf); [JIPMER 2018 — Results](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13312-018-1275-7)).
