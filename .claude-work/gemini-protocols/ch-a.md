# Batch CH-A

## Protocol: paediatric-arrest-seriously-ill-child
- **Title:** Paediatric cardiac arrest and seriously ill child
- **Summary:** Rapid ABCDE triage, weight estimation, 2025 paediatric CPR, defibrillation energies, arrest drugs, and reversible causes.
- **Category:** Cardiac
- **Keywords:** `paediatric arrest`, `PALS`, `PBLS`, `ETAT`, `ABCDE`, `defibrillation`, `adrenaline`, `amiodarone`, `Broselow`, `4Hs 4Ts`

### Step: Rapid Triage & ABCDE Assessment
- **Timing:** Immediate on arrival
- **Points:**
  - Triage immediately for emergency signs: obstructed/absent breathing, central cyanosis, shock (CRT >3 s, weak fast pulse), coma, or convulsions ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1, §1.1–1.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)).
  - Use quick-look assessment (Behaviour, Breathing, Body colour) followed by structured ABCDE; check palms/mucosa in darker skin tones ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Tables 1–2](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
  - Give 100% oxygen initially in failure or arrest; titrate post-stabilisation to SpO2 94–98% (AHA: 94–99% post-ROSC) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Recognition](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PALS — §11.3](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
  - Estimate weight using parent report first, then length-based habitus-adjusted tape (Broselow/PAWPER XL) over age formulas ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Weight](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.3](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
- **Scores:** `gcs`
- **Citations:** [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1, §1.1–1.3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/), [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Recognition, Weight](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines), [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.3, §11.3](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)

### Step: Paediatric CPR & Ventilation
- **Timing:** Start immediately if pulseless or HR <60/min with poor perfusion
- **Points:**
  - Start CPR if unresponsive and not breathing normally, or if HR <60/min with poor perfusion despite ventilation ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PBLS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PBLS — §7.1](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support)).
  - ERC: give 5 initial rescue breaths first; AHA: start with chest compressions (C-A-B) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PBLS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PBLS — §7.1](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support)).
  - Use 15:2 compression-to-ventilation ratio (2 healthcare providers), rate 100–120/min, depth ≥1/3 AP chest diameter (max 6 cm) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PBLS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PBLS — §7.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support)).
  - For infants, use two-thumb encircling hands (or heel of 1 hand); 2-finger technique is eliminated in 2025 ([AHA 2025 PBLS — §7.3](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support); [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PBLS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
  - With advanced airway, give continuous compressions plus 20–30 breaths/min (ERC: 25 infant, 20 >1y, 15 >8y, 10 >12y) ([AHA 2025 PALS — §6](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support); [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Ventilation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
- **Citations:** [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PBLS, Ventilation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines), [AHA 2025 PBLS — §7.1–7.3](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-basic-life-support), [AHA 2025 PALS — §6](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)

### Step: Defibrillation (VF / pVT)
- **Timing:** Shock immediately on shockable rhythm; resume CPR for 2 min
- **Points:**
  - ERC: deliver fixed 4 J/kg shocks (shocks 1–5), escalating up to 8 J/kg (max 360 J) if refractory ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Defibrillation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
  - AHA: deliver 2 J/kg first shock, 4 J/kg second shock, then ≥4 J/kg up to 10 J/kg (max adult dose) ([AHA 2025 PALS — §8](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
  - Use anteroposterior pad placement and paediatric AED attenuator for children <25 kg (~8 years) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Defibrillation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
- **Citations:** [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Defibrillation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines), [AHA 2025 PALS — §8](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)

### Step: Arrest Drugs & Hypoglycaemia
- **Points:**
  - Give adrenaline 10 mcg/kg (0.1 mL/kg of 1:10,000) IV/IO every 3–5 min (non-shockable: ASAP; shockable: after 2nd/3rd shock) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PALS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
  - For shock-refractory VF/pVT, give amiodarone 5 mg/kg IV/IO (max 300 mg; repeat max 150 mg) or lidocaine 1 mg/kg ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PALS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
  - Do not give routine sodium bicarbonate or calcium unless specific toxin or electrolyte indication exists ([AHA 2025 PALS — §7.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
  - Check blood glucose; treat hypoglycaemia with 10% dextrose 2 mL/kg IV/IO (ERC) or 5 mL/kg (WHO children) ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Disability](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1 Table 3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)).
- **Dose:** Adrenaline (1:10,000) — paediatric cardiac arrest | 10 mcg/kg per dose IV/IO every 3–5 min | max 1 mg | 0.1 mL/kg of 1:10,000 (0.1 mg/mL)
- **Dose:** Amiodarone — shock-refractory VF/pVT | 5 mg/kg per dose IV/IO bolus | max 300 mg | Repeat 5 mg/kg (max 150 mg) after 5th shock if refractory
- **Dose:** 10% Dextrose — paediatric hypoglycaemia | 2–5 mL/kg per dose IV/IO | max 250 mL | ERC: 2 mL/kg (0.2 g/kg); WHO children: 5 mL/kg (0.5 g/kg); recheck in 5–10 min
- **Citations:** [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PALS, Disability](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines), [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support), [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1 Table 3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)

### Step: Reversible Causes & Non-Arrest Shock
- **Points:**
  - Actively search for and treat reversible causes (4 Hs and 4 Ts) during resuscitation ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Table 3](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines)).
  - In non-arrest shock, give 10 mL/kg balanced crystalloid bolus (WHO: 10–20 mL/kg over 30–60 min) and reassess for overload ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Shock](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [WHO ETAT 2016 — pp. 11–12](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download)).
- **Checklist:** Reversible Causes (4 Hs & 4 Ts)
  - Hypoxia
  - Hypovolaemia
  - Hyper-/hypokalaemia and metabolic (hypoglycaemia, hypocalcaemia, acidosis)
  - Hypo-/hyperthermia
  - Tension pneumothorax, Tamponade (cardiac), Toxins, Thromboembolism
- **Citations:** [RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — Table 3, Shock](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines), [WHO ETAT 2016 — pp. 11–12](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/da37621b-f9ac-4392-9631-718b3d6b253d/download)

### Note
- ERC 2025 starts with 5 rescue breaths, 4 J/kg fixed shocks, and adrenaline after shock 3; AHA 2025 uses C-A-B, 2→4 J/kg shocks, and adrenaline after shock 2 ([RCUK/ERC 2025 PLS — PALS](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/paediatric-basic-life-support-guidelines); [AHA 2025 PALS — §7.2, §8](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/pediatric-advanced-life-support)).
- Avoid rapid standard fluid boluses in severe acute malnutrition; use modified fluids only for true shock ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1, §1.4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)).

## Protocol: neonatal-resuscitation
- **Title:** Neonatal resuscitation
- **Summary:** Golden Minute newborn stabilisation, positive-pressure ventilation, MR SOPA, 3:1 compressions, and neonatal adrenaline per 2025 NRP/NSSK guidelines.
- **Category:** Respiratory
- **Keywords:** `NRP`, `NSSK`, `Golden Minute`, `PPV`, `MR SOPA`, `newborn resuscitation`, `meconium`, `UVC`, `adrenaline`

### Step: Golden Minute, Cord Management & Initial Steps
- **Timing:** Complete initial steps and start PPV within 60 seconds
- **Points:**
  - Assess at birth: term, good tone, breathing/crying; if vigorous, defer cord clamping ≥60 seconds with skin-to-skin care ([AHA 2025 NLS — §8.1–8.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Cord](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth)).
  - Never perform umbilical cord milking in preterm infants <28 weeks (increased intraventricular haemorrhage risk) ([AHA 2025 NLS — §8.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Cord](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth)).
  - Maintain temperature 36.5–37.5 °C: dry ≥32 weeks under radiant warmer; place <32 weeks directly into plastic wrap without drying ([AHA 2025 NLS — §9.1](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [MoHFW NRP 2023 — Lesson 1](https://nhm.hp.gov.in/storage/app/media/uploaded-files/Neonatal%20Resuscitation%20printready_2023_compressed.pdf)).
  - Position head in neutral sniffing position and stimulate gently; do NOT routinely suction even in non-vigorous meconium-stained infants ([AHA 2025 NLS — §9.4](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Airway](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth)).
- **Citations:** [AHA 2025 NLS — §8.1–8.2, §9.1, §9.4](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation), [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Cord, Airway](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth), [MoHFW NRP 2023 — Lesson 1](https://nhm.hp.gov.in/storage/app/media/uploaded-files/Neonatal%20Resuscitation%20printready_2023_compressed.pdf)

### Step: Positive-Pressure Ventilation (PPV) & Oxygen
- **Timing:** Start PPV within 60 seconds of birth if apnoeic, gasping, or HR <100 bpm
- **Points:**
  - Start PPV at 30–60 breaths/min (PIP 20–30 cm H2O, PEEP 5 cm H2O) if apnoeic, gasping, or HR <100 bpm ([AHA 2025 NLS — §11.1–11.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).
  - Starting FiO2: 21% room air for ≥35 weeks (ERC: ≥32 wk); 21–30% for 32–34+6 weeks; 30–100% for <32 weeks ([AHA 2025 NLS — §12](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Oxygen](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth)).
  - If chest does not rise or HR fails to increase, perform MR SOPA corrective steps immediately ([AHA 2025 NLS — §11.1](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).
  - Place pre-ductal pulse oximeter on right hand/wrist and attach 3-lead ECG when resuscitation or alternative airway is needed ([AHA 2025 NLS — §12](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [CPS NRP FAQs — Monitoring](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs)).
- **Checklist:** MR SOPA Ventilation Corrective Steps
  - Mask adjustment (ensure tight seal; 2-person jaw thrust if needed)
  - Reposition head to neutral sniffing position
  - Suction mouth then nose ONLY if visible obstruction
  - Open mouth slightly
  - Pressure increase in 2–5 cm H2O steps (max ~40 cm H2O)
  - Alternative airway (laryngeal mask ≥34 wk / ≥2 kg, or endotracheal intubation)
- **Citations:** [AHA 2025 NLS — §11.1–11.2, §12](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation), [RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Oxygen](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth), [CPS NRP FAQs — Monitoring](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs)

### Step: Chest Compressions
- **Timing:** Start if HR <60 bpm despite 30 s of effective PPV
- **Points:**
  - Start chest compressions if HR remains <60 bpm after 30 seconds of effective PPV (preferably after securing airway) ([AHA 2025 NLS — Compressions](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).
  - Increase FiO2 to 100% as soon as chest compressions start ([AHA 2025 NLS — Compressions](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).
  - Use two-thumb encircling hands technique on lower third of sternum at 3:1 ratio (90 compressions + 30 breaths/min) ([AHA 2025 NLS — Compressions](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [AAFP NRP — Compressions](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)).
- **Citations:** [AHA 2025 NLS — Compressions](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation), [AAFP NRP — Compressions](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)

### Step: Medications, Volume & Stopping Resuscitation
- **Timing:** Give adrenaline if HR <60 bpm after 60 s of PPV + compressions
- **Points:**
  - Insert UVC (preferred) or IO; give adrenaline 0.01–0.03 mg/kg (suggested 0.02 mg/kg = 0.2 mL/kg) IV/IO q3–5 min ([AHA 2025 NLS — Epinephrine](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation); [CPS NRP FAQs — Epinephrine](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs)).
  - Follow each IV/IO adrenaline dose with a 3 mL normal saline flush; ETT dose (while getting IV) is 0.05–0.1 mg/kg ([CPS NRP FAQs — Epinephrine](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs); [AAFP NRP — Medications](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)).
  - If acute blood loss or shock is suspected, give 0.9% NaCl or O-negative RBCs 10 mL/kg IV/UVC over 5–10 min ([AAFP NRP — Volume](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)).
  - If heart rate remains undetectable after all appropriate resuscitation steps, discuss discontinuing efforts around 20 minutes after birth ([CPS NRP FAQs — Discontinuation](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs); [AAFP NRP — Discontinuation](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)).
- **Dose:** Adrenaline (1:10,000) — neonatal resuscitation (IV/UVC) | 0.01–0.03 mg/kg per dose IV/IO every 3–5 min | max — | Suggested 0.02 mg/kg (0.2 mL/kg) + 3 mL saline flush; ETT 0.05–0.1 mg/kg
- **Dose:** 0.9% Sodium chloride — neonatal volume expansion | 10 mL/kg per dose IV/UVC over 5–10 min | max — | Use O RhD-negative packed RBCs if substantial acute blood loss suspected
- **Citations:** [AHA 2025 NLS — Epinephrine](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation), [CPS NRP FAQs — Epinephrine, Discontinuation](https://cps.ca/en/nrp-prn/faqs), [AAFP NRP — Medications, Volume, Discontinuation](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2021/1000/p425)

### Note
- ERC 2025 starts with 5 inflation breaths (2–3 s each) then 30 breaths/min; AHA 2025 uses 30–60 breaths/min (0.5–1 s) without sustained inflations ([RCUK/ERC 2025 NLS — Lung Inflation](https://www.resus.org.uk/professional-library/2025-resuscitation-guidelines/newborn-resuscitation-and-support-transition-infants-birth); [AHA 2025 NLS — §11.1–11.2](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).
- AHA 2025 widened starting FiO2 for <32 weeks to 30–100% and upgraded delayed cord clamping to ≥60 seconds for vigorous term and preterm newborns ([AHA 2025 NLS — §8.1–8.2, §12](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/cpr-and-ecc-guidelines/neonatal-resuscitation)).

## Protocol: paediatric-sepsis
- **Title:** Paediatric sepsis and septic shock
- **Summary:** Early recognition, resource-stratified fluid boluses, 1-hour IV antibiotics, and vasoactive support for paediatric sepsis and septic shock.
- **Category:** Infection
- **Keywords:** `paediatric sepsis`, `septic shock`, `Phoenix score`, `FEAST`, `ceftriaxone`, `adrenaline`, `noradrenaline`, `lactate`

### Step: Recognition & Initial Workup
- **Timing:** Immediate assessment; cultures and lactate without delaying antibiotics
- **Points:**
  - Recognise shock early: prolonged CRT >2–3 s, mottled cold extremities, weak fast pulse, altered mentation, oliguria; hypotension is late ([IAP Shock STG — Recognition](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/shock-in-office-practice-in-children-iap-guidelines-109443); [NICE NG254 — Rec 1.7.10](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Managing-suspected-sepsis)).
  - Phoenix Sepsis Criteria: suspected infection plus ≥2 points (respiratory, cardiovascular, coagulation, neurological); septic shock adds ≥1 cardiovascular point ([Phoenix 2024 — Box 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10900966/)).
  - Obtain blood cultures and serum lactate urgently before antibiotics without delaying treatment; lactate >4 mmol/L requires immediate escalation ([SSC 2026 — Diagnostics](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [NICE NG254 — Rec 1.7.1, 1.7.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Managing-suspected-sepsis)).
  - Check bedside glucose immediately; treat hypoglycaemia (<45 mg/dL WHO; ≤60 mg/dL IAP) with 10% dextrose 2–5 mL/kg IV ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/); [IAP Shock STG — Glucose](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/shock-in-office-practice-in-children-iap-guidelines-109443)).
- **Scores:** `gcs`
- **Citations:** [IAP Shock STG — Recognition, Glucose](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/shock-in-office-practice-in-children-iap-guidelines-109443), [NICE NG254 — Rec 1.7.1, 1.7.5, 1.7.10](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Managing-suspected-sepsis), [Phoenix 2024 — Box 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10900966/), [SSC 2026 — Diagnostics](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines), [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/)

### Step: Empirical IV Antibiotics
- **Timing:** Within 1 hour for septic shock; within 3 hours for sepsis without shock
- **Points:**
  - Start IV broad-spectrum antibiotics within 1 hour for septic shock and within 3 hours for probable sepsis without shock ([SSC 2026 — Antimicrobials](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [NICE NG254 — Rec 1.7.2, 1.7.14](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Managing-suspected-sepsis)).
  - Community-acquired (>28 days): ceftriaxone 80–100 mg/kg/day IV (max 4 g); add ampicillin/amoxicillin if age 1–3 months ([NICE NG254 — Rec 1.8.7–1.8.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Antibiotic-therapy-intravenous-fluid-and-oxygen); [ICMR Paed STW — Sepsis](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf)).
  - Neonates (<28 days): ampicillin 50 mg/kg IV + gentamicin 5–7.5 mg/kg OD (or cefotaxime 50 mg/kg q8h) ([MoHFW FBNC 2023 — Sepsis](https://nhm.hp.gov.in/storage/app/media/FBNC%20Training%20module%20updated%2024%20June_2023_compressed.pdf); [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154448/)).
  - Hospital-acquired or septic shock (high Indian ESBL): piperacillin–tazobactam 100 mg/kg q8h + amikacin, or meropenem ± vancomycin ([ICMR AMR 2017 — Paediatrics](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_guidelines_2017.pdf); [IAP Febrile Neutropenia — Doses](https://www.scribd.com/document/728875501/28-Febrile-Neutropenia)).
- **Dose:** Ceftriaxone — community-acquired paediatric sepsis (>28 days) | 80–100 mg/kg per day IV | max 4 g | Avoid in neonates ≤40 wk CGA or with IV calcium (use cefotaxime)
- **Citations:** [SSC 2026 — Antimicrobials](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines), [NICE NG254 — Rec 1.7.2, 1.7.14, 1.8.7–1.8.8](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng254/chapter/Antibiotic-therapy-intravenous-fluid-and-oxygen), [ICMR Paed STW — Sepsis](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf), [MoHFW FBNC 2023 — Sepsis](https://nhm.hp.gov.in/storage/app/media/FBNC%20Training%20module%20updated%2024%20June_2023_compressed.pdf), [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154448/), [ICMR AMR 2017 — Paediatrics](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_guidelines_2017.pdf), [IAP Febrile Neutropenia — Doses](https://www.scribd.com/document/728875501/28-Febrile-Neutropenia)

### Step: Resource-Stratified Fluid Resuscitation
- **Timing:** First hour with reassessment after every bolus
- **Points:**
  - With ICU/ventilator access: give 10–20 mL/kg balanced crystalloid boluses up to 40–60 mL/kg in the first hour ([SSC 2026 — Fluid Therapy](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines)).
  - Without ICU/ventilator access: give NO fluid boluses if not hypotensive (maintenance only); if hypotensive, 10–20 mL/kg up to 40 mL/kg ([SSC 2026 — Fluid Therapy](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines)).
  - Reassess after every bolus and stop immediately if hepatomegaly, lung crepitations, gallop rhythm, or worsening tachypnoea develops ([SSC 2026 — Fluid Therapy](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154436/)).
  - Never give rapid 20 mL/kg boluses in SAM: use 15 mL/kg over 1 hour only for true shock ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)).
- **Dose:** Balanced crystalloid — paediatric septic shock bolus | 10–20 mL/kg per bolus IV/IO over 10–20 min | max 1000 mL | Stop immediately if hepatomegaly/rales; avoid boluses in SAM or non-hypotensive child without ICU
- **Checklist:** Fluid Overload Red Flags (Stop Boluses)
  - New or enlarging hepatomegaly (most reliable early sign in children)
  - Basal lung crepitations/rales or worsening tachypnoea/SpO2
  - New gallop rhythm or rising JVP
  - Severe acute malnutrition (use SAM protocol instead)
- **Citations:** [SSC 2026 — Fluid Therapy](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines), [WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 1, Ch. 6](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154450/)

### Step: Vasopressors, Inotropes & Refractory Shock
- **Points:**
  - Start adrenaline (epinephrine) or noradrenaline (norepinephrine) first-line over dopamine for fluid-refractory shock (0.05–0.1 mcg/kg/min, titrate to 1 mcg/kg/min) ([SSC 2020 — Vasoactives](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7095013/); [SSC 2026 — Vasoactives](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [Paed Vasoactives — Dosing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10169116/)).
  - Begin diluted vasoactive infusion via peripheral IV or IO rather than delaying for central venous access ([SSC 2026 — Vasoactives](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [IAP Shock STG — Vasoactives](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/shock-in-office-practice-in-children-iap-guidelines-109443)).
  - For catecholamine-refractory shock or suspected adrenal insufficiency, consider IV hydrocortisone 2 mg/kg bolus then 1 mg/kg q6h ([SSC 2026 — Steroids](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines); [SHIPSS 2026 — Protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41504575/)).
  - Avoid etomidate for intubation; target post-resuscitation SpO2 88–92% (SSC 2026) once stabilised ([SSC 2026 — Airway & Oxygen](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines)).
- **Citations:** [SSC 2020 — Vasoactives](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7095013/), [SSC 2026 — Vasoactives, Steroids, Airway](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines), [Paed Vasoactives — Dosing](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10169116/), [IAP Shock STG — Vasoactives](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/shock-in-office-practice-in-children-iap-guidelines-109443), [SHIPSS 2026 — Protocol](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41504575/)

### Note
- Phoenix Sepsis Criteria (2024) define established organ dysfunction and are NOT an early screening tool; treat clinical shock immediately ([Phoenix 2024 — Figure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10900966/)).
- In district hospitals without ICU/ventilators, giving rapid fluid boluses to non-hypotensive febrile children increases mortality (FEAST); use maintenance fluids only ([SSC 2026 — Fluid Therapy](https://survivingsepsis.org/guidelines-and-resources/surviving-sepsis-campaign-pediatric-guidelines)).

## Protocol: paediatric-status-epilepticus-febrile-seizures
- **Title:** Paediatric status epilepticus and febrile seizures
- **Summary:** Time-critical benzodiazepines, second-line anti-seizure loading doses, refractory seizure escalation, and febrile seizure red flags in children.
- **Category:** Neuro
- **Keywords:** `status epilepticus`, `febrile seizures`, `lorazepam`, `midazolam`, `levetiracetam`, `phenytoin`, `valproate`, `phenobarbitone`, `fits`

### Step: Stabilisation & Glucose Check (0–5 min)
- **Timing:** 0–5 minutes from seizure onset
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose convulsive status epilepticus if seizure lasts ≥5 min or ≥2 seizures occur without recovery between them ([ILAE 2015 — Definition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26336950/); [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)).
  - Protect airway in recovery position, give high-flow oxygen, monitor SpO2/ECG/BP, and establish IV/IO access ([IAP SE STG — Stabilisation](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224)).
  - Check bedside blood glucose immediately; if ≤2.6 mmol/L (<45–50 mg/dL), give 10% dextrose 5 mL/kg IV (neonates: 2 mL/kg) ([CPS SE — Glucose](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7850284/); [ICMR Neonatology STW — Hypoglycaemia](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1726568619_neonatology_all.pdf)).
- **Scores:** `gcs`
- **Citations:** [ILAE 2015 — Definition](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26336950/), [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures), [IAP SE STG — Stabilisation](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224), [CPS SE — Glucose](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7850284/), [ICMR Neonatology STW — Hypoglycaemia](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1726568619_neonatology_all.pdf)

### Step: First-Line Benzodiazepines (5–15 min)
- **Timing:** Start at 5 minutes; repeat once at 10 minutes if still seizing
- **Points:**
  - IV access: lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (max 4 mg), diazepam 0.2 mg/kg (max 10 mg), or midazolam 0.2 mg/kg ([AES 2016 — First-Line](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4749120/); [ICMR Paed STW — Status Epilepticus](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf)).
  - Without IV access: give IM/buccal/intranasal midazolam 0.2 mg/kg (max 10 mg) or rectal diazepam 0.5 mg/kg (max 10–20 mg) ([AES 2016 — First-Line](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4749120/); [IAP SE STG — First-Line](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224)).
  - Repeat at most ONE second benzodiazepine dose after 5–10 min if seizing persists (max 2 doses total including prehospital) ([NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.8–7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)).
- **Dose:** Lorazepam — 1st-line status epilepticus (IV) | 0.1 mg/kg per dose IV over 2–4 min | max 4 mg | Max 2 benzodiazepine doses total (including prehospital)
- **Dose:** Midazolam — 1st-line status epilepticus (IM/buccal/IN) | 0.2 mg/kg per dose IM/buccal/IN | max 10 mg | Use when IV access unavailable; alternative rectal diazepam 0.5 mg/kg
- **Citations:** [AES 2016 — First-Line](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4749120/), [ICMR Paed STW — Status Epilepticus](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf), [IAP SE STG — First-Line](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224), [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.8–7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)

### Step: Second-Line IV Anti-Seizure Medicines (15–30 min)
- **Timing:** Start immediately if seizure persists after 2 benzodiazepine doses
- **Points:**
  - IV levetiracetam (UK: 40 mg/kg max 2.5 g; AES: 60 mg/kg max 4.5 g) over 5–15 min; quickest, fewest adverse effects ([EcLiPSE — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477100/); [ESETT — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576920/); [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)).
  - IV phenytoin 20 mg/kg (max 1.5 g) in 0.9% NaCl ONLY over ≥20 min (max 1 mg/kg/min) with ECG/BP monitoring ([ICMR Paed STW — Status Epilepticus](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf); [EcLiPSE — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477100/)).
  - IV sodium valproate 20–40 mg/kg (max 3 g) over 10 min; avoid in liver/metabolic disease, <2 years, or childbearing potential ([Paed CSE Review — Table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6982635/); [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)).
  - IV phenobarbitone 20 mg/kg over 20 min is preferred in neonates and infants; monitor closely for respiratory depression ([ICMR Paed STW — Status Epilepticus](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Levetiracetam — 2nd-line established status epilepticus | 40–60 mg/kg per dose IV over 5–15 min | max 4500 mg | UK/EcLiPSE: 40 mg/kg (max 2.5 g); AES/ESETT: 60 mg/kg (max 4.5 g)
- **Dose:** Phenytoin — 2nd-line established status epilepticus | 20 mg/kg per dose IV over 20 min | max 1500 mg | Max rate 1 mg/kg/min (50 mg/min); dilute in 0.9% NaCl ONLY with ECG/BP monitoring
- **Dose:** Sodium valproate — 2nd-line established status epilepticus | 20–40 mg/kg per dose IV over 10 min | max 3000 mg | Avoid in liver/metabolic disease, <2 years, or childbearing potential
- **Dose:** Phenobarbitone — 2nd-line status epilepticus (infants/neonates) | 20 mg/kg per dose IV over 20 min | max 1000 mg | Monitor closely for respiratory depression and hypotension
- **Citations:** [EcLiPSE — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477100/), [ESETT — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576920/), [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures), [ICMR Paed STW — Status Epilepticus](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725952336_paediatrics_all.pdf), [Paed CSE Review — Table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6982635/)

### Step: Refractory Status Epilepticus (>30–40 min)
- **Timing:** Call PICU and anaesthetist early if 2nd-line fails
- **Points:**
  - Alert PICU/anaesthesia for rapid sequence intubation and continuous midazolam infusion (0.2 mg/kg bolus then 0.1 mg/kg/h) or anaesthetic agent ([NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.10](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures); [IAP SE STG — Refractory](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224)).
  - Treat underlying causes promptly (bacterial meningitis, encephalitis, hyponatraemia, hypocalcaemia, toxins; see Bacterial meningitis) ([IAP SE STG — Workup](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224)).
- **Citations:** [NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.10](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures), [IAP SE STG — Refractory, Workup](https://medicaldialogues.in/pediatrics-neonatology/guidelines/evaluation-and-management-of-status-epilepticus-in-childreniap-guidelines-101224)

### Step: Febrile Seizures (6 Months – 5 Years)
- **Points:**
  - Simple febrile seizure: generalised, <15 minutes, single episode in 24 hours, normal baseline; complex: focal, ≥15 min, or recurrent ([StatPearls Febrile — Classification](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448123/)).
  - Stop any seizure lasting ≥5 minutes with first-line benzodiazepine; antipyretics improve comfort only and do NOT prevent recurrence ([WHO Febrile Seizures — Fever Control](https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/operational-guidance/COK-MN-62-OPERATIONALGUIDANCE-2018-eng-Febrile-Seizures.pdf)).
  - Well-appearing children with simple febrile seizures do not need routine EEG, neuroimaging, or chronic anti-seizure drugs ([AAFP Febrile — Evaluation](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2012/0115/p149)).
- **Checklist:** Red Flags for Meningitis / Lumbar Puncture / Admission
  - Meningeal signs, bulging fontanelle, petechial rash, or ill/toxic appearance
  - Age 6–12 months with incomplete/unknown Hib or pneumococcal vaccination
  - Prior antibiotic treatment masking meningitis
  - Prolonged post-ictal drowsiness (GCS ≤13) or complex febrile seizure features
- **Citations:** [StatPearls Febrile — Classification](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK448123/), [WHO Febrile Seizures — Fever Control, LP](https://platform.who.int/docs/default-source/mca-documents/policy-documents/operational-guidance/COK-MN-62-OPERATIONALGUIDANCE-2018-eng-Febrile-Seizures.pdf), [AAFP Febrile — Evaluation](https://www.aafp.org/afp/2012/0115/p149)

### Note
- Levetiracetam, phenytoin/fosphenytoin, and valproate have equivalent second-line efficacy (~50%); levetiracetam dosing differs between UK (40 mg/kg, max 2.5 g) and US/ESETT (60 mg/kg, max 4.5 g) ([ESETT — Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7576920/); [EcLiPSE — Protocol](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5477100/)).
- NICE NG217 (updated 2025) applies strict sodium valproate safety precautions to IV valproate in status epilepticus ([NICE NG217 — Rec 7.1.9](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng217/chapter/7-Treating-status-epilepticus-repeated-or-cluster-seizures-and-prolonged-seizures)).

## Protocol: paediatric-dka
- **Title:** Paediatric diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA)
- **Summary:** ISPAD 2022 fluid deficit calculation, delayed insulin infusion, potassium replacement, and immediate cerebral oedema rescue in children.
- **Category:** Metabolic
- **Keywords:** `paediatric DKA`, `ISPAD`, `ketoacidosis`, `cerebral oedema`, `hypertonic saline`, `mannitol`, `insulin`, `T1DM`

### Step: Diagnosis & Severity Grading
- **Timing:** Immediate VBG, blood glucose, ketones, and electrolytes
- **Points:**
  - Diagnose DKA: glucose >11 mmol/L (>200 mg/dL), pH <7.3 or HCO3 <18 mmol/L, and BOHB ≥3 mmol/L (ketonuria ≥2+) ([ISPAD 2022 — §1–2, p. 835](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [ISPAE 2024 — p. 12](https://ispae.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISPAE-T1D-MODULE-3.pdf)).
  - Severity: Mild (pH <7.3 / HCO3 <18), Moderate (pH <7.2 / HCO3 <10), Severe (pH <7.1 / HCO3 <5) ([ISPAD 2022 — §4, p. 838](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
  - Euglycaemic DKA can occur with fasting or partial treatment; never exclude DKA on normal glucose alone ([ISPAD 2022 — §1, p. 835](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- **Scores:** `gcs`
- **Citations:** [ISPAD 2022 — §1–2, §4, pp. 835, 838](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf), [ISPAE 2024 — p. 12](https://ispae.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISPAE-T1D-MODULE-3.pdf)

### Step: Fluid Resuscitation & Deficit Replacement
- **Timing:** Start fluids immediately (hour 0) BEFORE insulin
- **Points:**
  - Non-shock bolus: 10–20 mL/kg 0.9% NaCl over 20–30 min; shock: 20 mL/kg rapid (NICE: 10 mL/kg) ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3, p. 841](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.23–1.4.24](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Deficit: mild 5%, moderate 7%, severe 10% (NICE: 5%/10%); replace deficit plus maintenance evenly over 24–48 h ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3, pp. 841–842](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.25–1.4.26](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Subtract initial non-shock bolus from deficit (do NOT subtract shock boluses per NICE); use 0.45%–0.9% NaCl or balanced crystalloid ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.3, p. 842](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.26](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Add 5% dextrose when glucose falls to 14–17 mmol/L (250–300 mg/dL) or falls >5 mmol/L/h; escalate to 10–12.5% if needed ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.4, p. 844](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [ISPAD 2022 — §6.3–6.4, pp. 841–844](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf), [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.23–1.4.26, 1.4.39](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Delayed IV Insulin Infusion
- **Timing:** Start ONLY AFTER 1–2 hours of IV fluids — NEVER at hour 0
- **Points:**
  - Start continuous IV regular insulin at 0.05–0.1 units/kg/h ONLY after 1–2 hours of IV fluids; NEVER give an IV bolus ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.4, p. 844](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [ICMR T1DM 2022 — Ch. 5, p. 49](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf)).
  - Use 0.05 units/kg/h in children <5 years, mild–moderate DKA (pH >7.15), or severe hypokalaemia ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.4, p. 844](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [BSPED 2021 — §D.3, p. 11](https://www.bsped.org.uk/media/v2ydcuv0/bsped-dka-guideline-v3.pdf)).
  - Never stop insulin when glucose normalises — add dextrose and keep insulin ≥0.05 units/kg/h until acidosis resolves ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.4, p. 844](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.40](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - If infusion pumps unavailable in mild–moderate DKA: SC rapid analog 0.15 units/kg q2h (or regular 0.13–0.17 units/kg q4h) ([ISPAD 2022 — pp. 844–845](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- **Dose:** Regular human insulin — paediatric DKA infusion (after 1–2 h fluids) | 0.05–0.1 units/kg per hour IV infusion | max — | NO IV bolus; start after 1–2 h fluids; use 0.05 units/kg/h if <5 years or pH >7.15
- **Citations:** [ISPAD 2022 — §6.4, pp. 844–845](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf), [ICMR T1DM 2022 — Ch. 5, p. 49](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf), [BSPED 2021 — §D.3, p. 11](https://www.bsped.org.uk/media/v2ydcuv0/bsped-dka-guideline-v3.pdf), [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.36–1.4.40](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Potassium & Sodium Bicarbonate
- **Points:**
  - Add KCl 40 mmol/L with insulin once urine output confirmed and K+ normal/low (max 0.5 mmol/kg/h) ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.4, p. 843](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.29](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - If hypokalaemic (<3.0–3.5 mmol/L) at presentation, start K+ immediately with fluids and defer insulin until K+ recovers ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.4, p. 843](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.31](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Defer K+ if >5.5 mmol/L until urine output confirmed and K+ falls; use bedside ECG if lab delayed ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.4, p. 843](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
  - Never give routine sodium bicarbonate (cerebral oedema risk); restrict to life-threatening hyperkalaemia or pH <6.9 with cardiac compromise ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.5, p. 845](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.4, §6.5, pp. 843, 845](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf), [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.29, 1.4.31, 1.4.34](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Cerebral Oedema Rescue
- **Timing:** Treat IMMEDIATELY on clinical suspicion — do NOT delay for CT scan
- **Points:**
  - Elevate head of bed 30°, reduce IV fluid rate by 1/3 (BSPED: ½ maintenance), and give hyperosmolar therapy immediately ([ISPAE 2024 — p. 22](https://ispae.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISPAE-T1D-MODULE-3.pdf); [BSPED 2021 — §H, p. 16](https://www.bsped.org.uk/media/v2ydcuv0/bsped-dka-guideline-v3.pdf); [ISPAD 2022 — §8.2.1, p. 847](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
  - Give 3% hypertonic saline 2.5–5 mL/kg IV over 10–15 min OR 20% mannitol 0.5–1 g/kg IV over 10–15 min ([ISPAD 2022 — §8.2.1, p. 847](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.57](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Repeat mannitol in 30 min (or give 3% saline in 15–30 min) if no response; avoid intubation unless respiratory failure ([ISPAD 2022 — §8.2.1, p. 847](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- **Dose:** 3% Hypertonic saline — DKA cerebral oedema | 2.5–5 mL/kg per dose IV over 10–15 min | max — | Give immediately on clinical signs; do NOT wait for CT scan
- **Dose:** 20% Mannitol — DKA cerebral oedema (alternative) | 0.5–1 g/kg per dose IV over 10–15 min | max — | Repeat in 30 min if no response, or give 3% hypertonic saline
- **Checklist:** Cerebral Oedema Warning Signs (Pre-Calculate Dose if <5 y or pH <7.1)
  - New or worsening headache, vomiting, irritability, lethargy, or fluctuating GCS
  - Sustained heart rate drop >20 bpm not explained by sleep or rehydration
  - Cranial nerve palsy (III, IV, VI), abnormal posture, or neurogenic breathing pattern
  - Age-inappropriate incontinence or rising diastolic BP >90 mmHg
- **Citations:** [ISPAD 2022 — §8.2, §8.2.1, p. 847](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf), [ISPAE 2024 — p. 22](https://ispae.org.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/ISPAE-T1D-MODULE-3.pdf), [BSPED 2021 — §H, p. 16](https://www.bsped.org.uk/media/v2ydcuv0/bsped-dka-guideline-v3.pdf), [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.57](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)

### Note
- Do NOT use the adult-oriented ICMR DKA STW in children — it instructs an IV insulin bolus and routine bicarbonate, which increase paediatric cerebral oedema risk ([ICMR T1DM 2022 — Ch. 5, p. 49](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/ICMR_Guidelines_for_Management_of_Type_1_Diabetes.pdf); [ISPAD 2022 — §6.4–6.5](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
- Fluid shock bolus and subtraction differ: ISPAD 2022 uses 20 mL/kg shock bolus and subtracts initial bolus; NICE NG18 uses 10 mL/kg shock bolus and does NOT subtract shock boluses ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf); [NICE NG18 — Rec 1.4.24, 1.4.26](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng18/chapter/recommendations)).
- Do not routinely replace urinary losses; transition to SC insulin once pH ≥7.3 and BOHB <1.0 mmol/L with 15–30 min (rapid) or 1–2 h (regular) overlap ([ISPAD 2022 — §6.3.3, §6.6](https://endodiab.barnlakarforeningen.se/wp-content/uploads/sites/9/2024/10/Ch.11._Pediatric_Diabetes_-_.pdf)).
