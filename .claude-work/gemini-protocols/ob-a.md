# Batch OB-A

## Protocol: antepartum-haemorrhage
- **Title:** Antepartum haemorrhage
- **Summary:** Emergency assessment, resuscitation, and definitive management of vaginal bleeding in late pregnancy, covering placenta praevia, placental abruption, vasa praevia, uterine rupture, anti-D prophylaxis, and preterm corticosteroids.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `APH`, `bleeding PV`, `placenta praevia`, `placental abruption`, `vasa praevia`, `uterine rupture`, `DIC`, `anti-D`, `Kleihauer`, `PAS`

### Step: Triage, Golden Rule & Initial Assessment
- **Timing:** Immediate on arrival
- **Points:**
  - Major causes include placenta praevia, placental abruption, vasa praevia, uterine rupture, and lower genital tract lesions ([RCOG GTG 63 — §2, p. 2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 1, p. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - RCOG classifies APH as spotting, minor (<50 mL settled), major (50–1000 mL, no shock), or massive (>1000 mL and/or shock) ([RCOG GTG 63 — §2, p. 3](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Do not perform a digital vaginal examination until ultrasound excludes placenta praevia; avoid rectal exams in known praevia ([RCOG GTG 63 — §4.4.2, p. 5; §7.3, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [WHO MCPC — pp. S-21–S-24](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf)).
  - Perform a gentle speculum examination only to visualise cervical dilatation or local genital tract causes ([RCOG GTG 63 — §7.2, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - At PHCs or non-CEmOC facilities, start IV crystalloid, monitor vitals, and refer urgently to a CEmOC centre with blood bank ([MoHFW STG OBG — p. 16](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/4571.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Initial Assessment Rules
  - Never perform digital vaginal examination before ultrasound excludes placenta praevia
  - Stabilise maternal ABCs before assessing the fetus in major or massive haemorrhage
  - Exclude earlier-gestation bleeding (<20–24 weeks; see Miscarriage and septic abortion)
  - Assess for abdominal trauma if history suggests injury (see Trauma in pregnancy)
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 63 — §2, §4.4.2, §7.2–7.3](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [WHO MCPC — pp. S-21–S-24](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf), [MoHFW STG OBG — p. 16](https://clinicalestablishments.mohfw.gov.in/sites/default/files/standard-treatment-guidelines/4571.pdf), [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 1, p. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)

### Step: Maternal Resuscitation & Blood Products
- **Timing:** Within 10–15 min for major or massive APH
- **Points:**
  - Call the multidisciplinary team, give oxygen 10–15 L/min via mask, apply left lateral tilt, and keep the woman warm ([RCOG GTG 63 — Appendix 1–2, pp. 20–21](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Insert two 14G cannulae (one 14G if ≤1000 mL without shock), take 20 mL blood (FBC, coag, U&E, LFT), and cross-match 4 units ([RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1.1, p. 8; Appendix 1–2, pp. 20–21](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Infuse up to 3.5 L warmed fluids (up to 2 L crystalloid, up to 1–2 L colloid) rapidly until blood arrives ([RCOG GTG 63 — Appendix 2, p. 21](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Transfuse cross-matched RBCs, or uncross-matched group-specific / O RhD-negative blood urgently; target Hb >8 g/dL and platelets >75 × 10⁹/L (transfuse if <50 × 10⁹/L) ([RCOG GTG 63 — Appendix 2, p. 21](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [RCOG GTG 27a — §7.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)).
  - Tranexamic acid is not routine antepartum; give uterotonics plus tranexamic acid 1 g IV over 10 min during/after birth (see Postpartum haemorrhage) ([RCOG GTG 27a — §7.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [FIGO PPH — Rec 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9313855/)).
- **Dose:** Fresh frozen plasma (FFP) — coagulopathy in massive APH | 12–15 mL/kg per dose IV | max — | Give 4 units (~1 L) per 6 units RBCs or if PT/aPTT >1.5x control
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1.1, §16.1, Appendix 1–2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [RCOG GTG 27a — §7.3–7.4](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/), [FIGO PPH — Rec 1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9313855/)

### Step: Fetal Assessment & Diagnostic Imaging
- **Points:**
  - Start CTG once maternal resuscitation is underway where fetal status influences delivery; avoid continuous CTG below 26+0 weeks if no fetal intervention planned ([RCOG GTG 63 — §8.2, p. 9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - If the fetal heart is not heard on auscultation, perform ultrasound to confirm or exclude intrauterine fetal death (IUFD) ([RCOG GTG 63 — §8.2, p. 9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Transvaginal ultrasound (TVS) is safe even in symptomatic bleeding and is more accurate than transabdominal scan to diagnose or exclude placenta praevia ([RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1.2, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [RCOG GTG 27a — §5.1.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)).
  - Ultrasound has poor sensitivity (~24%) for retroplacental clot and never excludes placental abruption, which remains a clinical diagnosis ([RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1.2, pp. 8–9](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1.2, §8.2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [RCOG GTG 27a — §5.1.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)

### Step: Placental Abruption & DIC Management
- **Points:**
  - Suspect abruption with continuous abdominal pain, tense woody tender uterus, hypertonic contractions, shock disproportionate to revealed bleeding, or fetal distress/IUFD ([RCOG GTG 63 — §7.1, p. 7](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 3, p. 23](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - DIC occurs in 30–40% of abruptions with IUFD; monitor serial platelets, fibrinogen, PT/aPTT, or hourly bedside clot test where labs are unavailable ([RCOG GTG 63 — §16.1, p. 14](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 3, p. 24](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - With a live fetus and maternal or fetal compromise, deliver immediately (usually emergency caesarean section) alongside active resuscitation ([RCOG GTG 63 — §13.1, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - With IUFD and a stable mother, vaginal delivery is recommended over caesarean section; reserve CS for uncontrollable bleeding or obstetric indications ([RCOG GTG 63 — §13.1, p. 11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 5, p. 27](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - Perform active management of third stage and anticipate PPH (see Postpartum haemorrhage); Couvelaire uterus alone does not require hysterectomy ([RCOG GTG 63 — §13.4, p. 13](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 8, p. 35](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Abruption Red Flags & Contraindications
  - Do NOT give tocolytics in placental abruption, major APH, or fetal compromise
  - Bedside clot failure or lysis within 30 min indicates fibrinogen <150 mg/dL
  - Avoid antifibrinolytics in established DIC unless primary hyperfibrinolysis is confirmed
  - Give up to 4 units FFP and 2 pools cryoprecipitate empirically in relentless bleeding
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 63 — §7.1, §11, §13.1, §13.4, §16.1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 3, Ch. 5, Ch. 7c, Ch. 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)

### Step: Placenta Praevia & Accreta Spectrum
- **Points:**
  - Placenta praevia reaches or covers the internal os; low-lying placenta lies <20 mm from the os; repeat TVS at 32 and 36 weeks ([RCOG GTG 27a — §2.1, §5.1.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)).
  - Admit bleeding heavier than spotting until settled; asymptomatic praevia may be outpatient if rapid hospital access exists (FOGSI: admit major praevia with prior bleed at 34 weeks) ([RCOG GTG 63 — §9, p. 10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [RCOG GTG 27a — §5.2.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 5, p. 15](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - RCOG GTG 27a (2026) recommends planned birth for uncomplicated praevia by 37+6 weeks (audit 37+0–37+6); FOGSI advises elective CS after 37 weeks ([RCOG GTG 27a — §6.1, §15.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 5, p. 16](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
  - Offer trial of labour for asymptomatic low-lying placenta (11–20 mm after 36 weeks); screen prior CS with anterior low placenta for accreta spectrum (PAS) ([RCOG GTG 27a — §6.2, §9.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)).
  - For uncomplicated PAS, plan birth at 35+0–36+6 weeks in an MDT unit; never attempt manual placental separation, and clamp cord immediately if placenta is transected ([RCOG GTG 27a — §7.4, §10.2, §11.3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/)).
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 27a — §2.1, §5.1.1, §5.2.1, §6.1–6.2, §7.4, §9.2, §10.2, §11.3, §15.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/), [RCOG GTG 63 — §9, p. 10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 5, pp. 15–16](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)

### Step: Vasa Praevia & Uterine Rupture
- **Timing:** Immediate — Category 1 caesarean section or laparotomy
- **Points:**
  - Suspect vasa praevia with painless dark bleeding at membrane rupture plus sudden fetal bradycardia or sinusoidal CTG; fetal mortality is ≥60% once ruptured ([RCOG GTG 27b — §2, §4–5, pp. 3–5](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:abaf682d-60d5-4893-90eb-b2866ad42226/files/m649d12ef3a0adf93ec9e136f73fabef4)).
  - Do not delay delivery for Apt or Kleihauer tests; perform Category 1 CS, clamp cord immediately leaving a long stump, and alert neonatology for transfusion ([RCOG GTG 27b — §4, §7, pp. 4, 6](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:abaf682d-60d5-4893-90eb-b2866ad42226/files/m649d12ef3a0adf93ec9e136f73fabef4); [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.2, §17.1.1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - For antenatally diagnosed asymptomatic vasa praevia, offer antenatal corticosteroids from 32 weeks and planned caesarean section at 34–36 weeks ([RCOG GTG 27b — §7–8, pp. 6–8](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:abaf682d-60d5-4893-90eb-b2866ad42226/files/m649d12ef3a0adf93ec9e136f73fabef4)).
  - Suspect uterine rupture with abnormal CTG (66–76%), persistent inter-contraction pain, acute scar tenderness, haematuria, loss of station, or shock (see Maternal collapse) ([RCOG GTG 45 — §8.1, pp. 14–15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/kpkjwd5h/gtg_45.pdf)).
  - Suspected uterine rupture requires immediate resuscitation and expeditious emergency laparotomy ([RCOG GTG 45 — §8.1, pp. 14–15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/kpkjwd5h/gtg_45.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Uterine Rupture Red Flags
  - Sudden fetal bradycardia or abnormal CTG (most consistent sign, 66–76%)
  - Severe abdominal pain persisting between contractions
  - Acute-onset previous scar tenderness or haematuria
  - Loss of station of presenting part or changed abdominal contour
  - Cessation of previously efficient contractions or maternal shock
- **Citations:** [RCOG GTG 27b — §2, §4–5, §7–8](https://ora.ox.ac.uk/objects/uuid:abaf682d-60d5-4893-90eb-b2866ad42226/files/m649d12ef3a0adf93ec9e136f73fabef4), [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.2, §17.1.1](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [RCOG GTG 45 — §8.1, pp. 14–15](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/kpkjwd5h/gtg_45.pdf)

### Step: Anti-D Immunoglobulin & Antenatal Corticosteroids
- **Timing:** Anti-D within 72 hours of sensitising event
- **Points:**
  - Give anti-D to all non-sensitised RhD-negative women with APH ≥20 weeks as soon as possible, ideally within 72 hours (partial benefit up to 10 days) ([FOGSI Anti-D 2022 — pp. 6, 8](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/anti-d-immunoglobulin-2022.pdf); [BCSH Anti-D 2014 — Summary](https://transfusionontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/BCSH-Guideline-for-the-use-of-anti-D-immunoglobulin-for-the-prevention-of-HDFN_Trans-Med_2014.pdf)).
  - FOGSI recommends 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM into the deltoid; repeat if a recurrent sensitising event occurs ≥3 weeks apart ([FOGSI Anti-D 2022 — pp. 6–7, 9](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/anti-d-immunoglobulin-2022.pdf)).
  - BCSH recommends ≥500 IU IM plus Kleihauer/FMH test (extra anti-D if FMH >4 mL); repeat ≥500 IU at minimum 6-weekly intervals for continual bleeding ([BCSH Anti-D 2014 — §5, Summary](https://transfusionontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/BCSH-Guideline-for-the-use-of-anti-D-immunoglobulin-for-the-prevention-of-HDFN_Trans-Med_2014.pdf); [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1, p. 8](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
  - Offer antenatal corticosteroids if preterm APH risks birth: MoHFW/WHO give dexamethasone 6 mg IM q12h × 4 at 24–34 weeks; RCOG up to 34+6 weeks (see Preterm labour) ([MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0); [RCOG GTG 63 — §10, p. 10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf); [RCOG GTG 74 — Key Points](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/khqhzmcc/gtg74-antenatal-corticosteroids-key-points.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Anti-D & Pre-Discharge Checks
  - Kleihauer/FMH test quantifies anti-D need in RhD-negative women; never use it to diagnose abruption
  - Document anti-D product, batch, dose, deltoid site, and informed verbal consent
  - Check cord blood Rh group at delivery for postnatal anti-D within 72 hours
  - Advise immediate return for further bleeding, abdominal pain, or reduced fetal movements
- **Citations:** [FOGSI Anti-D 2022 — pp. 6–9](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/anti-d-immunoglobulin-2022.pdf), [BCSH Anti-D 2014 — Summary, §5](https://transfusionontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/BCSH-Guideline-for-the-use-of-anti-D-immunoglobulin-for-the-prevention-of-HDFN_Trans-Med_2014.pdf), [RCOG GTG 63 — §8.1, §10](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf), [MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0), [RCOG GTG 74 — Key Points](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/khqhzmcc/gtg74-antenatal-corticosteroids-key-points.pdf)

### Note
- Gestational cut-off for APH differs: FOGSI Focus uses >20 weeks (Ch. 1) or >22 weeks (Ch. 4), WHO MCPC uses >22 weeks, and RCOG GTG 63 uses ≥24+0 weeks ([FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 1, p. 8; Ch. 4, p. 25](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf); [WHO MCPC — p. S-21](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf); [RCOG GTG 63 — §1, p. 2](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).
- Anti-D dosing and repeat intervals differ: FOGSI (India) uses 300 mcg (1500 IU) IM and repeats if recurrent events are ≥3 weeks apart (reflecting limited FMH lab access), whereas BCSH (UK) uses ≥500 IU IM with mandatory FMH testing and repeats every 6 weeks for continual bleeding ([FOGSI Anti-D 2022 — pp. 6–7, 9](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/tog/anti-d-immunoglobulin-2022.pdf); [BCSH Anti-D 2014 — Summary, §5](https://transfusionontario.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/BCSH-Guideline-for-the-use-of-anti-D-immunoglobulin-for-the-prevention-of-HDFN_Trans-Med_2014.pdf)).
- Planned birth timing for uncomplicated placenta praevia shifted in RCOG GTG 27a (5th ed., Sept 2026) from 36+0–37+0 weeks (2018) to no later than 37+6 weeks (audit 37+0–37+6 weeks), aligning with FOGSI's 37 completed weeks ([RCOG GTG 27a — §6.1, §15.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [FOGSI Focus APH — Ch. 5, p. 16](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/09/antepartum_heamorrhage.pdf)).
- Short-course tocolysis (48 h) may be considered by a senior obstetrician in symptomatic placenta praevia to complete corticosteroids, but is contraindicated in placental abruption, major APH, or fetal compromise ([RCOG GTG 27a — §5.2.2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13485456/); [RCOG GTG 63 — §11, pp. 10–11](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/pwdi1tef/gtg_63.pdf)).

## Protocol: preterm-labour
- **Title:** Preterm labour and PPROM
- **Summary:** Diagnosis and acute management of suspected or established preterm labour and preterm prelabour rupture of membranes (<37+0 weeks), including antenatal corticosteroids, nifedipine tocolysis, magnesium sulfate neuroprotection, and PPROM antibiotics.
- **Category:** Obstetric
- **Keywords:** `PTL`, `PPROM`, `preterm`, `dexamethasone`, `betamethasone`, `nifedipine`, `tocolysis`, `MgSO4`, `magnesium sulfate`, `neuroprotection`, `erythromycin`, `GBS`

### Step: Diagnosis of Preterm Labour & PPROM
- **Points:**
  - Preterm labour and PPROM occur before 37+0 weeks; established preterm labour is regular contractions with progressive cervical dilatation from 4 cm ([NICE NG25 — Terms](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [FOGSI PTL 2025 — Definitions](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/TOG-Conclave-Pre-term-labour-Practice-Algorithms.pdf)).
  - Diagnose PPROM by sterile speculum exam showing amniotic fluid pooling in the posterior fornix; avoid digital vaginal exam unless active labour or imminent birth ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.3.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [RCOG GTG 73 — Diagnosis](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/care-of-women-presenting-with-suspected-preterm-prelabour-rupture-of-membranes-from-24plus0-weeks-of-gestation-green-top-guideline-no-73/)).
  - If no pooling is seen, NICE advises vaginal IGFBP-1 or PAMG-1 testing and explicitly advises against nitrazine testing ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.3.1, 1.3.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - For intact membranes at ≤29+6 weeks, treat clinically for preterm labour without waiting for transvaginal ultrasound (TVUS) or fetal fibronectin (fFN) ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.7.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - At ≥30+0 weeks, diagnose PTL if TVUS cervical length ≤15 mm (or fFN >50 ng/mL if TVUS unavailable); where neither exists, treat clinically ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.7.4–1.7.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Checklist:** Diagnostic Don'ts & Infection Surveillance
  - Avoid digital vaginal examination in PPROM unless in active labour or imminent birth
  - Do NOT use nitrazine testing to diagnose PPROM
  - Do NOT combine TVUS cervical length and fFN testing
  - Assess PPROM infection using clinical signs, CRP, WBC, and CTG together — never one test alone
- **Citations:** [NICE NG25 — Terms, Rec 1.3.1, 1.3.4, 1.5.1–1.5.2, 1.7.3–1.7.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations), [RCOG GTG 73 — Diagnosis](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/care-of-women-presenting-with-suspected-preterm-prelabour-rupture-of-membranes-from-24plus0-weeks-of-gestation-green-top-guideline-no-73/), [FOGSI PTL 2025 — Definitions](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/TOG-Conclave-Pre-term-labour-Practice-Algorithms.pdf)

### Step: Antenatal Corticosteroids — Indications & Conditions
- **Timing:** Start immediately once preterm birth is likely within 7 days
- **Points:**
  - MoHFW (India) and WHO (2022) recommend antenatal corticosteroids at 24+0 to 34+0 weeks for true PTL, PPROM, severe pre-eclampsia, or APH (see Antepartum haemorrhage, Severe pre-eclampsia) ([MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0); [WHO ACS 2022 — Table 1, Rec 1.0](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585353/table/fm-ch1.tab1/)).
  - NICE NG25 offers ACS at 24+0–33+6 weeks, considers at 34+0–35+6 weeks, and discusses at 22+0–23+6 weeks ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.9.1–1.9.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - ACOG recommends ACS at 24+0–33+6 weeks and a single late-preterm betamethasone course at 34+0–36+6 weeks if birth is likely within 7 days with no prior ACS ([ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor); [ACOG CO 713 — Summary](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000002237~committee-opinion-no-713-antenatal-corticosteroid-therapy)).
  - Start ACS even if full course completion before birth is uncertain, but do not administer "just in case" ([WHO ACS 2022 — Rec 1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/)).
- **Checklist:** WHO 2022 Five Essential Conditions for Safe ACS
  - Gestational age assessment can be accurately undertaken
  - High likelihood of preterm birth within 7 days of starting therapy
  - No clinical evidence of maternal infection (frank chorioamnionitis is a contraindication)
  - Adequate childbirth care is available to safely manage preterm birth
  - Adequate preterm newborn care is available (resuscitation, KMC, thermal/feeding support, CPAP)
- **Citations:** [MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0), [WHO ACS 2022 — Table 1, Rec 1.0](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585353/table/fm-ch1.tab1/), [WHO ACS 2022 — Rec 1.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/), [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.9.1–1.9.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations), [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor), [ACOG CO 713 — Summary](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000002237~committee-opinion-no-713-antenatal-corticosteroid-therapy)

### Step: Antenatal Corticosteroids — Regimen & Repeat Course
- **Points:**
  - MoHFW (India) preferred regimen is Injection Dexamethasone 6 mg IM every 12 hours × 4 doses (total 24 mg over 48 hours) ([MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0); [MoHFW PMSMA — Preterm Labour](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/High-Risk-Conditions-in-preg-modified-Final.pdf)).
  - WHO (2022) states IM dexamethasone (6 mg q12h × 4) and IM betamethasone (12 mg q24h × 2; total 24 mg) are equally effective ([WHO ACS 2022 — Rec 1.9](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/)).
  - At sub-centres/PHCs, give 1 pre-referral dexamethasone dose and refer to a CEmOC/SNCU facility (complete all 4 doses if referral is delayed or refused) ([MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0)).
  - Maternal diabetes, hypertension, and pre-eclampsia are not contraindications; monitor blood glucose closely for 72 hours and adjust insulin ([MoHFW PMSMA — Preterm Labour](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/High-Risk-Conditions-in-preg-modified-Final.pdf); [WHO ACS 2022 — Remarks](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/)).
  - WHO and NICE allow one repeat course if ≥7 days since prior course with imminent birth (NICE: <34+0 weeks, birth within 48 h; max 2 courses) ([WHO ACS 2022 — Rec 1.10](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.9.4–1.9.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
- **Citations:** [MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0), [MoHFW PMSMA — Preterm Labour](https://pmsma.mohfw.gov.in/wp-content/uploads/2016/10/High-Risk-Conditions-in-preg-modified-Final.pdf), [WHO ACS 2022 — Rec 1.9, Rec 1.10, Remarks](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585362/), [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.9.4–1.9.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)

### Step: Short-Term Tocolysis (Nifedipine)
- **Timing:** Short-term up to 48 hours only
- **Points:**
  - Use tocolysis short-term (up to 48 hours) only to complete antenatal corticosteroids and/or arrange in-utero transfer to an SNCU/NICU — never as maintenance therapy ([WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/); [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor)).
  - WHO (2022) recommends oral nifedipine at 24+0–33+6 weeks; NICE NG25 considers nifedipine at 24+0–25+6 and offers it at 26+0–33+6 weeks (intact membranes) ([WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.8.2–1.8.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - WHO (2022) nifedipine regimen: 20 mg oral immediate-release initial dose, then 10 mg PO every 6 hours as monotherapy until transfer or course completion ([WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3 Remarks](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/)).
  - Offer atosiban if nifedipine is contraindicated; do NOT offer betamimetics (ritodrine, terbutaline) due to life-threatening maternal cardiovascular toxicity ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.8.4–1.8.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3 Remarks](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/)).
  - Avoid sublingual nifedipine (precipitous hypotension) and avoid or exercise extreme caution combining nifedipine with MgSO4 (synergistic hypotension/neuromuscular blockade) ([FOGSI PTL 2025 — Tocolysis](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/TOG-Conclave-Pre-term-labour-Practice-Algorithms.pdf)).
- **Checklist:** Contraindications to Tocolysis
  - Chorioamnionitis or intrauterine infection
  - Placental abruption or significant APH with haemodynamic instability
  - Severe pre-eclampsia or eclampsia
  - Intrauterine fetal death (IUFD), lethal fetal anomaly, or fetal compromise
  - Gestational age ≥34+0 weeks
  - Maternal cardiac disease or hypotension (contraindication to nifedipine)
- **Citations:** [WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/), [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.8.2–1.8.5](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations), [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor), [FOGSI PTL 2025 — Tocolysis](https://www.fogsi.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/TOG-Conclave-Pre-term-labour-Practice-Algorithms.pdf)

### Step: Magnesium Sulfate for Fetal Neuroprotection
- **Timing:** Within 24 hours of planned or expected preterm birth
- **Points:**
  - WHO (2015) and ACOG strongly recommend MgSO4 for fetal neuroprotection when preterm birth is imminent below 32 weeks gestation ([WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 3](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download); [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor)).
  - NICE NG25 offers MgSO4 at 24+0–29+6 weeks, considers it at 30+0–33+6 weeks, and discusses it at 23+0–23+6 weeks in established PTL or planned birth within 24 h ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.10.1–1.10.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - NICE regimen: 4 g IV bolus over 15 minutes, followed by 1 g/hour IV infusion until birth or for 24 hours, whichever is sooner ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.10.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - Monitor pulse, BP, respiratory rate, and patellar reflexes at least every 4 hours; reduce or stop MgSO4 if oliguria or renal failure develops (see Eclampsia) ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.10.5–1.10.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
  - For MgSO4 toxicity (respiratory depression or absent reflexes), stop infusion, support airway/breathing, and give calcium gluconate 1 g (10 mL of 10%) IV slowly over 3 minutes ([WHO MCPC — MgSO4 Toxicity](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf)).
- **Citations:** [WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 3](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download), [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor), [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.10.1–1.10.6](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations), [WHO MCPC — MgSO4 Toxicity](https://healthynewbornnetwork.org/hnn-content/uploads/MCPC-2nd-ed.pdf)

### Step: PPROM Antibiotics & Intrapartum GBS Prophylaxis
- **Points:**
  - WHO (2015), NICE NG25, and RCOG GTG 73 recommend oral erythromycin 250 mg every 6 hours for 10 days or until established labour in PPROM ([WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 5.1](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.4.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [RCOG GTG 73 — Antibiotics](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/care-of-women-presenting-with-suspected-preterm-prelabour-rupture-of-membranes-from-24plus0-weeks-of-gestation-green-top-guideline-no-73/)).
  - If erythromycin is contraindicated or not tolerated, give an oral penicillin (e.g., amoxicillin) for up to 10 days or until established labour ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.4.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 5.1](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download)).
  - ACOG PB 217 advises IV ampicillin 2 g + erythromycin 250 mg q6h × 48 h, then PO amoxicillin 250 mg + erythromycin 333 mg q8h × 5 days ([ACOG PB 217 — Latency Antibiotics](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/prelabor-rupture-of-membranes-number-217.pdf)).
  - Never prescribe co-amoxiclav (amoxicillin–clavulanate) in PPROM due to increased neonatal necrotising enterocolitis (NEC) risk ([NICE NG25 — Rec 1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 5.2](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download)).
  - Do not give prophylactic antibiotics for intact-membrane PTL; offer intrapartum IV benzylpenicillin in established PTL (add gentamicin + metronidazole if chorioamnionitis; see Sepsis and septic shock) ([WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 4](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download); [NICE NG195 — Rec 1.6.1–1.6.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng195/chapter/Prevention-and-risk-reduction)).
- **Checklist:** Antibiotic Safety & Chorioamnionitis Rules
  - NEVER give co-amoxiclav (amoxicillin–clavulanate) for PPROM prophylaxis
  - Do NOT give routine antibiotics for preterm labour with intact membranes before established labour
  - Use cephalosporin (e.g., cefotaxime) for non-severe penicillin allergy or IV vancomycin for severe allergy
  - Promptly treat clinical chorioamnionitis and expedite delivery (see Sepsis and septic shock)
- **Citations:** [WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 4, 5.1, 5.2](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download), [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.4.1–1.4.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations), [RCOG GTG 73 — Antibiotics](https://www.rcog.org.uk/guidance/browse-all-guidance/green-top-guidelines/care-of-women-presenting-with-suspected-preterm-prelabour-rupture-of-membranes-from-24plus0-weeks-of-gestation-green-top-guideline-no-73/), [ACOG PB 217 — Latency Antibiotics](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/prelabor-rupture-of-membranes-number-217.pdf), [NICE NG195 — Rec 1.6.1–1.6.4](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng195/chapter/Prevention-and-risk-reduction)

### Note
- Late-preterm corticosteroids (34+0–36+6 weeks) differ across guidelines: MoHFW and WHO (2022) restrict ACS to 24+0–34+0 weeks; NICE NG25 considers ACS at 34+0–35+6 weeks; RCOG GTG 74 offers up to 34+6 weeks; ACOG recommends a single betamethasone course up to 36+6 weeks if no prior ACS ([MoHFW ACS 2014 — Operational Guidelines](https://www.medbox.org/index.php/dl/5e148832db60a2044c2d3cf0); [WHO ACS 2022 — Table 1, Rec 1.0](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585353/table/fm-ch1.tab1/); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.9.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [RCOG GTG 74 — Key Points](https://www.rcog.org.uk/media/khqhzmcc/gtg74-antenatal-corticosteroids-key-points.pdf); [ACOG CO 713 — Summary](https://www.ovid.com/jnls/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000002237~committee-opinion-no-713-antenatal-corticosteroid-therapy)).
- Nifedipine tocolysis dosing varies: WHO (2022) recommends 20 mg PO initial then 10 mg q6h (for 3–7 days or until transfer), whereas NICE NG25 uses 20 mg PO then 10–20 mg TID–QID and ACOG PB 171 uses 30 mg PO loading then 10–20 mg q4–6h for up to 48 hours ([WHO Tocolytics 2022 — §3 Remarks](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK585030/); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.8.2–1.8.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor)).
- Magnesium sulfate neuroprotection upper gestational cut-off differs: WHO (2015) and ACOG recommend <32 weeks, while NICE NG25 offers at 24+0–29+6 weeks and considers at 30+0–33+6 weeks ([WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 3](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download); [ACOG PB 171 — Level A Rec](https://journals.lww.com/greenjournal/fulltext/10.1097/aog.0000000000001711~practice-bulletin-no-171-management-of-preterm-labor); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.10.2–1.10.3](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations)).
- PPROM antibiotic regimens differ: WHO, NICE NG25, and RCOG GTG 73 use oral erythromycin 250 mg q6h × 10 days (ORACLE I), whereas ACOG PB 217 uses a 7-day IV-to-oral ampicillin plus erythromycin regimen (NICHD MFMU) ([WHO Preterm 2015 — Rec 5.1](https://iris.who.int/bitstreams/be15a35e-d299-4882-8f84-86976d87fd7b/download); [NICE NG25 — Rec 1.4.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng25/chapter/recommendations); [ACOG PB 217 — Latency Antibiotics](https://tjodistanbul.org/uploads/prelabor-rupture-of-membranes-number-217.pdf)).
