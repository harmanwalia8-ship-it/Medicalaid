# Medicalaid — Batch D4: Antifungal, Antiviral, and Antiparasitic

## Drug: fluconazole
- **Name:** Fluconazole
- **Aliases:** `Diflucan`, `Forcan`, `Fluka`, `Syscan`
- **Class:** Triazole antifungal
- **Uses:** Mucosal and invasive candidiasis, cryptococcal meningitis, and bone marrow transplant candidiasis prophylaxis
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- Oral and IV daily doses are identical; give double loading dose on day 1 for steady state ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- IV infusion maximum rate is 200 mg/hour as continuous infusion ([FDA IV label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=02f74fe7-0649-46c5-a06a-0700cd265773)).
- Vaginal candidiasis: 150 mg PO single dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Oropharyngeal candidiasis — India: 100–150 mg (mild: 200 mg) PO daily for 7–14 days ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §8.1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/8/1)).
- Oropharyngeal candidiasis — FDA: 200 mg day 1, then 100 mg PO/IV daily for ≥14 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Oesophageal candidiasis — India: 200–400 mg PO/IV daily for 14 days ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §4.1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/4/1)).
- Oesophageal candidiasis — FDA: 200 mg day 1, then 100–400 mg daily ≥21 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Invasive candidiasis — India: 800 mg (12 mg/kg) load, then 400 mg (6 mg/kg) PO/IV daily ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §18.2](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/18/2)).
- Invasive candidiasis — FDA: up to 400 mg PO/IV daily; optimal duration not established ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Step-down from echinocandin after 5–7 days if stable, susceptible, and repeat cultures negative ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §18.2](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/18/2)).
- Cryptococcal meningitis — India/WHO: 1200 mg daily × 14 days with amphotericin B ± flucytosine ([WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/)).
- Cryptococcal consolidation — India/WHO: 400–800 mg (WHO: 800 mg) PO daily for 8 weeks ([India Crypto Protocol — Methods](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/6/e070500), [WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/)).
- Cryptococcal maintenance — India/WHO: 200 mg PO daily until immune reconstitution on ART ([WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/)).
- Cryptococcal meningitis — FDA: 400 mg day 1, then 200–400 mg daily for 10–12 weeks ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Bone marrow transplant prophylaxis: 400 mg PO/IV once daily until neutrophils >1000/mm³ × 7 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

- Oesophageal candidiasis (≥6 months): 6 mg/kg day 1, then 3–12 mg/kg PO/IV daily ≥21 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- **Dose:** Fluconazole — oesophageal candidiasis (≥6 months) | 3–12 mg/kg per day (after 6 mg/kg load) | max — | PO/IV ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204))
- Systemic candidiasis (≥3 months): 25 mg/kg day 1 (max 800 mg), then 12 mg/kg daily (max 400 mg) ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- **Dose:** Fluconazole — systemic candidiasis (≥3 months) | 12–12 mg/kg per day (after 25 mg/kg load, max 800 mg) | max 400 mg | PO/IV ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204))
- Systemic candidiasis (birth–3 months): 25 mg/kg load, then 12 mg/kg (GA ≥30 wk) or 9 mg/kg (GA <30 wk) daily ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Cryptococcal meningitis — India/WHO: 12 mg/kg daily (max 800 mg) induction × 14 days with amphotericin B ([WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/)).
- Cryptococcal consolidation (children/adolescents): 6–12 mg/kg PO daily (max 800 mg) for 8 weeks ([WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/)).
- **Dose:** Fluconazole — cryptococcal maintenance (children/adolescents) | 6–6 mg/kg per day | max — | PO ([WHO Cryptococcal Guidelines — §3.5](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK581839/))

### Before starting
- Obtain fungal cultures before starting; *Candida krusei* is intrinsically resistant and *C. glabrata* shows reduced susceptibility ([FDA label — Microbiology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Screen for QT-prolonging drugs, structural heart disease, hypokalaemia, and baseline renal or hepatic impairment ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Exclude pregnancy before initiating; avoid in first trimester unless infection is life-threatening ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204), [ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §8.1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/8/1)).
- Avoid oral suspension in hereditary fructose intolerance, glucose-galactose malabsorption, or sucrase-isomaltase deficiency ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor liver function tests if abnormal at baseline or during prolonged therapy; stop if hepatitis develops ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Monitor renal function in elderly or renally impaired patients on multiple-dose regimens ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Watch for progressive rash; discontinue immediately in superficial infections or progressing deep-infection rashes ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Kidney
- No dose adjustment needed for single 150 mg vaginal candidiasis dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Multiple doses: give full loading dose (50–400 mg), then 100% daily dose if CrCl >50 mL/min ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- CrCl ≤50 mL/min (no dialysis): give 50% of recommended daily dose after loading dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Haemodialysis: give 100% dose after each session (3-hour session removes ~50%); reduce non-dialysis days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Liver
- Use with caution in liver dysfunction; no formal dose adjustment specified ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Discontinue if clinical signs or symptoms of liver disease develop ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Common side effects
- Headache (13% single dose, 1.9% multiple), nausea (7% / 3.7%), and abdominal pain (6% / 1.7%) ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Diarrhoea (3% / 1.5%), vomiting (1.7%), skin rash (1.8%), dyspepsia, dizziness, and taste perversion ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Serious side effects
- QT prolongation and torsades de pointes via Ikr inhibition, especially with hypokalaemia or heart disease ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Rare serious hepatotoxicity including hepatitis, cholestasis, and fatal fulminant liver failure ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Exfoliative skin reactions including Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, DRESS, and AGEP ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Anaphylaxis, reversible adrenal insufficiency, seizures, leukopenia, agranulocytosis, and thrombocytopenia ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** >90% oral bioavailability; Tmax 1–2 h; unaffected by food. - **Distribution:** Vd approximates total body water; protein binding 11–12%; CSF ~80% of plasma in meningitis. - **Metabolism:** Minimal hepatic (~11% urinary metabolites). - **Elimination:** ~80% unchanged in urine; clearance 0.23 mL/min/kg. - **Half-life:** ~30 h (range 20–50 h); prolonged in renal impairment.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)

### Interactions
- Strong CYP2C19 inhibitor; moderate CYP2C9 and CYP3A4 inhibitor; inhibition persists 4–5 days after stopping ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Contraindicated with CYP3A4-metabolised QT-prolonging drugs: quinidine, pimozide, and erythromycin ([FDA label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Increases warfarin PT/INR, phenytoin AUC (+88%), sulfonylureas (hypoglycaemia), carbamazepine (+30%), and theophylline ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Increases oral tacrolimus (up to 5-fold), cyclosporine AUC (+92%), sirolimus, statins (myopathy), and midazolam ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Rifampicin reduces fluconazole AUC by 23%; consider higher fluconazole dose ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Avoid with voriconazole, abrocitinib, lemborexant, and olaparib; monitor QT with amiodarone ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Pregnancy
- Avoid in pregnancy except severe or life-threatening fungal infections where benefit outweighs fetal risk ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- High-dose chronic use (400–800 mg/day) in first trimester causes distinctive craniofacial, skeletal, and cardiac defects ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Single or repeated 150 mg first-trimester doses linked to spontaneous abortion and anomalies in observational studies ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).
- Use effective contraception during 400–800 mg/day therapy and for ~1 week after final dose ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

### Breastfeeding
- Present in breast milk at low levels after single 150 mg dose (~0.39 mg/kg/day infant dose); exercise caution ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?audience=consumer&setid=f694c617-3383-416c-91b6-b94fda371204)).

## Drug: itraconazole
- **Name:** Itraconazole
- **Aliases:** `Sporanox`, `Canditral`, `Itaspor`
- **Class:** Triazole antifungal
- **Uses:** Systemic mycoses (histoplasmosis, blastomycosis, aspergillosis), onychomycosis, and refractory oropharyngeal/oesophageal candidiasis
- **Clearance:** Hepatic, Biliary

### Boxed warning
- Contraindicated for onychomycosis in patients with ventricular dysfunction, congestive heart failure (CHF), or CHF history ([FDA capsule label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Negative inotropic effect; discontinue capsules or reassess oral solution if CHF signs or symptoms develop ([FDA capsule label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0), [FDA solution label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06)).
- Contraindicated with many CYP3A4 substrates (e.g., quinidine, methadone, pimozide, simvastatin, felodipine) due to fatal arrhythmias/toxicity ([FDA capsule label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Dose
- Capsules and oral solution are NOT interchangeable; oral solution gives higher systemic exposure ([FDA capsule label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Take capsules whole immediately after a full meal; use non-diet cola if on acid suppressors ([FDA capsule label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Take oral solution without food; swish 10 mL vigorously in mouth for several seconds, swallow ([FDA solution label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06)).
- Blastomycosis and histoplasmosis (capsules): 200 mg daily; increase by 100 mg up to 400 mg/day ([FDA capsule label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Aspergillosis (capsules, amphotericin-intolerant/refractory): 200–400 mg/day; divide doses >200 mg/day into two doses ([FDA capsule label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Life-threatening systemic mycoses (capsules): load 200 mg three times daily for 3 days, treat ≥3 months ([FDA capsule label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Toenail onychomycosis (capsules): 200 mg once daily for 12 consecutive weeks ([FDA capsule label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Fingernail onychomycosis (capsules): two 1-week pulses of 200 mg twice daily, 3 weeks apart ([FDA capsule label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Oropharyngeal candidiasis (oral solution only): 200 mg (20 mL) daily × 1–2 weeks; refractory: 100 mg BD ([FDA solution label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06), [ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §8.1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/8/1)).
- Oesophageal candidiasis — India: 200 mg PO daily for 14 days as alternative ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §4.1](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/4/1)).
- Oesophageal candidiasis — FDA (oral solution): 100 mg (up to 200 mg) daily ≥3 weeks ([FDA solution label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06)).

### Children
- Safety and efficacy not established in paediatric patients; animal studies showed bone growth defects ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Before starting
- Confirm fungal diagnosis by culture, KOH, or histology before therapy; exclude CHF and ventricular dysfunction ([FDA capsule label — Indications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Review all concomitant medications for contraindicated CYP3A4 substrates and gastric acid suppressors ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Check baseline liver enzymes; strongly avoid in active liver disease or prior drug hepatotoxicity ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- For onychomycosis in women of childbearing potential, start day 2–3 of menses with effective contraception ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Monitoring
- Consider liver function monitoring in all patients; stop immediately and test LFTs if liver symptoms occur ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Monitor for CHF signs (dyspnoea, oedema, weight gain), blood pressure, and serum potassium (pseudoaldosteronism) ([FDA capsule label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Stop if peripheral neuropathy or hearing loss develops ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Kidney
- Limited oral data; systemic exposure may drop 30–40% in moderate-to-severe renal impairment; use cautiously ([FDA capsule label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Not removed by haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis ([FDA capsule label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Liver
- Cirrhosis reduces Cmax by 47% and doubles half-life (~37 h vs 16 h); monitor closely ([FDA capsule label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Strongly discouraged in elevated liver enzymes or active liver disease unless life-threatening ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Common side effects
- Nausea (11%), rash (9%), vomiting (5%), oedema (4%), headache (4%), diarrhoea (3–11%) ([FDA capsule label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0), [FDA solution label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06)).
- Fatigue, fever, pruritus, abdominal pain, hypertension (3%), abnormal LFTs (3%), and hypokalaemia (2%) ([FDA capsule label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Serious side effects
- Rare serious hepatotoxicity including fatal acute liver failure, sometimes within the first week ([FDA capsule label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Congestive heart failure, pulmonary oedema, and fatal cardiac dysrhythmias with interacting drugs ([FDA capsule label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Pseudoaldosteronism (hypertension, hypokalaemia), peripheral neuropathy, transient or permanent hearing loss, and pancreatitis ([FDA capsule label — Warnings, Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, AGEP, anaphylaxis, leukopenia, neutropenia, and thrombocytopenia ([FDA capsule label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Bioavailability ~55% (capsules maximal after full meal; oral solution +30% fasting); Tmax 2–5 h (solution 2.5 h). - **Distribution:** Vd >700 L; protein binding 99.8%; high skin/nail uptake; very low CSF. - **Metabolism:** Extensive hepatic via CYP3A4 (saturable, nonlinear); active metabolite hydroxy-itraconazole. - **Elimination:** Inactive metabolites in faeces (54%) and urine (35%); <1% unchanged renal. - **Half-life:** 16–28 h single dose; 34–42 h repeated dosing; steady state ~15 days.
- **Citations:** [FDA capsule label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0), [FDA solution label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=43ded1aa-f825-40fe-aa9a-addd36a07f06)

### Interactions

- Contraindicated: lovastatin, simvastatin, lomitapide, felodipine, nisoldipine, ivabradine, ranolazine, eplerenone, finerenone, ticagrelor, cisapride, irinotecan ([FDA capsule label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- Colchicine: contraindicated in renal or hepatic impairment; avoid apixaban, rivaroxaban, sirolimus, and everolimus ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).
- CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, rifabutin, isoniazid, phenytoin, phenobarbital, carbamazepine, efavirenz, nevirapine): avoid 2 weeks before and during therapy ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

- Monitor or reduce dose of warfarin, digoxin, atorvastatin, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, dexamethasone, diltiazem, verapamil, and inhaled steroids ([FDA capsule label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Pregnancy
- Contraindicated for onychomycosis in pregnancy; use contraception during therapy and for 2 months after stopping ([FDA capsule label — Contraindications, Pregnancy](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=a4d555fa-787c-40fb-bb7d-b0d4f7318fd0)).

### Breastfeeding

## Drug: amphotericin-b
- **Name:** Amphotericin B
- **Aliases:** `amphotericin B liposomal`, `amphotericin B deoxycholate`, `L-AmB`, `AmBisome`, `Fungisome`, `Fungizone`
- **Class:** Polyene antifungal
- **Uses:** Invasive fungal infections, mucormycosis, cryptococcal meningitis, and visceral leishmaniasis (kala-azar)
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Boxed warning
- Conventional deoxycholate: reserve for progressive, life-threatening fungal infections; avoid for noninvasive disease with normal neutrophils ([FDA D-AmB — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Conventional deoxycholate: never exceed 1.5 mg/kg/day; overdose risks fatal cardiac or cardiopulmonary arrest ([FDA D-AmB — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Verify product name and dose before administration, especially if prescribed dose exceeds 1.5 mg/kg ([FDA D-AmB — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Dose
- Liposomal and conventional deoxycholate are NOT interchangeable mg-for-mg; never substitute directly ([FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Liposomal — kala-azar: India: 10 mg/kg single IV dose over 2 h; FDA: 3 mg/kg days 1–5, 14, 21 ([NCVBDC KA — §8.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/opertional-guideline-KA-2015.pdf), [FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Liposomal — mucormycosis: 5 mg/kg/day IV (10 mg/kg/day for CNS spread) for ≥4–6 weeks ([CAM Review — Treatment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8601521/)).
- Liposomal — HIV cryptococcal meningitis: WHO: 10 mg/kg single IV dose + flucytosine + fluconazole; FDA: 6 mg/kg/day IV ([WHO Crypto — Induction](https://www.nicd.ac.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022-WHO-Guideline-Crypto-Final-9789240052178-eng.pdf), [FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Liposomal — systemic mycoses: 3–5 mg/kg/day IV; empirical febrile neutropenia: 3 mg/kg/day IV ([FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Liposomal infusion: dilute in 5% dextrose only (never saline); infuse over 2 h (1 h if tolerated) ([FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Deoxycholate — test dose: 1 mg IV in 20 mL 5% dextrose over 20–30 min; check vitals q30 min × 2–4 h ([FDA D-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Deoxycholate — systemic mycoses: start 0.25–0.3 mg/kg/day IV, escalate to 0.5–1 mg/kg/day; max 1.5 mg/kg/day ([FDA D-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Deoxycholate — HIV cryptococcal meningitis: India: 0.7–1 mg/kg/day IV × 14 d + fluconazole; WHO: 1 mg/kg/day × 7 d + flucytosine ([India Crypto Protocol — Methods](https://bmjopen.bmj.com/content/13/6/e070500), [WHO Crypto — Induction](https://www.nicd.ac.za/wp-content/uploads/2023/01/2022-WHO-Guideline-Crypto-Final-9789240052178-eng.pdf)).
- Deoxycholate infusion: dilute to 0.1 mg/mL in 5% dextrose (never saline); infuse slowly over 2–6 h ([FDA D-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Children
- Liposomal (1 month–16 yr): same mg/kg dosing as adults; safety under 1 month not established ([FDA L-AmB — Pediatric Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- **Dose:** Liposomal amphotericin B — systemic fungal infections (1 mo–16 yr) | 3–5 mg/kg per day | max — | IV over 2 h ([FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display))
- **Dose:** Liposomal amphotericin B — visceral leishmaniasis (<15 yr) | 10–10 mg/kg per dose | max — | single IV over 2 h ([Bihar VL Study — Methods](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5817748/))
- Conventional deoxycholate: controlled paediatric data lacking; use smallest effective dose, never >1.5 mg/kg/day ([FDA D-AmB — Pediatric Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Before starting
- Pre-hydrate with normal saline IV (adults 1 L; children 10–15 mL/kg, max 1 L) to reduce nephrotoxicity ([FDA D-AmB — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce), [Crypto Infusion Guide — Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13058496/)).
- Flush IV line with 5% dextrose before and after infusion; saline precipitates the drug ([FDA L-AmB — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Check baseline serum creatinine, BUN, potassium, magnesium, LFTs, and CBC ([FDA L-AmB — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Premedicate with antipyretics, antihistamines, or low-dose corticosteroids if reactions occur; meperidine 25–50 mg IV treats rigors ([FDA D-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor serum creatinine, BUN, potassium, magnesium, LFTs, and CBC/haemoglobin regularly during therapy ([FDA D-AmB — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Observe vitals closely during initial infusions for fever, rigors, hypotension, hypoxia, or anaphylaxis ([FDA L-AmB — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Liposomal product may falsely elevate serum phosphate on PHOSm laboratory assays ([FDA L-AmB — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).

### Kidney
- Liposomal causes less nephrotoxicity than deoxycholate and is preferred in pre-existing renal impairment ([FDA L-AmB — Indications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Deoxycholate causes azotaemia, renal tubular acidosis, hypokalaemia, and hypomagnesaemia; cumulative >5 g risks permanent damage ([FDA D-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Neither formulation is significantly cleared by haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis ([FDA L-AmB — Overdosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display), [FDA D-AmB — Pharmacokinetics](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Liver
- Effect of hepatic impairment on liposomal or deoxycholate pharmacokinetics is unknown; monitor LFTs regularly ([FDA L-AmB — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).

### Common side effects
- Infusion reactions: fever, chills/rigors (higher with deoxycholate), nausea, vomiting, headache, back/chest pain ([FDA L-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).
- Hypokalaemia, hypomagnesaemia, hypocalcaemia, raised creatinine/BUN, normocytic anaemia, diarrhoea, and thrombophlebitis ([FDA D-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce), [FDA L-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).

### Serious side effects
- Acute renal failure, nephrocalcinosis, renal tubular acidosis, and nephrogenic diabetes insipidus ([FDA D-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Anaphylaxis, bronchospasm, pulmonary oedema, cardiac arrest/arrhythmias, acute liver failure, agranulocytosis, and SJS/TEN ([FDA D-AmB — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce), [FDA L-AmB — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** IV only; nonlinear PK (liposomal). - **Distribution:** >90% protein-bound (D-AmB); CSF <2.5% plasma; Vss 0.10–0.44 L/kg (liposomal). - **Metabolism:** Unknown. - **Elimination:** D-AmB slow renal (2–5% active; urine ≥7 wk); liposomal unstudied. - **Half-life:** D-AmB 24 h initial, ~15 d terminal; liposomal 7–10 h (24 h), 100–153 h terminal.
- **Citations:** [FDA L-AmB — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display), [FDA D-AmB — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)

### Interactions
- Nephrotoxins (aminoglycosides, cyclosporine, pentamidine, antineoplastics): additive renal toxicity; monitor renal function intensively ([FDA D-AmB — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

- Flucytosine: synergistic, but impaired renal clearance increases flucytosine toxicity ([FDA D-AmB — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).
- Leukocyte transfusions: risk of acute pulmonary toxicity; separate infusions as far apart as possible ([FDA D-AmB — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

### Pregnancy

### Breastfeeding
- Human milk excretion unknown; discontinue nursing or drug given potential serious infant toxicity ([FDA L-AmB — Nursing Mothers](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=f7be6506-4d20-401e-a0ff-02ad7c33158a&type=display), [FDA D-AmB — Nursing Mothers](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=a0a54943-9ce4-4f3e-b681-a1a9144c16ce)).

## Drug: terbinafine
- **Name:** Terbinafine
- **Aliases:** `terbinafine hydrochloride`, `Lamisil`
- **Class:** Allylamine antifungal
- **Uses:** Dermatophyte onychomycosis, tinea corporis, tinea cruris, tinea pedis, and tinea capitis
- **Clearance:** Hepatic, Renal

### Dose
- Fingernail onychomycosis: 250 mg orally once daily for 6 weeks, with or without food ([FDA Terbinafine — §2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Toenail onychomycosis: 250 mg orally once daily for 12 weeks ([FDA Terbinafine — §2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Tinea corporis, cruris, or pedis (extensive/naïve): 250 mg orally once daily for 2–4 weeks ([ECTODERM India — Systemic Therapy](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6057051/)).
- Recalcitrant dermatophytosis (India): 500 mg/day orally for >4 weeks may be considered (off-label) ([ECTODERM India — Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6057051/)).
- Tinea capitis (>35 kg): 250 mg orally once daily for 6 weeks (granules taken with food) ([FDA Lamisil Granules — §2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/022071s009lbl.pdf)).

### Children
- Tinea capitis (≥4 yr, oral granules × 6 wk): <25 kg: 125 mg OD; 25–35 kg: 187.5 mg OD; >35 kg: 250 mg OD ([FDA Lamisil Granules — §2](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/022071s009lbl.pdf)).
- Onychomycosis: safety and efficacy of oral tablets not established in children ([FDA Terbinafine — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Superficial tinea (India): prefer topicals; reserve oral terbinafine for extensive/recalcitrant disease in children >2 yr ([ECTODERM India — Paediatric](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6057051/)).

### Before starting
- Confirm dermatophyte infection (KOH mount, culture, or nail biopsy) before starting oral therapy ([FDA Terbinafine — §1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Check baseline serum transaminases (ALT, AST) in all patients; avoid in chronic or active liver disease ([FDA Terbinafine — §5.1, §5.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Contraindicated with history of allergic reaction to oral terbinafine due to anaphylaxis risk ([FDA Terbinafine — §4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor LFTs periodically; stop immediately if LFTs rise or jaundice, dark urine, or RUQ pain occur ([FDA Terbinafine — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Check CBC if infection signs appear or >6 wk use in immunodeficiency; stop if ANC <1,000/mm³ ([FDA Terbinafine — §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).
- Stop promptly if taste/smell disturbance, depressive symptoms, progressive rash, or lupus signs occur ([FDA Terbinafine — §5.2–5.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).

### Kidney
- Clearance drops ~50% when CrCl ≤50 mL/min; use not adequately studied in CrCl ≤50 mL/min ([FDA Terbinafine — §8.6, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).

### Liver
- Not recommended in chronic or active liver disease; clearance drops ~50% in hepatic cirrhosis ([FDA Terbinafine — §5.1, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).

### Common side effects
- Headache, diarrhoea, dyspepsia, nausea, abdominal pain, flatulence, rash, pruritus, LFT abnormalities, and taste disturbance ([FDA Terbinafine — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=bcf7e761-786b-4ac8-aecd-ea1c745880b1)).

### Serious side effects

## Drug: oseltamivir
- **Name:** Oseltamivir
- **Aliases:** `oseltamivir phosphate`, `Fluvir`, `Antiflu`, `Tamiflu`
- **Class:** Influenza neuraminidase inhibitor (antiviral)
- **Uses:** Treatment and prophylaxis of influenza A and B
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- Adult treatment (>40 kg; ≥13 yr FDA / >12 yr India): 75 mg PO twice daily for 5 days ([NCDC Protocol — §4.3](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/66872871241561442115.pdf), [FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=ee3c9555-60f2-4f82-a760-11983c86e97b)).

- India: treat Category B (high fever/high-risk) and C (hospitalised) empirically; withhold in Category A; WHO: severe influenza only ([NCDC Categorization — p. 1–3](https://ncdc.mohfw.gov.in/uploads/pdf/72513234671561442053-1.pdf), [WHO Influenza Summary — §3.1](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13277511/)).

- Test for HIV-1 before starting HBV monotherapy; never use TDF alone in HIV-1 ([NVHCP HBV — p. 24](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.1, §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Test for HBsAg before initiating TDF for HIV ([FDA label — §2.1, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Monitoring
- Serum creatinine/eGFR and urine protein every 6 months (every 3 mo Year 1 in HBV; closer if CrCl <60 or phosphate <2 mg/dL) ([NACO HIV — Table 2.2.11](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/files/National_Guidelines_for_HIV_Care_and_Treatment%202021.pdf), [NVHCP HBV — p. 24, 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).
- Chronic HBV: ALT and APRI/FIB-4 every 6 months; HBV DNA annually ([NVHCP HBV — p. 24](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).
- Consider BMD assessment in patients with fragility fracture history or osteoporosis risk factors ([FDA label — §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Evaluate renal function if persistent bone pain, extremity pain, fractures, or muscle weakness develops ([FDA label — §5.2, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Kidney
- India: prefer entecavir or TAF for HBV if eGFR <60, proteinuria, or phosphate <2.5 mg/dL; switch HIV to ABC+3TC+DTG if creatinine >ULN ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23, 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [NACO HIV — Table 2.2.9](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/files/National_Guidelines_for_HIV_Care_and_Treatment%202021.pdf)).

- Haemodialysis: 300 mg every 7 days or after ~12 hours total dialysis, given post-HD ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- CrCl <10 mL/min not on HD and paediatric renal impairment: no dosing data ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Liver
- No dose adjustment required in moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Monitor closely in decompensated cirrhosis (higher renal toxicity risk) and for flares after stopping ([FDA label — §5.1, §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Common side effects
- HIV: rash, diarrhoea, nausea, headache, pain, depression, asthenia (≥10%) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Compensated HBV: nausea (9%), abdominal pain, diarrhoea, headache, dizziness, fatigue, rash ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Decompensated HBV: abdominal pain (22%), nausea (20%), insomnia (18%), pruritus (16%), vomiting, dizziness, fever ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Serious side effects
- Acute renal failure, Fanconi syndrome, proximal renal tubulopathy, hypophosphataemia, nephrogenic diabetes insipidus ([FDA label — §5.2, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

- Severe acute exacerbation of hepatitis B upon discontinuation ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis (suspend if suspected; may be fatal) ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS) in HIV ([FDA label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418), [NACO HIV — §2.2.9](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/files/National_Guidelines_for_HIV_Care_and_Treatment%202021.pdf)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** ~25% oral bioavailability fasted (+40% AUC high-fat meal); Tmax 1.0 ± 0.4 h. - **Distribution:** Vss 1.2–1.3 L/kg; plasma protein binding <0.7% (serum <7.2%). - **Metabolism:** Diester hydrolysis to tenofovir, then intracellular phosphorylation; not a CYP substrate/inhibitor. - **Elimination:** 70–80% unchanged in urine (IV) via filtration and active tubular secretion. - **Half-life:** ~17 h (terminal plasma).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)

### Interactions
- Avoid concurrent/recent nephrotoxic or tubular-secreted drugs (high-dose/multiple NSAIDs, aminoglycosides, aciclovir, cidofovir, ganciclovir, valganciclovir) ([FDA label — §5.2, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Do not combine with adefovir dipivoxil in chronic hepatitis B ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Didanosine: increases didanosine levels (pancreatitis/neuropathy); reduce didanosine dose and monitor closely ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Atazanavir: lowers atazanavir; must boost with ritonavir (300/100 mg) ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- Boosted PIs (LPV/r, ATV/r, DRV/r) and HCV DAAs (SOF/VEL, LDV/SOF): raise tenofovir; monitor renal toxicity ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

### Pregnancy
- No increased major birth-defect risk in registry data; preferred HBV agent in childbearing women ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418), [NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).
- HIV: TLD (TDF 300 mg) is preferred lifelong first-line ART in pregnancy ([NACO HIV — §2.2.6](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/files/National_Guidelines_for_HIV_Care_and_Treatment%202021.pdf)).
- HBV MTCT prophylaxis: WHO: TDF from 2nd trimester if HBV DNA ≥200,000 IU/mL or HBeAg+ (or all HBsAg+ if testing unavailable); India NVHCP 2019: prioritise birth-dose vaccine + HBIG ([WHO HBV 2024 — §7.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615006/), [NVHCP HBV — p. 24–25](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).

### Breastfeeding
- Chronic HBV: breastfeeding permitted on TDF alongside infant birth-dose vaccine; very low milk levels ([NVHCP HBV — p. 25](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [WHO HBV 2024 — §7.3.1](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615006/), [FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).
- HIV: India: supports exclusive breastfeeding with maternal TLD ART and infant prophylaxis; FDA: advises against breastfeeding ([NACO HIV — §2.2.6](https://naco.mohfw.gov.in/files/National_Guidelines_for_HIV_Care_and_Treatment%202021.pdf), [FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=33fd6418-fbdc-42ca-a50d-ce2a476a5418)).

## Drug: entecavir
- **Name:** Entecavir
- **Aliases:** `Baraclude`, `Entehep`, `Entavir`
- **Class:** Guanosine nucleoside analogue reverse transcriptase inhibitor (antiviral)
- **Uses:** Chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) infection with active viral replication and liver inflammation
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Boxed warning
- Severe acute hepatitis B exacerbations occur after stopping; monitor liver function closely for several months ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Avoid in untreated HIV/HBV coinfection due to HIV NRTI resistance risk; use only with HAART ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis, including fatal cases, reported with nucleoside analogues ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Dose
- Take on an empty stomach, ≥2 hours after and ≥2 hours before meals ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Adults and adolescents ≥16 years, nucleoside-naïve, compensated liver disease: 0.5 mg PO once daily ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Adults with decompensated liver disease: 1 mg PO once daily ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Lamivudine-exposed/refractory HBV: India prefers tenofovir over entecavir; FDA recommends entecavir 1 mg PO once daily ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Preferred over tenofovir DF in age >60 years, bone disease, or eGFR <60 mL/min/1.73 m² ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).

### Children
- Approved for ≥2 years and ≥10 kg; safety and efficacy not established under 2 years ([FDA label — §2.3, §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- **Dose:** Entecavir — chronic HBV, treatment-naïve (≥2 yr, ≥10 kg) | 0.015–0.015 mg/kg per day | max 0.5 mg | PO once daily ([FDA label — §8.4, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20))
- **Dose:** Entecavir — chronic HBV, lamivudine-experienced (≥2 yr, ≥10 kg) | 0.03–0.03 mg/kg per day | max 1 mg | PO once daily ([FDA label — §8.4, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20))
- Weight-banded oral solution (0.05 mg/mL) for 10–30 kg: 3–9 mL daily (naïve) or 6–18 mL (lamivudine-experienced) ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Children >30 kg: 0.5 mg tablet or 10 mL daily (naïve); 1 mg tablet or 20 mL (lamivudine-experienced) ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Before starting
- Offer HIV antibody testing to all patients; exclude untreated HIV/HBV coinfection ([NVHCP HBV — p. 24](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Check baseline CBC, LFT (ALT/AST), APRI/FIB-4, abdominal ultrasound, HBV DNA, and renal function ([NVHCP HBV — p. 24, 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).

### Monitoring
- Check ALT, APRI/FIB-4, and renal function every 6 months; check HBV DNA annually ([NVHCP HBV — p. 24](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).
- High renal risk: monitor serum creatinine/eGFR every 3 months in year 1, then every 6 months ([NVHCP HBV — p. 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).
- Monitor hepatic function clinically and via labs for at least several months after stopping ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Suspend immediately if clinical or lab signs of lactic acidosis or pronounced hepatotoxicity appear ([FDA label — §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Kidney
- CrCl ≥50 mL/min: no adjustment; 0.5 mg daily (naïve) or 1 mg daily (refractory/decompensated) ([NVHCP HBV — p. 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- CrCl 30 to <50 mL/min: 0.25 mg daily or 0.5 mg q48h (1 mg regimen: 0.5 mg daily or 1 mg q48h) ([NVHCP HBV — p. 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- CrCl 10 to <30 mL/min: 0.15 mg daily or 0.5 mg q72h (1 mg regimen: 0.3 mg daily or 1 mg q72h) ([NVHCP HBV — p. 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- CrCl <10 mL/min, HD (give after session), or CAPD: 0.05 mg daily or 0.5 mg q7d (1 mg: 0.1 mg daily or 1 mg q7d) ([NVHCP HBV — p. 47](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Use oral solution for doses <0.5 mg; once-daily dosing is preferred over extended intervals ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Paediatric renal impairment: insufficient data; consider dose reduction or interval extension similar to adults ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Liver
- No dosage adjustment required for hepatic impairment ([FDA label — §2.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Decompensated liver disease requires 1 mg once daily and carries higher lactic acidosis risk ([FDA label — §2.2, §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Common side effects
- Headache, fatigue, dizziness, and nausea (≥3% in adults) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Children additionally report abdominal pain, rash, abnormal taste, diarrhoea, and vomiting ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Serious side effects
- Severe acute hepatitis B flare on discontinuation ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Lactic acidosis and severe hepatomegaly with steatosis (may be fatal) ([FDA label — §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Anaphylactoid reaction, alopecia, rash, and transaminase elevations (postmarketing) ([FDA label — §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Tmax 0.5–1.5 h; food lowers Cmax 44–46% and AUC 18–20%; tablet 100% bioavailable vs solution. - **Distribution:** Vd exceeds total body water; ~13% serum protein-bound. - **Metabolism:** Not a CYP450 substrate/inhibitor/inducer; minor glucuronide and sulfate conjugates. - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (62–73% unchanged in urine via filtration and tubular secretion). - **Half-life:** Terminal 128–149 h; effective accumulation ~24 h.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)

### Interactions

- No significant interaction with lamivudine, adefovir, or tenofovir DF; no CYP450 interactions ([FDA label — §7, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Monitor renal function closely in liver transplant recipients on cyclosporine or tacrolimus ([FDA label — §8.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Pregnancy
- India: not recommended in pregnancy; prefer tenofovir in women of childbearing age ([NVHCP HBV — p. 23](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf), [WHO HBV — 2024](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK615000/)).
- FDA: insufficient human data to assess birth-defect or miscarriage risk; register exposures in APR ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).

### Breastfeeding
- Unknown if excreted in human milk (present in rat milk); weigh maternal need vs infant risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=046e61c9-9298-4b2e-b76e-b26b81fecd20)).
- Mothers with HBV may breastfeed vaccinated infants unless bleeding or exuding nipple lesions exist ([NVHCP HBV — p. 25](https://nvhcp.mohfw.gov.in/common_libs/TechnicalGuidelines-for-Diagnosis-management-of-Hepatitis-B.pdf)).

### Children
- STH deworming (1–2 years): 200 mg PO single dose (half 400 mg tablet crushed with water, or 5 mL syrup) ([MoHFW NDD — p. 9](https://nhmmizoram.org/upload/NDD%20-%20Pocketbook%20for%20ASHA,%20AWW%20and%20Teachers%20(1).pdf), [WHO STH — 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK487918/)).

### Before starting
- Obtain pregnancy test in women of reproductive potential prior to systemic/prolonged therapy ([FDA label — §2.3, §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

- Neurocysticercosis: examine retina for lesions first; start corticosteroids and anticonvulsants to prevent cerebral hypertension/seizures ([FDA label — §2.2, §5.3, §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Evaluate at-risk hydatid patients for undiagnosed neurocysticercosis before starting ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Do not administer deworming tablets to acutely sick children; defer until healthy ([MoHFW NDD — p. 11](https://nhmmizoram.org/upload/NDD%20-%20Pocketbook%20for%20ASHA,%20AWW%20and%20Teachers%20(1).pdf)).

### Monitoring
- Prolonged therapy: check CBC and LFTs at start of each 28-day cycle and every 2 weeks ([FDA label — §2.3, §5.1, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Monitor CBC more frequently in liver disease or hepatic echinococcosis ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Stop if clinically significant cytopenia occurs; consider stopping if hepatic enzymes exceed 2× ULN ([FDA label — §5.1, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Kidney
- Pharmacokinetics not studied in renal impairment; urinary excretion of active metabolite is <1% ([FDA label — §8.6, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Liver
- Extrahepatic biliary obstruction doubles sulfoxide Cmax, increases AUC 7-fold, and prolongs half-life to 31.7 h ([FDA label — §8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Baseline liver enzyme elevations increase hepatotoxicity and bone marrow suppression risk; monitor closely ([FDA label — §5.1, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Common side effects
- Elevated liver enzymes (16% in hydatid), headache (11% in neurocysticercosis), abdominal pain, nausea, vomiting ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Dizziness, vertigo, fever, and reversible alopecia ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Serious side effects
- Fatal bone marrow suppression: granulocytopenia, agranulocytosis, pancytopenia, aplastic anaemia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia ([FDA label — §5.1, §6.1, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Hepatitis, acute liver failure, raised intracranial pressure, seizures, retinal damage in ocular cysticercosis ([FDA label — §5.3, §5.4, §5.5, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Stevens–Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, acute renal failure, and rhabdomyolysis (postmarketing) ([FDA label — §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Poor oral absorption; fatty meal raises sulfoxide plasma levels up to 5-fold; Tmax 2–5 h. - **Distribution:** Sulfoxide 70% protein-bound; enters bile, liver, cyst fluid, and CSF. - **Metabolism:** Rapid hepatic conversion to active albendazole sulfoxide, then inactive sulfone; auto-induces metabolism. - **Elimination:** Primarily biliary; <1% urinary excretion of sulfoxide. - **Half-life:** 8–12 h (albendazole sulfoxide).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)

### Interactions
- Dexamethasone raises steady-state albendazole sulfoxide trough levels by ~56% ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Praziquantel (with food) increases albendazole sulfoxide Cmax and AUC by ~50% ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Cimetidine doubles albendazole sulfoxide concentrations in bile and hydatid cyst fluid ([FDA label — §7.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).
- Theophylline: albendazole induces CYP1A; monitor plasma theophylline levels during and after therapy ([FDA label — §7.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Pregnancy
- India/WHO (STH deworming): avoid in 1st trimester; give single 400 mg dose in 2nd (or 3rd) trimester ([MoHFW Pregnancy Deworming — p. 6](https://nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2021-03/Guidelines%20for%20Deworming%20in%20Pregnancy.pdf), [WHO STH — 2017](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK487918/)).
- India (lymphatic filariasis MDA): exclude pregnant women ([NCVBDC ELF — p. 18](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).
- FDA (systemic/prolonged therapy): teratogenic in animals; avoid in pregnancy, use contraception during and 1 month after therapy ([FDA label — §5.2, §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

### Breastfeeding
- Minimally excreted in human milk; single 400 mg dose acceptable (exclude LF IDA within 1 week postpartum) ([CDC Echinococcosis — 2024](https://www.cdc.gov/echinococcosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html), [NCVBDC ELF — p. 18](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).
- FDA: excreted in animal milk; exercise caution in nursing mothers ([FDA label — §8.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=62a3f663-9e76-47f6-b089-54e4db01f697)).

- **Class:** Macrocyclic lactone (avermectin) antiparasitic
- **Uses:** Strongyloidiasis, scabies, onchocerciasis, and lymphatic filariasis mass drug administration

- Monitor INR closely if co-administered with warfarin ([FDA label — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).

### Kidney
- Renal excretion <1%; dose adjustment unlikely, though renal-impairment PK studies are lacking ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display), [Mathachan 2021 — Renal Failure](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354388/)).
- Exclude bedridden patients with kidney failure from IDA MDA ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Liver
- Extensively hepatically metabolised; no formal hepatic-impairment PK data, so use caution ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display), [Mathachan 2021 — Hepatic Impairment](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354388/)).
- Exclude bedridden patients with liver failure from IDA MDA ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Common side effects
- Dizziness, pruritus, diarrhoea, nausea, fatigue, abdominal pain, and mild transaminase elevation ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).
- Onchocerciasis (Mazzotti): pruritus, rash, fever, arthralgia, lymphadenopathy, peripheral oedema, tachycardia, headache, myalgia ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).

### Serious side effects
- Fatal encephalopathy in heavy *Loa loa* co-infection (confusion, lethargy, seizures, coma) ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).
- Neurotoxicity (somnolence, confusion, stupor, coma, death) reported even without *Loa loa* ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).
- Severe Mazzotti reaction, orthostatic hypotension, Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, hepatitis, seizures ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Oral Tmax ~4 h; high-fat meal increases bioavailability ~2.5-fold. - **Distribution:** ~93.2% plasma protein-bound; poor CNS entry via P-gp. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic, mainly CYP3A4 (minor CYP2D6, CYP2E1). - **Elimination:** Almost exclusively faecal over ~12 days; <1% urinary. - **Half-life:** ~18 h.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display), [Mathachan 2021 — Pharmacokinetics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354388/)

### Interactions
- Warfarin: rare elevated INR; monitor coagulation closely ([FDA label — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).
- Azithromycin increases ivermectin AUC by 31% and Cmax by 27% ([Mathachan 2021 — Pharmacokinetics](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354388/)).
- Alcohol increases exposure; orange juice reduces absorption; potentiates benzodiazepines and valproate ([Mathachan 2021 — Table 5](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8354388/)).

### Pregnancy
- Avoid in pregnancy; animal teratogenicity shown and human safety not established ([FDA label — Pregnancy](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display), [ICMR STW — Scabies](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725967365_scabies.pdf)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in milk at low levels; use only if maternal benefit outweighs infant risk ([FDA label — Nursing Mothers](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=681888c9-af79-4b7d-ae80-c3f4f6f1effd&type=display)).
- Postpartum IDA MDA: exclude within 1 week of birth; eligible after 1 week ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

- **Dose:** Diethylcarbamazine — tropical pulmonary eosinophilia | 6–6 mg/kg per day, in 3 divided doses after meals | max — | PO × 14–21 days ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html))
- MDA age-banded single dose: 2–5 years: 100 mg; 6–14 years: 200 mg; ≥15 years: 300 mg ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.6](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Before starting
- Exclude onchocerciasis in exposed patients; DEC is contraindicated due to severe Mazzotti reaction ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html)).
- Check *Loa loa* microfilaraemia if exposed; extreme caution/avoid if >8,000 mf/mL ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html)).
- Exclude pregnancy, infants, hypersensitivity, cardiac or renal disease, and acute severe illness ([Hetrazan IP — §4.3, §4.4](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804), [NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Monitoring
- Repeat night blood smear every 6 months for 2 years until microfilaria-negative ([NCVBDC ELF — §6.5](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).
- Watch for allergic, neurologic, and renal reactions in heavy microfilaraemia ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html), [Hetrazan IP — §4.4](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).

### Kidney
- Reduce dose in renal impairment or sustained alkaline urine; avoid in severe renal dysfunction ([Hetrazan IP — §4.2, §4.3](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804), [NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Liver
- Exclude bedridden patients with liver failure from MDA; no specific hepatic dose cut-offs verified ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Common side effects
- Dizziness, nausea, vomiting, fever, headache, anorexia, weakness, lethargy, myalgia, arthralgia ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html), [NCVBDC ELF — §4.3.7](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf), [Hetrazan IP — §4.8](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).
- Localised pruritus, lymph node enlargement, and inflammatory reaction around dying worms ([NCVBDC ELF — §4.3.7](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf), [Hetrazan IP — §4.8](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).

### Serious side effects
- Severe Mazzotti reaction in onchocerciasis with severe skin and ocular inflammation ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html)).
- Fatal encephalopathy and renal failure in *Loa loa* microfilaraemia >8,000 mf/mL ([CDC LF — Clinical Treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/filarial-worms/hcp/clinical-care/index.html), [Hetrazan IP — §4.4](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).
- Urticaria, asthma exacerbation in asthmatics, and reversible proteinuria ([Hetrazan IP — §4.8](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Readily absorbed orally; Tlag 0.74 h, Cmax ~1,550 ng/mL (6 mg/kg). - **Distribution:** Widely distributed; V 3–5 L/kg (pop-PK V/F 103.3 L). - **Metabolism:** Rapid, extensive; active metabolite DEC-N-oxide. - **Elimination:** >50% unchanged in acidic urine (reduced in alkaline urine); CL/F 8.63 L/h. - **Half-life:** 4–12 h.
- **Citations:** [Hetrazan IP — §5.2](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804), [Bala 2021 — Results](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8448115/)

### Interactions
- Urinary alkalinisers increase plasma DEC levels, prolong half-life, and heighten toxicity ([Hetrazan IP — §5.2](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).
- No significant PK interaction with co-administered ivermectin or albendazole ([Bala 2021 — Discussion](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8448115/)).

### Pregnancy
- Contraindicated in pregnancy; delay treatment until after delivery ([Hetrazan IP — §4.3, §4.6](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804), [NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

### Breastfeeding
- Breast milk excretion unknown ([Hetrazan IP — §4.6](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14804)).
- Postpartum MDA: eligible beyond 1 week after delivery; excluded within first week for IDA ([NCVBDC ELF — Table 4.1](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Guidelines/Fil/ELF-Guideline-2024.pdf)).

- **Aliases:** `Distocide`, `Biltricide`, `Cysticide`

### Dose

- Taeniasis (*T. solium*, *T. saginata*): 5–10 mg/kg PO single dose; 10 mg/kg preferred ([StatPearls — Praziquantel](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544251/), [WHO taeniasis — Rec 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK587135/)).
- Parenchymal neurocysticercosis: 50 mg/kg/day PO in 3 divided doses × 10–14 days with corticosteroids ([WHO EML app — NCC](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/2021-eml-expert-committee/applications-for-new-indications-for-existing-listed-medicines/i.1_albendazole-mebendazole-praziquantel.pdf), [WHO NCC — PICO 2–3](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK573846/)).
- Neurocysticercosis (>2 viable parenchymal cysts): combine with albendazole 15 mg/kg/day × 10–14 days ([IDSA NCC — Recs](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6248812/)).
- Take tablets with water during meals; do not chew (bitter taste causes gagging/vomiting) ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- 600 mg scored tablets split into four 150 mg segments; India NLEM lists 600 mg tablet ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9), [NLEM 2022 — §6.1](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf)).

### Children
- Approved from ≥1 year; safety under 1 year not established ([FDA label — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Age <6 years: may crush/disintegrate tablet in semi-solid food or liquid; use within 1 h ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- **Dose:** Praziquantel — schistosomiasis (≥1 yr, FDA) | 20–20 mg/kg per dose PO TID (4–6 h apart) × 1 day | max — | with meals ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9))
- **Dose:** Praziquantel — schistosomiasis (≥2 yr, WHO) | 40–40 mg/kg per dose PO single dose | max — | with meals ([Schistosomiasis review — Intro](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10731898/))
- **Dose:** Praziquantel — clonorchiasis/opisthorchiasis (≥1 yr) | 25–25 mg/kg per dose PO TID (4–6 h apart) × 1 day | max — | with meals ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9))
- **Dose:** Praziquantel — taeniasis | 5–10 mg/kg per dose PO single dose | max — | 10 mg/kg preferred ([StatPearls — Praziquantel](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK544251/))
- **Dose:** Praziquantel — neurocysticercosis | 50–50 mg/kg per day, in 3 divided doses × 10–14 days | max — | PO with corticosteroids ([WHO EML app — NCC](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/2021-eml-expert-committee/applications-for-new-indications-for-existing-listed-medicines/i.1_albendazole-mebendazole-praziquantel.pdf))

### Before starting

- Hospitalise for duration of treatment if cerebral cysticercosis coexists ([FDA label — §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor cardiac rhythm in patients with pre-existing arrhythmias ([FDA label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Monitor for neurological deterioration (seizures, raised ICP) during neurocysticercosis or acute schistosomiasis treatment ([FDA label — §5.1, §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Monitor closely for toxicity in moderate-to-severe hepatic impairment (Child-Pugh B or C) ([FDA label — §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Kidney
- No dose adjustment required; unchanged drug does not accumulate ([FDA label — §8.7, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Liver
- Child-Pugh A: no dose adjustment needed ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Child-Pugh B/C: AUC rises 3.55× (B) and 15× (C); monitor closely for adverse reactions ([FDA label — §8.6, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Common side effects
- Malaise, headache, dizziness, abdominal discomfort, nausea, fever, urticaria; worse with heavy worm burden ([FDA label — §6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Vomiting, abdominal pain, somnolence, fatigue, anorexia, myalgia, pruritus, rash ([FDA label — §6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Serious side effects
- Paradoxical clinical deterioration in acute schistosomiasis: respiratory failure, encephalopathy, papilloedema, cerebral vasculitis ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Cardiac arrhythmias: bradycardia, ectopic rhythms, AV block, ventricular fibrillation ([FDA label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Seizures, anaphylaxis, Stevens–Johnson syndrome, hepatitis, pneumonitis, bloody diarrhoea ([FDA label — §6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** 80% oral; Tmax 1–3 h; food increases AUC 2.7–4×. - **Distribution:** ~80% albumin-bound; CSF 14–20% of plasma; enters breast milk. - **Metabolism:** Rapid hepatic CYP450 with high first-pass effect. - **Elimination:** 80% renal (>99% metabolites); 15–35% biliary/faecal. - **Half-life:** 0.8–1.5 h (up to 8.5 h in Child-Pugh C).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9), [PK review — Metabolism](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5404606/), [Trematode review — PK](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3780935/)

### Interactions
- Strong CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin): contraindicated; plasma praziquantel becomes undetectable ([FDA label — §4, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Moderate CYP3A4 inducers (efavirenz): avoid; reduces AUC by 77% and Cmax by 79% ([FDA label — §5.6, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- CYP450 inhibitors (cimetidine, ketoconazole, itraconazole, erythromycin, ritonavir) and grapefruit juice (AUC ↑ 1.9×) increase exposure ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Corticosteroids (dexamethasone) decrease plasma praziquantel levels, though co-administration is needed in neurocysticercosis ([CDC — Cysticercosis](https://www.cdc.gov/cysticercosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html)).

### Pregnancy
- Human studies show no increased birth defects, miscarriage, or adverse maternal/fetal outcomes ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).
- Permitted during pregnancy when clinically indicated or in schistosomiasis control programmes ([CDC — Cysticercosis](https://www.cdc.gov/cysticercosis/hcp/clinical-care/index.html)).

### Breastfeeding
- Present in human milk at low concentrations; weigh maternal benefit against minimal infant risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=34ce1cdd-648e-4f1e-8512-bf3d4cc22eb9)).

## Drug: quinine
- **Name:** Quinine
- **Aliases:** `quinine sulfate`, `quinine dihydrochloride`
- **Class:** Antimalarial (cinchona alkaloid)
- **Uses:** Severe *P. falciparum* malaria (if artesunate unavailable) and uncomplicated *P. falciparum* in early pregnancy
- **Clearance:** Hepatic, Renal

### Boxed warning
- Do NOT use for nocturnal leg cramps; causes life-threatening thrombocytopenia, HUS/TTP, and chronic renal impairment ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Dose
- Salt conversion: 324 mg quinine sulfate = 269 mg base; 10 mg dihydrochloride salt = 8.3 mg base ([FDA label — §11](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- Uncomplicated *P. falciparum* (1st trimester): India: quinine salt 10 mg/kg PO TID × 7 days; WHO: artemether-lumefantrine preferred, quinine + clindamycin alternative ([NCVBDC 2013 — p. 9](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Diagnosis-Treatment-Malaria-2013.pdf), [WHO evidence — p. 1](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/da6fe026-0df4-406a-a3fb-787a576d4158/content)).
- Uncomplicated *P. falciparum* (adults ≥16 yr): quinine sulfate 648 mg PO q8h × 7 days with food ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Severe malaria (IV, if artesunate unavailable): loading 20 mg dihydrochloride salt/kg in 5% dextrose over 4 h ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- Severe malaria maintenance: 10 mg salt/kg IV q8h (max rate 5 mg/kg/h); NEVER give IV bolus ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf)).
- Omit loading dose if quinine or mefloquine was received within 24 h ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- If IV therapy continues >48 h, reduce maintenance to 7 mg salt/kg q8h ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf)).
- Give parenteral therapy ≥24 h, then switch to oral ACT or oral quinine + doxycycline/clindamycin (total 7 days) ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf), [NCVBDC 2013 — p. 17–18](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Diagnosis-Treatment-Malaria-2013.pdf)).
- India NLEM lists 300 mg/mL injection; WHO eEML lists 300 mg/mL and 60 mg/mL IV infusion ([NLEM 2022 — §6.10](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf), [WHO eEML — Quinine](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/381)).

### Children
- Oral capsule safety/efficacy under 16 years not established on US label ([FDA label — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Severe malaria (all ages): IV loading 20 mg salt/kg over 4 h, then 10 mg salt/kg q8h; max rate 5 mg/kg/h ([NCVBDC 2014 — §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf)).
- Oral step-down (<8 yr): quinine 10 mg salt/kg PO TID + clindamycin 10 mg/kg q12h (total 7 days); avoid doxycycline ([NCVBDC 2013 — p. 17–18](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/Doc/Diagnosis-Treatment-Malaria-2013.pdf)).

### Before starting
- Contraindicated in prolonged QT interval, G6PD deficiency, myasthenia gravis, or optic neuritis ([FDA label — §4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Contraindicated in prior hypersensitivity to quinine, mefloquine, or quinidine, or history of blackwater fever/ITP/TTP/HUS ([FDA label — §4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor blood glucose closely (especially pregnancy and severe malaria) for hyperinsulinaemic hypoglycaemia ([FDA label — §5.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- Monitor ECG (QTc, PR, QRS) and electrolytes (potassium); stop if severe arrhythmia develops ([FDA label — §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- Watch for bleeding/petechiae (thrombocytopenia), dark urine (haemolysis/blackwater fever), and hypersensitivity ([FDA label — §5.2, §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Kidney
- Severe chronic renal failure (oral): 648 mg loading, then 324 mg q12h × 7 days; mild/moderate unstudied ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Severe malaria with acute renal failure (IV): reduce maintenance by one-third after 48 h (not needed on RRT) ([ISCCM Malaria — Severe](https://ijccm.org/abstractArticleContentBrowse/IJCCM/36774/JPJ/fullText)).
- Minimally removed by haemodialysis (~6.5%/h) or haemofiltration ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Liver
- Child-Pugh A/B: no dose adjustment, but monitor closely for toxicity (AUC ↑ 55% in Child-Pugh B) ([FDA label — §2.3, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Child-Pugh C: do not administer; use alternative antimalarial ([FDA label — §2.3, §8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Common side effects
- Cinchonism (nearly universal): tinnitus, hearing impairment, headache, dizziness/vertigo, flushing, sweating, nausea, blurred/colour vision ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Vomiting, diarrhoea, abdominal pain, gastric irritation ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Serious side effects
- Severe hypoglycaemia (insulin release), especially in pregnancy and severe malaria ([FDA label — §5.8, §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- QT/PR/QRS prolongation, torsades de pointes, ventricular fibrillation, AV block, lethal hypotension (with bolus) ([FDA label — §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0), [Malaria review — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3499999/)).
- Immune-mediated thrombocytopenia, HUS/TTP, G6PD haemolysis, blackwater fever, DIC, agranulocytosis ([FDA label — §5.2, §5.6, §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Deafness, blindness, optic neuritis, anaphylaxis, Stevens–Johnson syndrome/TEN, granulomatous hepatitis, acute interstitial nephritis ([FDA label — §5.6, §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** 76–88% oral; Tmax 2.8 h (5.9 h in malaria). - **Distribution:** Vd/F 2.5–7.1 L/kg; 69–92% protein-bound (78–95% in malaria); CSF 2–7%. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic CYP3A4 (major) to 3-hydroxyquinine; minor CYP1A2/2C/2D6/2E1. - **Elimination:** Primarily hepatic; ~20% unchanged in urine (slower in alkaline urine). - **Half-life:** 9.7–12.5 h (prolonged in malaria; 26 h in severe renal failure).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)

### Interactions
- Avoid QT-prolonging drugs (Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, halofantrine, mefloquine, pimozide); mefloquine also raises seizure risk ([FDA label — §5.3, §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Avoid rifampicin (↓ AUC 75–85%, treatment failure), ritonavir (↑ AUC/Cmax 4×), erythromycin/macrolides, and Al/Mg antacids ([FDA label — §5.3, §5.4, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Avoid neuromuscular blockers (pancuronium, succinylcholine); potentiates blockade causing respiratory depression/apnoea ([FDA label — §5.5, §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Monitor digoxin (↑ AUC 33%), warfarin/INR, carbamazepine/phenobarbital (↑ AUC 104%/81%), CYP3A4 statins (rhabdomyolysis), CYP2D6 substrates ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Cimetidine (↑ AUC 42%) and tetracycline (↑ levels 2×) increase quinine exposure; monitor closely ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Pregnancy
- India: preferred in 1st trimester uncomplicated and severe *P. falciparum* malaria; WHO: artemether-lumefantrine/artesunate preferred ([NCVBDC 2014 — §4.3, §6.4](https://ncvbdc.mohfw.gov.in/WriteReadData/l892s/20627628441542176662.pdf), [WHO evidence — p. 1](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/da6fe026-0df4-406a-a3fb-787a576d4158/content)).
- Crosses placenta (cord:maternal 0.32); >1,000 exposures show no increased teratogenicity, but high maternal hypoglycaemia risk ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).
- Does not induce uterine contractions at antimalarial doses ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in milk (<2–3 mg/day; <0.4% maternal dose); generally compatible, use with caution ([FDA label — §8.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=1f32f05c-a215-40be-b951-e41a7b54a8a0)).

## Possible issues
- **Diethylcarbamazine (DEC) US FDA status:** DEC is not commercially FDA-approved in the US (available only via CDC IND); dosing, safety, and PK were verified from India NCVBDC 2024, CDC 2024, WHO eEML, Pfizer India `Hetrazan` IP (`Paper`), and population-PK literature. Note Pfizer's older `Hetrazan` IP lists legacy regimens superseded by current NCVBDC/CDC 6 mg/kg/day dosing.
- **India vs WHO/FDA divergences flagged inline:** `fluconazole` (mucosal/invasive/cryptococcal doses), `amphotericin-b` (India NCVBDC single-dose 10 mg/kg liposomal kala-azar vs FDA 7-dose regimen; WHO single-dose liposomal cryptococcal induction vs NACO deoxycholate), `aciclovir`/`valaciclovir` (NACO 2024 genital HSV regimens and pregnancy avoidance of valaciclovir; ICMR 14–21 d HSV encephalitis vs FDA 10 d), `oseltamivir` (NCDC Category B/C empirical therapy vs WHO 2024 severe-only; infant age-banded vs 3 mg/kg), `tenofovir-disoproxil` & `entecavir` (NVHCP 2019 HBV MTCT & lamivudine-refractory preferences vs WHO 2024/FDA; NACO breastfeeding with ART vs FDA), `albendazole` (2nd/3rd-trimester STH deworming vs FDA systemic avoidance), `ivermectin` (scabies food timing and off-label FDA status), and `quinine` (India NCVBDC 1st-trimester preference vs WHO 2022 artemether-lumefantrine/artesunate preference).
- **Paediatric formulation availability in India:** FDA paediatric `terbinafine` oral granules (tinea capitis) and `entecavir` 0.05 mg/mL oral solution are not widely marketed in India; confirm local availability before prescribing.
- **Missing organ cut-offs & brand verifications:** `terbinafine` (CrCl ≤50 mL/min clearance drops ~50% without a numeric dose adjustment), `ivermectin`, and `diethylcarbamazine` lack quantitative CrCl/Child-Pugh dosing tables in primary labels.
