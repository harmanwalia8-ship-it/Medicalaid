# Medicalaid — Batch D3: Quinolones, Macrolides, and Other Oral Agents

## Drug: ciprofloxacin
- **Name:** Ciprofloxacin
- **Aliases:** `Ciplox`, `Cifran`, `Ciprobid`
- **Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial (WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Complicated UTI, pyelonephritis, intra-abdominal, bone/joint, skin, infectious diarrhoea, prostatitis, anthrax, plague
- **Clearance:** Renal, Hepatic, Biliary

### Boxed warning
- Associated with disabling, potentially irreversible reactions that can co-occur: tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Stop immediately and avoid all fluoroquinolones if any serious reaction occurs ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- May worsen myasthenia gravis muscle weakness; avoid in known history ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Reserve for patients without alternatives in acute uncomplicated cystitis, acute sinusitis, and AECB ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Dose
- Essential Watch-group antibacterial available as 250 mg and 500 mg tablets in India ([NLEM 2022 — §6.2.2.3](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf), [WHO eEML — Watch](https://list.essentialmeds.org/antibiotics/WATCH)).
- UTI: 250–500 mg PO q12h or 200–400 mg IV q8–12h for 7–14 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Acute uncomplicated cystitis (reserve only): 250 mg PO q12h for 3 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Infectious diarrhoea: 500 mg PO q12h — India: 3 days; FDA: 5–7 days ([ICMR — 2019](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf), [FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Complicated intra-abdominal (with metronidazole): 400 mg IV q12h — India: 750 mg PO q12h; FDA: 500 mg PO q12h ([ICMR — 2019](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf), [FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Skin/soft-tissue or lower respiratory tract: 500–750 mg PO q12h or 400 mg IV q8–12h for 7–14 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Bone and joint: 500–750 mg PO q12h or 400 mg IV q8–12h for 4–8 weeks ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Chronic bacterial prostatitis: 500 mg PO or 400 mg IV q12h for 28 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Nosocomial pneumonia: 400 mg IV q8h for 10–14 days ([FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Typhoid fever (susceptible isolates): 500 mg PO q12h for 10 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Inhalational anthrax PEP: 500 mg PO or 400 mg IV q12h for 60 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Plague: 500–750 mg PO q12h or 400 mg IV q8–12h for 14 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- IV-to-PO equivalents: 200 mg IV q12h = 250 mg PO q12h; 400 mg IV q12h = 500 mg PO q12h; 400 mg IV q8h = 750 mg PO q12h ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Infuse IV over 60 minutes into a large vein; avoid rapid infusion ([FDA IV label — §2.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Take PO 2 h before or 6 h after multivalent cations; avoid dairy or calcium-fortified juice alone ([FDA oral label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

- **Dose:** Ciprofloxacin — plague (birth–17 yr, IV) | 10–10 mg/kg per dose every 8–12 h | max 400 mg | IV over 60 min for 14 days ([FDA IV label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74))

- No dosing data for children with moderate-to-severe renal impairment (CrCl <50 mL/min/1.73 m²) ([FDA oral label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Before starting
- Contraindicated in quinolone hypersensitivity or concomitant tizanidine use ([FDA oral label — §4.1, §4.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Avoid in myasthenia gravis, prior fluoroquinolone tendinopathy or neuropathy, QT prolongation, or uncorrected hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia ([FDA oral label — §5.2–§5.5, §5.12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Reserve in known or high-risk aortic aneurysm only when no alternative antibacterial exists ([FDA oral label — §5.9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Use caution in epilepsy, CNS seizure risk, adults >60 years, or concurrent corticosteroid therapy ([FDA oral label — §5.2, §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Obtain culture and susceptibility; avoid empiric use where local Enterobacteriaceae quinolone resistance is high ([FDA oral label — §1.13](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [ICMR — 2017](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_guidelines_2017.pdf)).
- Not first-choice for presumed or confirmed *Streptococcus pneumoniae* pneumonia ([FDA oral label — §1.10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Monitoring
- Stop immediately for tendon pain/swelling, paraesthesias, psychiatric/CNS symptoms, rash, or hepatitis signs ([FDA oral label — §5.1–§5.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Monitor blood glucose closely in diabetic patients on insulin or oral hypoglycaemics ([FDA oral label — §5.19](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Check renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function periodically during prolonged therapy ([FDA IV label — §5.18](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).
- Ensure liberal hydration and avoid alkaline urine to prevent crystalluria ([FDA oral label — §2.4, §5.18](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Monitor PT/INR with warfarin and drug levels if theophylline or phenytoin is unavoidable ([FDA oral label — §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Kidney
- Oral, CrCl >50 mL/min: usual dose; CrCl 30–50 mL/min: 250–500 mg q12h ([FDA oral label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Oral, CrCl 5–29 mL/min: 250–500 mg q18h ([FDA oral label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Oral, haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis: 250–500 mg q24h after dialysis (<10% dialysed) ([FDA oral label — §2.3, §10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- IV, CrCl >30 mL/min: usual dose; CrCl 5–29 mL/min: 200–400 mg q18–24h ([FDA IV label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).

### Liver
- Stable chronic cirrhosis: no significant PK changes observed; acute hepatic insufficiency not studied ([FDA oral label — §8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Discontinue immediately if hepatitis symptoms or jaundice occur ([FDA oral label — §5.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Common side effects
- Nausea (2.5%), diarrhoea (1.6%), abnormal LFTs (1.3%), vomiting (1%), and rash (1%) ([FDA oral label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- IV (>1%): infusion-site reactions, CNS disturbance, headache, restlessness, and eosinophilia ([FDA IV label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)).

### Serious side effects
- Tendinitis and tendon rupture (Achilles most common; may occur months after therapy) ([FDA oral label — §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Peripheral neuropathy (sensory/sensorimotor; potentially irreversible) ([FDA oral label — §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- CNS and psychiatric effects: seizures, status epilepticus, psychosis, delirium, depression, suicidal thoughts, pseudotumor cerebri ([FDA oral label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Myasthenia gravis exacerbation and aortic aneurysm or dissection within 2 months ([FDA oral label — §5.5, §5.9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- QT prolongation and torsades de pointes ([FDA oral label — §5.12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Severe dysglycaemia (including hypoglycaemic coma/death) and severe hepatotoxicity/hepatic failure ([FDA oral label — §5.8, §5.19](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Anaphylaxis, SJS/TEN/AGEP, *Clostridioides difficile* colitis, interstitial nephritis, pancytopenia, and phototoxicity ([FDA oral label — §5.6, §5.7, §5.11, §5.14, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability ~70% (70–80% in IV label); Tmax 1–2 h; food delays peak without lowering total absorption. - **Distribution:** 20–40% protein-bound; high prostate, lung, bile, and bone levels; CSF <10% of serum peak. - **Metabolism:** Partial hepatic metabolism (~15% oral, ~10% IV as less-active metabolites); inhibits CYP1A2. - **Elimination:** 40–50% oral (50–70% IV) unchanged in urine via active tubular secretion; 20–35% oral (~15% IV) faecal. - **Half-life:** ~4 h (oral) and 5–6 h (IV) in normal renal function; 4–5 h in children.
- **Citations:** [FDA oral label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8), [FDA IV label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=f406e796-17d9-4465-b8a7-00d966a4ba74)

### Interactions
- Tizanidine: contraindicated; CYP1A2 inhibition raises tizanidine Cmax 7-fold and AUC 10-fold (severe hypotension/sedation) ([FDA oral label — §4.2, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Theophylline: avoid; CYP1A2 inhibition risks seizures, cardiac arrest, and death ([FDA oral label — §5.10, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Multivalent cations (Mg/Al antacids, iron, zinc, calcium, sucralfate, phosphate binders, didanosine): cut absorption ≤90%; space 2 h before or 6 h after ([FDA oral label — §2.4, §7, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- QT-prolonging drugs (Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, TCAs, macrolides, antipsychotics): avoid due to arrhythmia risk ([FDA oral label — §5.12, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Warfarin: enhances anticoagulation; monitor PT/INR and bleeding closely ([FDA oral label — §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Oral antidiabetics (sulfonylureas) and insulin: potentiated, potentially fatal hypoglycaemia; monitor blood glucose ([FDA oral label — §5.19, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- NSAIDs (non-aspirin): may provoke CNS stimulation and convulsions; use cautiously ([FDA oral label — §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- Duloxetine (5-fold AUC) and zolpidem: avoid; clozapine, ropinirole, sildenafil, caffeine, methotrexate, phenytoin, cyclosporine, probenecid: monitor closely ([FDA oral label — §7, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Pregnancy
- Observational human data show no identified risk of major birth defects or miscarriage, though sample sizes are limited ([FDA oral label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in human milk; breastfeeding not recommended (or pump and discard) during treatment plus 2 days after last dose ([FDA oral label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).
- For inhalational anthrax PEP, weigh breastfeeding benefits against risks; monitor infant for diarrhoea and candidiasis ([FDA oral label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c47250c2-bece-46b5-8b3b-b7c97d9005d8)).

## Drug: levofloxacin
- **Name:** Levofloxacin
- **Aliases:** `Levoflox`, `Loxof`, `Glevo`
- **Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial (L-isomer of ofloxacin; WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Drug-resistant tuberculosis, pneumonia, complicated UTI, pyelonephritis, prostatitis, skin infections, anthrax, plague
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Boxed warning
- Associated with disabling, potentially irreversible reactions that can co-occur: tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, CNS effects ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4be13bd5-0c39-43c3-9232-b1226ccd4dbc)).
- Stop immediately and avoid all fluoroquinolones if any serious reaction occurs ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4be13bd5-0c39-43c3-9232-b1226ccd4dbc)).
- May worsen myasthenia gravis muscle weakness; avoid in known history ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4be13bd5-0c39-43c3-9232-b1226ccd4dbc)).
- Reserve for patients without alternatives in uncomplicated UTI, acute bacterial exacerbation of chronic bronchitis, and acute bacterial sinusitis ([FDA oral label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4be13bd5-0c39-43c3-9232-b1226ccd4dbc)).

### Dose
- Essential Watch-group antituberculosis medicine (100 mg dispersible; 250, 500, 750 mg tablets) ([WHO eEML — Levofloxacin](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/353)).
- Adult DR-TB (longer oral regimen, PO once daily): 16–29 kg: 250 mg; 30–45 kg: 750 mg; ≥46 kg: 1000 mg ([NTEP — Longer Oral Regimen](https://ntep.in/node/2626/CP-dosages-mxdr-tb-drugs-adult-longer-oral-mxdr-tb-regimen)).
- Community-acquired pneumonia: 500 mg PO/IV q24h for 7–14 days, or 750 mg PO/IV q24h for 5 days ([FDA oral label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=4be13bd5-0c39-43c3-9232-b1226ccd4dbc), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=ba69722b-d7f2-4519-8a63-9ee91294c499)).

## Drug: ofloxacin
- **Name:** Ofloxacin
- **Aliases:** `Zanocin`, `Zenflox`, `Oflox`
- **Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial (WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Complicated UTI, prostatitis, pelvic inflammatory disease, cervicitis/urethritis, and selected respiratory or skin infections
- **Clearance:** Renal, Biliary

### Boxed warning
- Causes disabling, potentially irreversible tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, and CNS effects that can occur together ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Stop immediately and avoid all fluoroquinolones in patients who develop any of these reactions ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- May worsen muscle weakness in myasthenia gravis; avoid in patients with known myasthenia gravis ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Reserve for acute bronchitis exacerbations and uncomplicated cystitis only when no alternative treatments exist ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).

### Dose
- Complicated UTI: 200 mg PO q12h for 10 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Uncomplicated cystitis (reserve only): 200 mg PO q12h for 3 days (*E. coli*/*K. pneumoniae*) or 7 days (other pathogens) ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Prostatitis (*E. coli*): 300 mg PO q12h for 6 weeks ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Nongonococcal urethritis/cervicitis (*C. trachomatis*): 300 mg PO q12h for 7 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Acute pelvic inflammatory disease: 400 mg PO q12h for 10–14 days; add metronidazole 400 mg PO BD for 14 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b), [FOGSI GCPR — PID](https://icogonline.org/wp-content/uploads/pdf/gcpr/Acute_Pelvic_Inflammatory_Disease.pdf)).
- CAP, uncomplicated SSSI, or ABECB (reserve ABECB): 400 mg PO q12h for 10 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Maximum adult dose: 800 mg/day; take with or without food and maintain liberal hydration ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Watch-group antibacterial; systemic ofloxacin is not listed on India's NLEM 2022 or WHO EML for TB ([WHO eEML — Ofloxacin](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/231), [NLEM 2022](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf)).

- Theophylline: may raise steady-state theophylline levels and seizure risk; monitor levels ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Warfarin: may enhance anticoagulation; monitor PT/INR closely ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Corticosteroids: further increases tendinitis and tendon rupture risk, especially over age 60 ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).
- Lab interference: may cause false-positive urine opiate immunoassay screens ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).

### Pregnancy

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in breast milk at concentrations similar to plasma; decide whether to discontinue nursing or drug ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=93ed9f70-b44f-496b-bdfc-c1bd5f1c260b)).

## Drug: moxifloxacin
- **Name:** Moxifloxacin
- **Aliases:** `Moxicip`, `Moxif`, `Avelox`
- **Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial (WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Drug-resistant TB (BPaLM/MDR-TB), community-acquired pneumonia, complicated intra-abdominal and skin infections; avoid for UTI
- **Clearance:** Hepatic, Biliary, Renal

### Boxed warning
- Causes disabling, potentially irreversible tendinitis, tendon rupture, peripheral neuropathy, and CNS effects that can occur together ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Stop immediately and avoid all fluoroquinolones in patients who develop any of these reactions ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- May worsen muscle weakness in myasthenia gravis; avoid in patients with known myasthenia gravis ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Reserve for acute bacterial sinusitis and acute bronchitis exacerbations only when no alternative treatments exist ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Dose
- MDR/RR-TB (BPaLM, age ≥14 years): 400 mg PO once daily for 26 weeks (6 months); no weight bands ([NTEP — BPaLM](https://ntep.in/node/6946/bpalm-updates-management-dr-tb)).
- MDR-TB (other regimens, >14 years, ≥30 kg): standard 400 mg PO once daily; high-dose 400–800 mg/day by weight band ([WHO DR-TB — Annex 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539514/)).
- Community-acquired pneumonia: 400 mg PO or IV once daily for 7–14 days ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Complicated intra-abdominal infections: 400 mg PO or IV once daily for 5–14 days ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Skin/skin structure infections: 400 mg PO or IV once daily for 7 days (uncomplicated) or 7–21 days (complicated) ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Plague (treatment or prophylaxis): 400 mg PO or IV once daily for 10–14 days ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Acute sinusitis (10 days) and ABECB (5 days): 400 mg PO/IV once daily; reserve only if no alternatives ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- IV administration: infuse 400 mg/250 mL slowly over 60 minutes; avoid bolus; 1:1 IV-to-PO conversion ([FDA IV label — §2.1, §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c6fa4129-2f92-4179-ac5d-bb7b4a538789)).
- Avoid for UTI: achieves poor urinary concentrations and is not indicated for urinary tract infections ([PMC — UTI Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1071654/)).
- Watch-group antibacterial listed on India's NLEM 2022 and WHO EML for MDR-TB ([NLEM 2022](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf), [WHO eEML — Moxifloxacin](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/354)).

### Children
- WHO: used in MDR-TB (<15 years); FDA: safety and efficacy not established under 18 years (9.3% QT prolongation in paediatric cIAI trial) ([WHO DR-TB — Annex 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539514/), [FDA label — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- **Dose:** Moxifloxacin — MDR-TB (<15 years, <31 kg) | 10–15 mg/kg per day, once daily | max 400 mg | oral (use 10 mg/kg if <6 months) ([WHO DR-TB — Annex 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539514/))
- Weight bands (100 mg dispersible tablet): 5–6 kg: 80 mg; 7–9 kg: 150 mg; 10–15 kg: 200 mg; 16–23 kg: 300 mg; 24–30 kg: 400 mg ([WHO DR-TB — Annex 2](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK539514/)).

### Before starting
- Screen for quinolone hypersensitivity, myasthenia gravis, prior tendinopathy, peripheral neuropathy, and seizure history ([FDA label — §4, §5.1–§5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Avoid with known QT prolongation, bradycardia, acute ischaemia, uncorrected hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia, or QT-prolonging drugs ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Obtain baseline ECG and electrolytes for DR-TB/BPaLM; avoid if repeat QTcF >500 ms with normal electrolytes ([NTEP — BPaLM](https://ntep.in/node/6946/bpalm-updates-management-dr-tb)).

### Monitoring
- DR-TB/BPaLM: ECG at baseline, Day 15, Day 30, then monthly; check K, Mg, Ca if QTcF prolongs ([NTEP — BPaLM](https://ntep.in/node/6946/bpalm-updates-management-dr-tb), [IDSA TB — 2025](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/treatment-of-drug-susceptible-tb/treatment-of-drug-resistant-and-drug-susceptible-tb-2025-update/)).
- Monitor ECG in patients with liver cirrhosis due to higher QT prolongation risk ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Monitor blood glucose closely in diabetic patients taking insulin or oral hypoglycaemics ([FDA label — §5.12](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Watch for tendon pain, neuropathy, CNS/psychiatric changes, rash, jaundice, or persistent diarrhoea ([FDA label — §5.1–§5.10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Kidney
- No dosage adjustment needed in mild, moderate, severe, or end-stage renal disease, including HD and CAPD ([FDA label — §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Poorly dialysed (~9% removed by HD, ~3% by CAPD); do not use for UTI ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d), [PMC — UTI Review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1071654/)).

### Liver
- No dosage adjustment recommended for Child-Pugh Class A, B, or C hepatic impairment ([FDA label — §8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Use with caution and monitor ECG in cirrhosis because metabolic disturbances increase QT prolongation risk ([FDA label — §5.6, §8.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Common side effects
- Nausea (7%), diarrhoea (6%), headache (4%), dizziness (3%) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Vomiting (2%), constipation (2%), abdominal pain (2%), insomnia (2%), anaemia (1%), pyrexia (1%), ALT increase (1%), hypokalaemia (1%) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Serious side effects
- Higher QT prolongation risk than ofloxacin/levofloxacin (mean +6 ms PO, +10 ms Day 1 IV); rare torsades de pointes ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d), [NTEP — QT Prolongation](https://ntep.in/node/1896/CP-management-dr-tb-adr-qt-prolongation)).
- Tendinitis and tendon rupture (higher risk if age >60, on corticosteroids, or transplant recipient) ([FDA label — §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Irreversible peripheral neuropathy; seizures, psychosis, hallucinations, delirium, and suicidal ideation ([FDA label — §5.3, §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Myasthenia gravis exacerbation and increased risk of aortic aneurysm or dissection ([FDA label — §5.5, §5.9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Anaphylaxis, Stevens–Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, cholestatic hepatitis, and fatal hepatic failure ([FDA label — §5.7, §5.8, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- *C. difficile*-associated diarrhoea, severe hypoglycaemia (coma/death), and photosensitivity ([FDA label — §5.10, §5.12, §5.13](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** ~90% oral bioavailability; unaffected by high-fat meals or yogurt. - **Distribution:** 30–50% protein-bound; Vd 1.7–2.7 L/kg; high lung, sinus, and abdominal tissue levels. - **Metabolism:** ~52% via sulfate (38%, M1) and glucuronide (14%, M2) conjugation; no CYP450 involvement. - **Elimination:** ~45% unchanged (~20% urine, ~25% faeces); M1 excreted in faeces, M2 exclusively in urine. - **Half-life:** 12 ± 1.3 h; steady state reached after ≥3 days of 400 mg once daily.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)

### Interactions
- Multivalent cations (Al/Mg antacids, sucralfate, iron, zinc, didanosine): take moxifloxacin ≥4 h before or ≥8 h after ([FDA label — §2.2, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Calcium/dairy: India (BPaLM): avoid milk/calcium 2 h before and after; FDA: calcium supplement/yogurt did not reduce AUC ([NTEP — BPaLM](https://ntep.in/node/6946/bpalm-updates-management-dr-tb), [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- QT-prolonging drugs (Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, erythromycin, antipsychotics, TCAs, bedaquiline/clofazimine): avoid or monitor ECG closely in DR-TB ([FDA label — §5.6, §7.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d), [NTEP — QT Prolongation](https://ntep.in/node/1896/CP-management-dr-tb-adr-qt-prolongation)).
- NSAIDs: increased risk of CNS stimulation and convulsions ([FDA label — §7.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Antidiabetics (insulin, sulfonylureas): risk of severe hypoglycaemia or hyperglycaemia; monitor blood glucose closely ([FDA label — §7.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Warfarin: enhances anticoagulant effect; monitor PT/INR and bleeding closely ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- Corticosteroids: further increases tendinitis and tendon rupture risk, especially over age 60 ([FDA label — §5.2, §8.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).
- CYP450 drugs (theophylline, cyclosporine, itraconazole, oral contraceptives): no clinically significant PK interaction ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

### Pregnancy
- No human data; animal studies showed fetal harm at maternally toxic doses; BPaLM excluded in pregnancy ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d), [NTEP — BPaLM](https://ntep.in/node/6946/bpalm-updates-management-dr-tb)).

### Breastfeeding
- Unknown if present in human milk (detected in rat milk); weigh maternal benefit against infant risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6cd420c3-113e-4fc2-b59f-bfc7c485c14d)).

## Drug: norfloxacin
- **Name:** Norfloxacin
- **Aliases:** `Norbactin`, `Alflox`, `Norbid`
- **Class:** Fluoroquinolone antibacterial (WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Urinary tract infections, prostatitis, gonorrhoea, SBP prophylaxis in cirrhosis, and traveller's diarrhoea
- **Clearance:** Renal, Biliary, Hepatic

### Boxed warning
- Increased risk of tendinitis and tendon rupture at all ages, higher >60 years, with corticosteroids, or transplants ([FDA archived label — Boxed Warning](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- May exacerbate muscle weakness in myasthenia gravis; avoid in patients with known myasthenia gravis ([FDA archived label — Boxed Warning](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Disabling, potentially persistent peripheral neuropathy and neuropsychiatric effects, plus QT prolongation and rare torsades ([Health Canada Monograph — §3](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).

### Dose
- Take with water ≥1 hour before or ≥2 hours after meals or dairy; hydrate well ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Uncomplicated cystitis (*E. coli*, *K. pneumoniae*, *P. mirabilis*): 400 mg PO q12h for 3 days ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Uncomplicated UTI (other indicated organisms): 400 mg PO q12h for 7–10 days ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Complicated UTI: 400 mg PO q12h for 10–21 days ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Acute or chronic bacterial prostatitis: 400 mg PO q12h for 28 days ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Uncomplicated urethral or cervical gonorrhoea (susceptible strains): 800 mg PO single dose ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- SBP prophylaxis in high-risk cirrhosis (low ascitic protein, variceal bleed, prior SBP): 400 mg PO once daily ([ICMR AMRTG 2022 — SBP](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/chapter5-sbp.html)).
- Traveller's diarrhoea (adults, when fluoroquinolone appropriate): 400 mg PO twice daily for 1–3 days ([Paper — PMC1539099](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1539099/)).

### Children
- Not recommended under 18 years; safety unestablished and causes permanent articular cartilage lesions in juvenile animals ([FDA archived label — Pediatric Use](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Avoid in patients before epiphyseal closure ([Health Canada Monograph — §4.2, §7.1.3](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).

### Before starting
- Exclude quinolone allergy, prior fluoroquinolone tendinitis/tendon rupture, and myasthenia gravis ([FDA archived label — Contraindications, Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Screen for QT prolongation, hypokalaemia, bradycardia, Class IA/III antiarrhythmics, epilepsy/CNS disorders, and G6PD deficiency ([FDA archived label — Warnings, Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Assess aortic aneurysm/dissection risk factors (Marfan, Ehlers-Danlos, hypertension, elderly) and renal function ([Health Canada Monograph — §7](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).
- Check syphilis serology at diagnosis and 3 months later when treating gonorrhoea ([FDA archived label — Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Monitoring
- Stop immediately if tendon pain/swelling, neuropathy, CNS/psychiatric symptoms, rash, jaundice, or phototoxicity occur ([FDA archived label — Warnings, Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Periodically monitor renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function during prolonged therapy ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Monitor blood glucose with hypoglycaemics, INR with warfarin, and serum theophylline or cyclosporine levels ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Kidney
- CrCl >30 mL/min/1.73 m²: standard adult dose ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- CrCl ≤30 mL/min/1.73 m²: reduce to 400 mg PO once daily for indicated duration ([FDA archived label — Dosage](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Not recommended in anuric patients; not dialysable ([Health Canada Monograph — §4.2, §5](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).

### Liver
- No hepatic dose adjustment specified in label; stop immediately if jaundice or hepatitis develops ([FDA archived label — Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Common side effects
- Nausea (4.2%), headache (2.8%), dizziness (1.7%), and asthenia (1.3%) in multiple-dose trials ([FDA archived label — Adverse Reactions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Abdominal cramping, diarrhoea, heartburn, rash, and mild ALT/AST, eosinophil, or WBC abnormalities ([FDA archived label — Adverse Reactions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Serious side effects
- Tendinitis and tendon rupture (Achilles, shoulder, hand) during or months after treatment ([FDA archived label — Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Potentially irreversible peripheral neuropathy and Guillain-Barré syndrome ([FDA archived label — Warnings, Post-Marketing](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Seizures, increased intracranial pressure, hallucinations, psychosis, depression, and suicidal thoughts ([Health Canada Monograph — §7](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).
- Exacerbation of myasthenia gravis with respiratory weakness or death ([FDA archived label — Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Aortic aneurysm and aortic dissection ([Health Canada Monograph — §7](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).
- QTc prolongation and torsades de pointes ([FDA archived label — Precautions, Post-Marketing](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Severe hypoglycaemia (including coma or death) and hyperglycaemia ([Health Canada Monograph — §7](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).
- Anaphylaxis, SJS, TEN, DRESS, fatal hepatic failure, interstitial nephritis, *C. difficile* colitis, G6PD haemolysis, and phototoxicity ([FDA archived label — Warnings, Post-Marketing](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** 30–40% fasting oral absorption; Tmax ~1 h; food lowers peak serum levels ~30%. - **Distribution:** 10–15% protein-bound; concentrates in urine (≥200 mcg/mL), kidney, prostate, and bile. - **Metabolism:** Partial hepatic metabolism to 6 piperazine-ring metabolites with lesser antimicrobial potency. - **Elimination:** 26–32% unchanged + 5–8% metabolites in urine (GFR + tubular secretion); ~30% faecal; biliary. - **Half-life:** 3–4 h (normal), 4 h (elderly), 6.5 h when CrCl ≤30 mL/min/1.73 m².
- **Citations:** [FDA archived label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf), [Health Canada Monograph — §10.3](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)

### Interactions
- Multivalent cations (antacids, iron, zinc), sucralfate, and didanosine reduce absorption; separate by ≥2 hours ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- CYP1A2 inhibition raises theophylline, caffeine, clozapine, ropinirole, and tizanidine levels; monitor closely ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Warfarin: enhances anticoagulation; monitor PT/INR closely ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Glyburide/sulfonylureas: risk of severe hypoglycaemia; monitor blood glucose ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Cyclosporine: raises serum levels; monitor levels and adjust cyclosporine dose ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- NSAIDs: increased risk of CNS stimulation and seizures ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Corticosteroids: increased risk of tendinitis and tendon rupture ([FDA archived label — Warnings](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Avoid Class IA/III antiarrhythmics (QTc prolongation) and nitrofurantoin (in vitro urinary antagonism) ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).
- Probenecid diminishes urinary excretion of norfloxacin; avoid concomitant use ([FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf), [Health Canada Monograph — §9.4](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF)).

### Pregnancy
- Safety unestablished; crosses placenta; embryonic loss in monkeys at 10× human dose (no teratogenicity); use only if benefit justifies risk ([Health Canada Monograph — §7, §7.1.1](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF), [FDA archived label — Precautions](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/drugsatfda_docs/label/2013/019384s066lbl.pdf)).

### Breastfeeding
- Undetectable in milk up to 6 h after a single 200 mg dose; repeated-dose milk levels unmeasured ([Health Canada Monograph — §7.1.2](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF), [LactMed — Norfloxacin](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501054/)).
- Health Canada: exercise caution; LactMed: acceptable with infant monitoring for diarrhoea or candidiasis ([Health Canada Monograph — §7.1.2](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00080787.PDF), [LactMed — Norfloxacin](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501054/)).

## Drug: clarithromycin
- **Name:** Clarithromycin
- **Aliases:** `Claribid`, `Crixan`
- **Class:** Macrolide antibiotic (WHO AWaRe Watch)
- **Uses:** Respiratory, throat, sinus, skin infections, *H. pylori* eradication, and disseminated MAC
- **Clearance:** Hepatic, Renal

### Dose
- Take tablets with or without food ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP): 250 mg PO q12h for 7–14 days ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- India: combine with a beta-lactam for outpatient CAP with comorbidities or inpatient CAP ([ICMR AMRTG 2022 — CAP](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/chapter4-cap-treatment-guideline.html)).
- Acute bacterial exacerbation of chronic bronchitis: 250–500 mg PO q12h for 7–14 days ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Acute maxillary sinusitis: 500 mg PO q12h for 14 days ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Pharyngitis/tonsillitis: 250 mg PO q12h for 10 days; second-line for anaphylactic penicillin allergy ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6), [ICMR AMRTG — URTI](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter4-urti-table1.html)).
- Uncomplicated skin and skin structure infections: 250 mg PO q12h for 7–14 days ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- *H. pylori* triple therapy: 500 mg PO q12h + amoxicillin 1 g + lansoprazole 30 mg for 10–14 days ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6), [WHO eEML — H. pylori](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/617)).
- *H. pylori* triple therapy (omeprazole): 500 mg PO q12h + amoxicillin 1 g + omeprazole 20 mg for 10 days ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- *H. pylori* dual therapy: 500 mg PO q8h + omeprazole 40 mg once daily for 14 days ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Disseminated MAC treatment or prophylaxis: 500 mg PO q12h; combine with ethambutol for treatment ([FDA label — §2.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Listed on India's essential medicines list and WHO Watch group ([NLEM 2022](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf), [WHO eEML — Pharyngitis](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/616/print?format=pdf)).

### Children
- Approved ≥6 months for pharyngitis, CAP, sinusitis, otitis media, and skin infections; safety unestablished <6 months ([FDA label — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- **Dose:** Clarithromycin — paediatric infections (≥6 mo) | 15–15 mg/kg per day, in 2 divided doses q12h × 10 d | max 500 mg/dose | PO ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6))
- Approved ≥20 months for disseminated MAC treatment or prophylaxis; safety unstudied <20 months ([FDA label — §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- **Dose:** Clarithromycin — paediatric MAC (≥20 mo) | 7.5–7.5 mg/kg per dose q12h | max 500 mg/dose | PO ([FDA label — §2.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6))
- Second-choice option for paediatric acute pharyngitis and acute otitis media in anaphylactic penicillin allergy ([WHO eEML — Pharyngitis](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/616/print?format=pdf), [ICMR AMRTG — URTI](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter4-urti-table1.html)).

### Before starting
- Exclude macrolide hypersensitivity or prior clarithromycin-induced cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction ([FDA label — §4.1, §4.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Avoid in QT prolongation, ventricular arrhythmia, uncorrected hypokalaemia/hypomagnesaemia, bradycardia, or Class IA/III antiarrhythmics ([FDA label — §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Review all concomitant medicines for contraindicated CYP3A4 or P-gp interactions ([FDA label — §4.2–§4.7, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Weigh benefits against increased 1–10-year all-cause mortality risk in coronary artery disease ([FDA label — §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Check renal function; avoid in known or suspected clarithromycin-resistant *H. pylori* ([FDA label — §1.8, §2.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Monitoring
- Stop immediately if hepatitis signs (jaundice, dark urine, pruritus, anorexia, tender abdomen) or severe rash appear ([FDA label — §5.1, §5.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Monitor INR with warfarin, blood glucose with hypoglycaemics, and serum levels of digoxin, carbamazepine, or theophylline ([FDA label — §5.4, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Evaluate for *C. difficile*-associated diarrhoea if watery or bloody stools occur up to 2+ months post-therapy ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Kidney
- Severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min): reduce clarithromycin dose by 50% ([FDA label — §2.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Moderate impairment (CrCl 30–60 mL/min) with atazanavir or ritonavir: reduce dose by 50% ([FDA label — §2.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Severe impairment (CrCl <30 mL/min) with atazanavir or ritonavir: reduce dose by 75% ([FDA label — §2.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Serum levels are not appreciably affected by haemodialysis or peritoneal dialysis ([FDA label — §10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Liver
- No dose adjustment needed in hepatic impairment if renal function is normal ([FDA label — §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Contraindicated in patients with prior clarithromycin-associated cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction ([FDA label — §4.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Coexisting severe renal impairment: reduce dose or prolong dosing intervals ([FDA label — §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Common side effects
- Abdominal pain, diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, and dysgeusia (taste perversion) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Dyspepsia, headache, insomnia, rash, candidiasis, and abnormal liver function tests ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Serious side effects
- QT prolongation, ventricular tachycardia, and fatal torsades de pointes; elderly are more susceptible ([FDA label — §5.2, §8.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Hepatotoxicity, cholestatic or hepatocellular hepatitis, and fatal hepatic failure ([FDA label — §5.3, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Anaphylaxis, SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP, and Henoch-Schönlein purpura ([FDA label — §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- *C. difficile*-associated diarrhoea and pseudomembranous colitis ([FDA label — §5.6, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Exacerbation or new-onset myasthenia gravis ([FDA label — §5.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Acute pancreatitis, interstitial nephritis, renal failure, agranulocytosis, rhabdomyolysis, and reversible deafness ([FDA label — §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** ~50% absolute bioavailability (250 mg); Tmax 2–3 h; food does not alter extent. - **Distribution:** High tissue/intracellular levels exceeding serum (lung 8.8 vs 1.7 mcg/mL); no CSF data. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic conversion to active 14-OH clarithromycin; strong CYP3A4 and P-gp inhibitor. - **Elimination:** 20–30% unchanged in urine (40% suspension) plus 10–15% as 14-OH metabolite. - **Half-life:** 3–4 h (250 mg q12h), 5–7 h (500 mg q8–12h); 14-OH metabolite 5–9 h.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §7, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)

### Interactions
- Contraindicated: cisapride, pimozide, lurasidone, ergotamine/dihydroergotamine, lomitapide, lovastatin, and simvastatin ([FDA label — §4.2, §4.5–§4.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Colchicine: contraindicated in renal or hepatic impairment; reduce colchicine dose if organ function is normal ([FDA label — §4.4, §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Statins: suspend lovastatin/simvastatin; cap atorvastatin at 20 mg/day and pravastatin at 40 mg/day ([FDA label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Avoid QT-prolonging drugs and Class IA/III antiarrhythmics (quinidine, disopyramide, amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide) ([FDA label — §5.2, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- CYP3A4 calcium channel blockers (amlodipine, verapamil, diltiazem, nifedipine): hypotension, bradyarrhythmia, and elderly acute kidney injury ([FDA label — §5.4, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Raises levels/effects of warfarin, digoxin, carbamazepine, theophylline, cyclosporine, tacrolimus, quetiapine, sildenafil, and oral midazolam ([FDA label — §5.4, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Oral hypoglycaemics and insulin: increased risk of significant hypoglycaemia; monitor blood glucose ([FDA label — §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- Atazanavir: halve clarithromycin dose; do not exceed 1000 mg/day with protease inhibitors ([FDA label — §2.7, §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).
- CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, rifabutin, efavirenz, nevirapine) lower clarithromycin levels; separate zidovudine by ≥2 hours ([FDA label — §7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Pregnancy
- Not recommended unless no alternative is appropriate; animal studies show cardiovascular and cleft-palate malformations ([FDA label — §5.7, §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in milk at <2% maternal weight-adjusted dose; infant adverse effects comparable to amoxicillin ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=9d5848f0-e9f0-4112-a7ca-bcc8ea2e69c6)).

## Drug: erythromycin

- **Aliases:** `erythromycin base`, `erythromycin stearate`, `erythromycin ethylsuccinate`
- **Class:** Macrolide antibiotic (WHO AWaRe Watch)

- Chlamydia in pregnancy: 500 mg base PO q6h × 7 days (or 250 mg q6h × 14 days if intolerant) ([FDA base label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=383f9011-a3e0-4d7d-b058-5f8ab319dc8c&type=display)).

### Children

- **Dose:** Erythromycin — neonatal chlamydial conjunctivitis/pneumonia | 50–50 mg/kg per day, in 4 divided doses | max — | PO for 14–21 days ([FDA base label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=383f9011-a3e0-4d7d-b058-5f8ab319dc8c&type=display))

### Before starting

### Kidney

- Reversible hearing loss occurs mainly in renal insufficiency, high doses, and elderly patients ([FDA base label — Adverse Reactions, Geriatric Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=383f9011-a3e0-4d7d-b058-5f8ab319dc8c&type=display)).

### Liver

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Readily absorbed orally with interindividual variation; fasting optimal for base/stearate, ethylsuccinate unaffected by food. - **Distribution:** Largely plasma-protein bound; diffuses into body fluids; low CSF levels unless inflamed; crosses placenta, enters milk. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic demethylation via CYP3A4; moderate CYP3A4 inhibitor. - **Elimination:** Concentrated in liver and excreted in bile; <5% active in urine; not dialysable. - **Half-life:** 1.5–2 hours.

### Interactions
- Contraindicated: simvastatin, lovastatin (rhabdomyolysis); ergotamine, dihydroergotamine (vasospasm); cisapride, pimozide, terfenadine, astemizole (fatal arrhythmias) ([FDA base label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=383f9011-a3e0-4d7d-b058-5f8ab319dc8c&type=display)).
- Avoid Class IA/III antiarrhythmics (quinidine, procainamide, amiodarone, sotalol, dofetilide — additive QT prolongation) ([FDA base label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=383f9011-a3e0-4d7d-b058-5f8ab319dc8c&type=display)).

### Breastfeeding

## Drug: co-trimoxazole
- **Name:** Co-trimoxazole
- **Aliases:** `trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole`, `TMP-SMX`, `sulfamethoxazole + trimethoprim`
- **Class:** Sulfonamide + dihydrofolate reductase inhibitor (5:1 SMX:TMP; WHO AWaRe Access)
- **Uses:** *Pneumocystis* pneumonia treatment/prophylaxis, HIV OI prophylaxis, susceptible UTIs, shigellosis, and traveller's diarrhoea
- **Clearance:** Renal, Hepatic

### Dose
- Fixed 5:1 ratio: SS = 80 mg TMP + 400 mg SMX (480 mg total); DS = 160 mg TMP + 800 mg SMX (960 mg total) ([FDA oral label — Description](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display), [NLEM 2022](https://cdn.who.int/media/docs/default-source/essential-medicines/national-essential-medicines-lists-%28neml%29/searo_neml/india-2022.pdf)).
- HIV OI / PCP prophylaxis: 1 DS tablet (160 mg TMP / 800 mg SMX; 960 mg total) PO daily ([NTEP/NACO — CPT](https://ntep.in/node/3276/CP-co-trimoxazole-prophylactic-therapy-persons-hiv-and-tb-coinfection), [FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).
- Start HIV CPT if CD4 <350/mm³, WHO stage 3/4, HIV-TB coinfection, or post-PCP secondary prophylaxis ([NTEP/NACO — CPT](https://ntep.in/node/3276/CP-co-trimoxazole-prophylactic-therapy-persons-hiv-and-tb-coinfection)).
- PCP treatment: 15–20 mg/kg/day TMP (75–100 mg/kg/day SMX) PO q6h × 14–21 days, or IV q6–8h × 14 days ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530)).
- IV infusion: max 960 mg/day TMP; dilute in D5W and infuse over 60–90 minutes (never bolus or IM) ([FDA IV label — §2.1, §2.3–2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530)).
- Susceptible UTI: 1 DS tablet (160/800 mg) PO q12h × 10–14 days; severe UTI IV: 8–10 mg/kg/day TMP q6–12h × 14 days ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530)).
- Shigellosis or traveller's diarrhoea: 1 DS tablet (160/800 mg) PO q12h × 5 days ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).
- Ensure adequate hydration during therapy to prevent crystalluria ([FDA oral label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).

### Children
- Age cut-off: FDA: contraindicated <2 months (kernicterus); India: start HIV-exposed infant CPT from 6 weeks ([FDA IV label — §4, §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530), [NTEP/NACO — CPT](https://ntep.in/node/3276/CP-co-trimoxazole-prophylactic-therapy-persons-hiv-and-tb-coinfection)).
- UTI or acute otitis media (≥2 months): 8 mg/kg/day TMP + 40 mg/kg/day SMX PO q12h × 10 days (shigellosis: 5 days) ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).
- PCP treatment (≥2 months): 15–20 mg/kg/day TMP + 75–100 mg/kg/day SMX PO q6h × 14–21 days or IV q6–8h × 14 days ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display), [FDA IV label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530)).
- Severe HIV-associated pneumonia/PCP (<12 months): 8 mg/kg TMP + 40 mg/kg SMX IV/PO q8h × 21 days ([WHO Pocket Book — Ch. 4](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK154448/)).
- HIV CPT once daily (India): <5 kg 20/100 mg; 5–10 kg 40/200 mg; 10–15 kg 60/300 mg; 15–22 kg 80/400 mg; >22 kg 120–160 mg TMP ([NTEP/NACO — CPT Table 3](https://ntep.in/node/3276/CP-co-trimoxazole-prophylactic-therapy-persons-hiv-and-tb-coinfection)).
- Non-HIV PCP prophylaxis: 150 mg/m²/day TMP + 750 mg/m²/day SMX PO q12h × 3 consecutive days/week (max 320/1600 mg/day) ([FDA oral label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).
- Do not use for group A streptococcal pharyngitis (fails to prevent rheumatic fever) ([FDA oral label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display)).

- Screen for G6PD deficiency (haemolysis risk; use dapsone for HIV CPT in G6PD-deficient children) ([FDA oral label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9f130443-c8e7-9122-8f27-f72f07f3f29e&type=display), [NTEP/NACO — CPT](https://ntep.in/node/3276/CP-co-trimoxazole-prophylactic-therapy-persons-hiv-and-tb-coinfection)).
- Check baseline renal function, serum potassium, and CBC, especially in elderly or high-dose PCP ([FDA IV label — §5.18–5.19](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=bf7a0845-de4f-46be-841a-0faf47f8b530)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapidly absorbed orally; Tmax 1–4 h; steady state in 3 days. - **Distribution:** ~70% SMX and ~44% TMP protein-bound; enters sputum, vaginal/middle-ear fluid, placenta, and breast milk. - **Metabolism:** SMX hepatic N-acetylation, glucuronidation, and CYP2C9 oxidation (inhibits CYP2C9); TMP oxidative/glutathione metabolism (inhibits CYP2C8, OCT2). - **Elimination:** Primarily renal (filtration + tubular secretion); 0–72 h urine recovery 84.5% total SMX (30% free) and 66.8% free TMP. - **Half-life:** SMX ~10 h, TMP 8–10 h; both prolonged in severe renal impairment.

## Drug: tinidazole

- Not indicated for asymptomatic *Entamoeba histolytica* cyst passage ([FDA label — §1.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).

### Monitoring

### Kidney

- Metallic or bitter taste, nausea, dyspepsia, abdominal cramps, vomiting, anorexia, constipation ([FDA label — Highlights, §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).

- Raised transaminases; fatal acute liver failure reported with related nitroimidazoles in Cockayne syndrome ([FDA label — §6.1, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid and complete orally; Tmax 1.6 h fasted (delayed ~2 h with food, AUC unchanged). - **Distribution:** Wide tissue and CSF distribution (crosses blood-brain barrier); Vd ~50 L; protein binding 12%. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic oxidation, hydroxylation, and conjugation mainly via CYP3A4; forms 2-hydroxymethyl metabolite. - **Elimination:** Excreted by liver and kidneys; 20–25% unchanged in urine, ~12% in faeces. - **Half-life:** 12–14 h (mean 13.2 h); reduced to 4.9 h during haemodialysis.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)

### Interactions
- Alcohol / ethanol / propylene glycol: avoid during therapy and 3 days after (disulfiram-like reaction) ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Disulfiram: avoid tinidazole within 2 weeks of disulfiram (psychotic reactions reported with metronidazole) ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Warfarin / coumarins: prolongs PT/INR; monitor and adjust anticoagulant dose up to 8 days post-therapy ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Lithium: may raise serum lithium; monitor serum lithium and creatinine after several days ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Cyclosporine, tacrolimus, fluorouracil: monitor for increased toxicity of co-administered agent ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Phenytoin / fosphenytoin: may reduce IV phenytoin clearance and induce tinidazole metabolism ([FDA label — §7.1, §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- CYP3A4 inducers (rifampicin, phenobarbital) decrease tinidazole levels; inhibitors (cimetidine, ketoconazole) increase levels ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).
- Cholestyramine: separate dosing to avoid reduced absorption; oxytetracycline may antagonise effect ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).

### Pregnancy
- India/WHO: avoid in pregnancy (contraindicated in 1st trimester; use metronidazole for TV/BV); FDA: insufficient human data ([Pfizer India PI — §4.6](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14826), [WHO STI Guidelines — §3.1, §3.4](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/4c257fbc-c0a9-4016-8da7-8ba5c2409307/content), [FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in breast milk; avoid breastfeeding (or pump and discard) during treatment and for 72 hours after last dose ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=6f3ce597-64c1-4204-a67a-8b6f6e7e8bca), [Pfizer India PI — §4.6](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14826)).

## Drug: ornidazole
- **Name:** Ornidazole
- **Aliases:** `Ornida`, `Tiberal`
- **Class:** 5-nitroimidazole antimicrobial and antiprotozoal
- **Uses:** Amoebiasis, giardiasis, trichomoniasis, bacterial vaginosis, systemic anaerobic infections, surgical prophylaxis

### Dose
- Always take oral tablets after meals ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).

- Severe amoebic dysentery or liver abscess (IV): 0.5–1 g initial, then 500 mg IV q12h × 3–6 days ([TMDA SmPC — §4.2](https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/1678428948-T20H0511SmPCv1.pdf)).
- Giardiasis: 1.5 g PO once daily in the evening for 1–2 days ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Acute trichomoniasis: 1.5 g PO single dose in evening; treat sexual partner identically ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [WHO STI Guidelines — §3.1](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/4c257fbc-c0a9-4016-8da7-8ba5c2409307/content)).
- Chronic trichomoniasis: 500 mg PO twice daily for 5 days ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Systemic anaerobic infections (IV): 0.5–1 g initial, then 500 mg IV q12h × 5–10 days; switch to PO early ([TMDA SmPC — §4.2](https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/1678428948-T20H0511SmPCv1.pdf)).
- Surgical prophylaxis (oral): 1.5 g PO 12 hours pre-op, then 500 mg q12h for 3–5 days post-op ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Surgical prophylaxis (IV): 1 g IV infused over 15–30 minutes about 30 minutes pre-op ([TMDA SmPC — §4.2](https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/1678428948-T20H0511SmPCv1.pdf)).
- Widely marketed in India as 500 mg tablets ([Aristo Pharma India — Ornida](https://www.aristopharma.co.in/product/ornida-500mg/)).

- Leukopenia, hepatitis, jaundice, abnormal liver function tests ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.8](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid oral absorption; mean bioavailability ~90%; peak plasma concentration within 3 h. - **Distribution:** Vd ~1 L/kg (IV); plasma protein binding ~13%; penetrates CSF, tissues, and body fluids. - **Metabolism:** Hepatic metabolism to 2-hydroxymethyl and α-hydroxymethyl metabolites (less active than parent). - **Elimination:** 85% eliminated within 5 days mainly as metabolites in urine; 4% unchanged in urine. - **Half-life:** ~13 h (11–14 h); prolonged to ~22 h in severe cirrhosis.
- **Citations:** [Medsafe Data Sheet — §5.2](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [Merdjan 1985 — Abstract](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC1463717/), [Taburet 1986 — Abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/3742940/)

### Interactions
- Alcohol: avoid during therapy and at least 3 days after (disulfiram-like reaction reported) ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [Turk J Emerg Med 2026 — Case](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13171132/)).
- Coumarin anticoagulants (warfarin): potentiates anticoagulant effect; monitor INR more frequently ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Lithium: may elevate plasma lithium; monitor serum lithium levels more frequently ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- 5-fluorouracil: reduces fluorouracil clearance, increasing toxicity risk ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Vecuronium bromide: prolongs muscle relaxant effect ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf)).
- Cimetidine prolongs ornidazole half-life; phenobarbital, phenytoin, and rifampicin reduce half-life ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.5](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [TMDA SmPC — §4.5](https://www.tmda.go.tz/uploads/1678428948-T20H0511SmPCv1.pdf)).

### Pregnancy
- Avoid during pregnancy, especially early pregnancy, unless clearly necessary; no controlled human studies ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.6](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [WHO STI Guidelines — §3.1](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/4c257fbc-c0a9-4016-8da7-8ba5c2409307/content)).

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in low amounts in breast milk; avoid breastfeeding for 3 days after a single dose ([LactMed — Ornidazole](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598881/)).
- Weigh maternal benefit against infant risk for multi-day courses; consider topical alternatives for BV ([Medsafe Data Sheet — §4.6](https://www.medsafe.govt.nz/profs/datasheet/a/arrowornidazoletab.pdf), [LactMed — Ornidazole](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK598881/)).

## Drug: cefadroxil
- **Name:** Cefadroxil
- **Aliases:** `cefadroxil monohydrate`, `Odoxil`, `Droxyl`
- **Class:** First-generation oral cephalosporin antibiotic (WHO AWaRe Access)
- **Uses:** Streptococcal pharyngitis, uncomplicated urinary tract, and skin and soft tissue infections
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- Uncomplicated lower UTI (cystitis): 1–2 g/day orally once daily or in 2 divided doses ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Other urinary tract infections: 2 g/day orally in 2 divided doses ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Skin and skin structure infections: 1 g/day orally once daily or in 2 divided doses ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Group A streptococcal pharyngitis/tonsillitis (non-anaphylactic penicillin allergy): 1 g/day orally once or twice daily for 10 days ([ICMR AMRTG — URTI](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter4-urti-table1.html), [FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Take without regard to meals; taking with food may reduce gastrointestinal upset ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Children
- **Dose:** Cefadroxil — GAS pharyngitis/tonsillitis | 30–30 mg/kg per day, once daily or in 2 divided doses q12h | max 1 g/day | oral × 10 days ([IDSA — Table 2](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7108032/), [FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display))
- **Dose:** Cefadroxil — impetigo | 30–30 mg/kg per day, once daily or in 2 divided doses q12h | max — | oral ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display))
- **Dose:** Cefadroxil — UTI or other skin/soft tissue infections | 30–30 mg/kg per day, in 2 divided doses q12h | max — | oral ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display))
- Oral suspension weight dosing begins at 4.5 kg; refrigerate after reconstitution and discard after 14 days ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Before starting
- Contraindicated in known cephalosporin allergy; avoid in immediate/anaphylactic penicillin allergy ([FDA label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [ICMR AMRTG — URTI](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter4-urti-table1.html)).
- Check prior beta-lactam allergy history; cross-sensitivity occurs in up to 10% of penicillin-allergic patients ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Use cautiously in colitis/GI disease history or markedly impaired renal function (CrCl <50 mL/min/1.73 m²) ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Confirm bacterial infection and obtain cultures/susceptibility where indicated; stewardship Access group medicine ([FDA label — Indications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [WHO eEML — Cefadroxil](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/685)).

### Monitoring
- Monitor renal function before and during therapy in known/suspected renal impairment or elderly patients ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Watch for watery or bloody diarrhoea during therapy and for over 2 months afterward ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Monitor for superinfection with nonsusceptible organisms during prolonged therapy ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Kidney
- CrCl >50 mL/min/1.73 m²: standard adult dosing; no adjustment required ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- CrCl ≤50 mL/min/1.73 m²: give 1000 mg initial dose, then 500 mg at extended intervals ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- CrCl 25–50 mL/min: 500 mg q12h; 10–25 mL/min: q24h; 0–10 mL/min: q36h ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Haemodialysis removes an average of 63% of a 1 g oral dose over 6–8 hours ([FDA label — Overdosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Unadjusted dosing in renal impairment increases cephalosporin seizure risk ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Liver
- No hepatic dose adjustment is listed; cefadroxil is minimally metabolised ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [Tan et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8733657/)).
- Rare idiosyncratic cholestatic liver injury and transient transaminase elevations can occur; discontinue if hepatotoxicity develops ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [LiverTox — Oral Cephalosporins](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548358/)).

### Common side effects
- Diarrhoea; dyspepsia, nausea, and vomiting occur rarely ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Rash, urticaria, and pruritus ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Genital pruritus, genital candidiasis (moniliasis), and vaginitis ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Transient fever and mild serum transaminase elevations ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [LiverTox — Oral Cephalosporins](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK548358/)).

### Serious side effects
- Anaphylaxis, angioedema, and serum sickness; stop immediately and initiate emergency therapy ([FDA label — Warnings, Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- *Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhoea and pseudomembranous colitis, up to >2 months post-therapy ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Stevens–Johnson syndrome, erythema multiforme, and toxic epidermal necrolysis (class effect) ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Hepatic dysfunction, cholestasis, and rare idiosyncratic hepatic failure ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Neutropenia, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopenia, and haemolytic or aplastic anaemia (class effect) ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Seizures (especially in unadjusted renal impairment) and renal dysfunction/toxic nephropathy (class effect) ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid, near-complete oral absorption via PEPT1; peak serum 16 mcg/mL (500 mg) and 28 mcg/mL (1000 mg). - **Distribution:** Apparent Vd ~23–25 L; low plasma protein binding (~15–28%); penetrates skin, lung, and pleural fluid. - **Metabolism:** Minimally metabolised. - **Elimination:** >90% excreted unchanged in urine within 24 h (peak urine ~1800 mcg/mL after 500 mg); ~63% dialysed in 6–8 h. - **Half-life:** ~1.13 h in healthy adults (~1.61 h in paediatric study); prolonged with probenecid (1.63 h) and renal impairment.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology, Overdosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [Mariño & Dominguez-Gil, 1981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6795136/), [Tan et al., 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8733657/), [AAC Pediatric PK, 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11064491/)

### Interactions
- Probenecid inhibits renal tubular secretion, prolonging cefadroxil serum half-life from 1.13 h to 1.63 h ([Mariño & Dominguez-Gil, 1981](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/6795136/)).
- Positive direct Coombs' test can interfere with cross-matching, haematologic studies, and newborn Coombs' testing ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Pregnancy
- Pregnancy Category B; animal studies up to 11× human dose showed no fetal harm, but human studies are lacking ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).
- Use during pregnancy or labour and delivery only if clearly needed ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display)).

### Breastfeeding
- FDA: exercise caution in nursing mothers; LactMed: low milk levels (0.4–0.9 mg/L after 500 mg), acceptable during breastfeeding ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=27009a05-4d2b-4618-be76-39f348240eda&type=display), [LactMed — Cefadroxil](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501292/)).
- Watch breastfed infants for occasional diarrhoea or thrush from gut flora disruption ([LactMed — Cefadroxil](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501292/)).

## Possible issues
- **Norfloxacin US discontinuation:** Norfloxacin is discontinued in the US and has no active DailyMed label; dosing and warnings were verified from the archived 2013 FDA Noroxin label and the updated 2025 Health Canada Product Monograph (mapped to `Paper`), alongside ICMR 2022 SBP guidance and NIH LactMed.
- **Ornidazole non-FDA status:** Ornidazole is not US FDA-approved and has no DailyMed label; dosing, safety, and PK were verified from the New Zealand Medsafe Data Sheet, Tanzania TMDA IV SmPC, Aristo Pharma India product page, peer-reviewed PK/safety studies, and NIH LactMed (all mapped to `Paper`).
- **India vs FDA dosing and age divergences:** Flagged inline for `ciprofloxacin` (infectious diarrhoea 3 d vs 5–7 d; intra-abdominal PO 750 mg vs 500 mg), `moxifloxacin` (paediatric MDR-TB use and BPaLM dairy separation), `co-trimoxazole` (HIV-exposed infant CPT from 6 weeks vs FDA <2 months contraindication; routine CBC monitoring vs none in NACO CPT), and `tinidazole` (BV single-dose 2 g vs multi-day FDA regimens; 1st-trimester contraindication in India/WHO vs insufficient data in FDA).
- **Pre-PLR FDA labels:** `erythromycin`, `co-trimoxazole` (oral), `ofloxacin`, and `cefadroxil` retain older pre-PLR FDA label structures without numbered `§` subsections; `cefadroxil` also lacks label-stated half-life, Vd, protein binding, and hepatic guidance, which were supplemented from peer-reviewed PK studies and NIH LiverTox/LactMed.
- **Indian brand aliases:** Brand aliases (`Ciplox`, `Levoflox`, `Zanocin`, `Moxicip`, `Norbactin`, `Claribid`, `Odoxil`, `Droxyl`, etc.) reflect standard Indian market names verified in pharmacy/manufacturer records rather than official CDSCO monographs; `Fasigyn` and `Ornida` were verified directly from Pfizer India and Aristo Pharma product documents.
