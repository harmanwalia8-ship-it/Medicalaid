## Drug: ampicillin
- **Name:** Ampicillin
- **Aliases:** `ampicillin sodium`, `ampicillin trihydrate`
- **Class:** Aminopenicillin (broad-spectrum beta-lactam antibiotic)
- **Uses:** Susceptible enterococcal, Listeria, streptococcal, respiratory, GI, UTI, sepsis, endocarditis, and meningitis infections.
- **Clearance:** Renal, Biliary

### Dose
- Respiratory or soft-tissue infection (≥40 kg): 250–500 mg IV/IM q6h ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- GI or genitourinary infection (≥40 kg): 500 mg IV/IM q6h ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Oral respiratory infection (>20 kg): 250 mg PO q6h, 30 min before or 2 h after meals ([FDA Cap — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Oral GI or genitourinary infection (>20 kg): 500 mg PO q6h on empty stomach ([FDA Cap — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Meningitis or septicaemia: 150–200 mg/kg/day IV divided q3–4h; severe endocarditis: 2 g IV q4h ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([ICMR IE — Empirical Therapy](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter13-therapy-ie-empirical-therapy.html))
- Give 1–2 g direct IV slowly over 10–15 min to avoid seizures ([FDA Inj — Directions for Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Children
- Neonates ≤34 wk GA, ≤7 d (meningitis/sepsis): 100 mg/kg/day IV/IM divided q12h ([FDA Inj — Table 1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Neonates ≤34 wk GA, 8–27 d (meningitis/sepsis): 150 mg/kg/day IV/IM divided q12h ([FDA Inj — Table 1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Neonates >34 wk GA, ≤28 d: FDA: 150 mg/kg/day IV/IM q8h; WHO: 50 mg/kg q12h wk 1, q8h >wk 1 ([FDA Inj — Table 1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([WHO eEML — Neonatal Meningitis](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/1263))
- Children <40 kg (respiratory/soft tissue): 25–50 mg/kg/day IV/IM divided q6–8h, max 2 g/day ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Children ≤20 kg (oral GI/GU): 100 mg/kg/day PO divided q6h, max 2 g/day ([FDA Cap — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Children >28 d (meningitis/sepsis/IE): 150–200 mg/kg/day IV divided q3–4h, max 12 g/day ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([ICMR IE — Empirical Therapy](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/chapter13-therapy-ie-empirical-therapy.html))
- **Dose:** Ampicillin — paediatric meningitis/sepsis (>28 d) | 150–200 mg/kg per day, in 6 divided doses q4h | max 12 g/day | IV ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- **Dose:** Ampicillin — paediatric respiratory/soft tissue (<40 kg) | 25–50 mg/kg per day, in 4 divided doses q6h | max 2 g/day | IV/IM ([FDA Inj — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Before starting
- Screen for beta-lactam hypersensitivity or prior ampicillin cholestatic jaundice ([FDA Cap — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Avoid in suspected infectious mononucleosis or lymphoid leukaemia due to high rash risk ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12892/smpc))
- Do not use bacteriostatic water for reconstitution in newborns ([FDA Inj — Directions for Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Obtain cultures before first dose; ineffective against penicillinase-producing bacteria ([FDA Cap — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))

### Monitoring
- Monitor renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function periodically during prolonged or high-dose therapy ([FDA Cap — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Observe for anaphylaxis for at least 30 min after first dose ([StatPearls — Outcomes](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519569/))
- Watch for watery or bloody diarrhoea up to 2+ months post-therapy ([FDA Inj — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Kidney
- CrCl <50 mL/min: 1–2 g IV q6–8h ([StatPearls — Dosage](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519569/))
- CrCl <30 mL/min: 1–2 g IV q8–12h ([StatPearls — Dosage](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519569/))
- CrCl <15 mL/min: 1–2 g IV q12–24h ([StatPearls — Dosage](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519569/))
- Removed by haemodialysis (give additional post-dialysis dose), not by peritoneal dialysis ([FDA Inj — Overdosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12892/smpc))

### Liver
- Contraindicated if prior history of ampicillin-associated cholestatic jaundice or hepatic dysfunction ([FDA Cap — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Monitor liver function at regular intervals in patients with hepatic impairment ([FDA Cap — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))

### Common side effects
- Diarrhoea, nausea, vomiting, glossitis, and stomatitis (more common with oral form) ([FDA Cap — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Maculopapular pruritic rash and urticaria ([FDA Cap — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Transient mild serum transaminase (AST/SGOT) elevation ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Serious side effects
- Anaphylaxis and angioedema (more frequent with parenteral route) ([FDA Inj — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions: SJS, TEN, DRESS, AGEP, exfoliative dermatitis ([FDA Cap — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- *Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhoea and pseudomembranous colitis ([FDA Inj — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Seizures, especially with rapid IV push or renal failure ([FDA Inj — Directions for Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Interstitial nephritis, hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, agranulocytosis, thrombocytopaenia, haemolytic anaemia ([EMC SmPC — §4.8](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12892/smpc))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability 30–55% (reduced by food); 500 mg PO Cmax ~3.0 mcg/mL. - **Distribution:** ~20% protein-bound; enters CSF only with inflamed meninges (13–14% of serum); high bile levels. - **Metabolism:** ~21% metabolised. - **Elimination:** Primarily renal tubular secretion (26–43% unchanged in urine) and active biliary secretion. - **Half-life:** 1.0–1.3 h (normal renal function); 8–20 h when CrCl <10 mL/min.
- **Citations:** [FDA Inj — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74), [FDA Cap — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954), [StatPearls — Pharmacokinetics](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK519569/)

### Interactions
- Allopurinol: substantially increases incidence of ampicillin skin rash ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))
- Probenecid: blocks renal tubular secretion, raising ampicillin levels and toxicity risk ([FDA Cap — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Aminoglycosides: never mix in same syringe or IV line (inactivates aminoglycoside) ([EMC SmPC — §4.5](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12892/smpc))
- Bacteriostatic antibiotics (tetracyclines, macrolides, chloramphenicol, sulfonamides): may antagonise bactericidal action ([FDA Cap — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- Combined oral contraceptives: may reduce efficacy and increase breakthrough bleeding ([FDA Cap — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=0a66c5c3-63bf-46e3-835e-26b555ab6954))
- False-positive urine glucose with copper-sulfate tests (use enzymatic glucose-oxidase tests) ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Pregnancy
- Animal studies show no teratogenicity; widely used in pregnancy when clinically indicated ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74)) ([EMC SmPC — §4.6](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/12892/smpc))
- Oral absorption is reduced during labour; use IV route intrapartum ([FDA Inj — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=64a04e8c-8f78-46b3-8f45-56ef225a4f74))

### Breastfeeding
- Low milk levels; acceptable in nursing mothers — watch infant for diarrhoea or thrush ([LactMed — Summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK500994/))

## Drug: cloxacillin
- **Name:** Cloxacillin
- **Aliases:** `cloxacillin sodium`
- **Class:** Penicillinase-resistant isoxazolyl penicillin (anti-staphylococcal beta-lactam)
- **Uses:** Methicillin-susceptible *Staphylococcus aureus* (MSSA) skin, soft-tissue, bone, joint, CNS, and bloodstream infections.
- **Clearance:** Renal, Hepatic

### Dose
- Mild-to-moderate oral infection: 250–500 mg PO q6h, 1–2 h before meals ([HC Oral — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Standard parenteral infection: 250–500 mg IV/IM q6h ([HC IV — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Severe MSSA infection (pyomyositis/CNS): India: 2 g IV q4–6h; Canada monograph: max 6 g/day ([ICMR — §9.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/9/5)) ([ICMR — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5)) ([HC IV — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Give IV bolus over 2–4 min or IV infusion over 30–40 min ([HC IV — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Continue at least 5 days (minimum 10 days for *S. pyogenes* infections) ([HC Oral — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Children
- Oral ≤5 kg: 250 mg/day PO divided q6h ([HC Oral — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Oral >5–40 kg: 50 mg/kg/day PO divided q6h, max 2 g/day ([HC Oral — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- IV/IM ≤20 kg (standard): 25–50 mg/kg/day IV/IM divided q6h, max 2 g/day ([HC IV — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Severe/CNS MSSA infection (children): 50 mg/kg IV q6h (200 mg/kg/day), max 8–12 g/day ([ICMR — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5))
- **Dose:** Cloxacillin — paediatric oral MSSA (>5–40 kg) | 50 mg/kg per day, in 4 divided doses q6h | max 2 g/day | PO fasting ([HC Oral — Dosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- **Dose:** Cloxacillin — paediatric severe/CNS MSSA | 50 mg/kg per dose q6h | max 2 g/dose | IV ([ICMR — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5))

### Before starting
- Contraindicated with history of allergic reactions to penicillins or cephalosporins ([HC Oral — Contraindications](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Ineffective against MRSA; obtain cultures before starting and de-escalate once susceptibility returns ([HC IV — Indications](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Use cautiously in premature and newborn infants due to limited safety data ([HC IV — Use in Children](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))

### Monitoring
- Check renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function periodically during long-term therapy ([HC Oral — Precautions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Monitor for neurotoxicity (myoclonus, seizures) with high IV doses in renal failure ([HC IV — Precautions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))

### Kidney
- Half-life lengthens from 25 min to 49 min in uraemia; avoid excessive accumulation ([HC Oral — Pharmacology](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- High doses in renal failure with inflamed meninges increase seizure risk ([HC IV — Precautions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))

### Liver
- Monitor hepatic function periodically during prolonged therapy; mild AST/SGOT elevations reported ([HC Oral — Precautions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Common side effects
- Nausea, vomiting, epigastric discomfort, flatulence, and loose stools ([HC Oral — Adverse Reactions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Rash, urticaria, pruritus, eosinophilia, and IV-site thrombophlebitis ([HC Oral — Adverse Reactions](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Serious side effects
- Anaphylaxis (occasionally fatal) ([HC Oral — Warnings](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Severe cutaneous adverse reactions: AGEP, DRESS, SJS, TEN ([HC Oral — Warnings](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))
- Neutropaenia, agranulocytosis, leukopaenia, thrombocytopaenia, and anaemia (usually reversible) ([HC IV — Adverse Effects](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00021521.PDF))
- Neurotoxicity: myoclonus, seizures, depressed consciousness with massive doses or renal failure ([HC Oral — Overdosage](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Rapid, incomplete oral absorption (delayed by food); fasting 500 mg capsule Cmax 8.5 mcg/mL, Tmax 0.88 h. - **Distribution:** 94% protein-bound; poor CNS penetration unless meninges inflamed; crosses placenta (~50% maternal plasma). - **Metabolism:** Partial hepatic metabolism. - **Elimination:** Urinary excretion 37% after 500 mg PO and 62% after 750 mg IV. - **Half-life:** 25 min (healthy volunteers); 49 min (uraemia).
- **Citations:** [HC Oral — Pharmacology](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF)

### Interactions
- Probenecid: enhances and prolongs cloxacillin serum concentrations ([HC Oral — Pharmacology](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Pregnancy
- Crosses placenta (~50% of maternal plasma); no rabbit teratogenicity, human safety unestablished ([HC Oral — Warnings](https://pdf.hres.ca/dpd_pm/00055444.PDF))

### Breastfeeding
- Low milk levels (0.1–0.3 mg/L); acceptable in nursing mothers — watch infant for diarrhoea/thrush ([LactMed — Summary](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK501639/))

## Drug: benzathine-benzylpenicillin
- **Name:** Benzathine benzylpenicillin
- **Aliases:** `benzathine penicillin G`, `penicillin G benzathine`, `Penidure LA 12`
- **Class:** Long-acting depot natural penicillin (beta-lactam antibiotic)
- **Uses:** First-line syphilis treatment, Group A streptococcal pharyngitis, and rheumatic fever secondary prophylaxis.
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Boxed warning
- Deep IM only; never inject IV or admix with IV solutions — causes cardiorespiratory arrest and death ([FDA label — Boxed Warning](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))

### Dose
- Early syphilis (primary, secondary, early latent): 2.4 million IU deep IM single dose ([India NACP 2024 — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13318318/)) ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- Late or unknown-duration syphilis: 2.4 million IU deep IM weekly × 3 doses (max 14-day gap) ([India NACP 2024 — Table 3](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13318318/)) ([WHO Syphilis — §4.2](https://hth-ksp.who.int/sti/guidelines/treatment-and-care/who-guidelines-for-the-treatment-of-treponema-pallidum-syphilis-1-jan-2016/content?id=794665494368.0286))
- GAS pharyngitis (adults): 1.2 million IU deep IM single dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- RF/RHD secondary prophylaxis: India: 1.2 million IU IM q21d; FDA: 1.2 million IU monthly or 600,000 IU q2wk ([Indian RHD PK — Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13152110/)) ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- Inject slowly into upper-outer gluteal quadrant or ventrogluteal site; avoid anterolateral thigh ([FDA label — Method of Administration](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- Lidocaine 1% may be added to reduce injection pain in RHD prophylaxis ([WHO RF/RHD — §4.7.2 Rec 7](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/fbd835e1-b9b7-414a-b6aa-9c279472bbdf/content))

### Children
- GAS pharyngitis (<27 kg / <60 lb): 300,000–600,000 IU deep IM single dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- GAS pharyngitis (older children): 900,000 IU deep IM single dose ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- RF/RHD prophylaxis: 600,000 IU IM q15d (<27 kg) or 1.2 million IU q21d (>27 kg) ([Indian RHD PK — Introduction](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13152110/))
- Congenital syphilis (<2 yr, asymptomatic/low-risk): 50,000 IU/kg deep IM single dose (max 2.4 million IU) ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16)) ([WHO Syphilis — §4.3 Rec 10](https://hth-ksp.who.int/sti/guidelines/treatment-and-care/who-guidelines-for-the-treatment-of-treponema-pallidum-syphilis-1-jan-2016/content?id=794665494368.0286))
- Use midlateral thigh in neonates, infants, and small children; vary sites if repeated ([FDA label — Method of Administration](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))

### Before starting
- Contraindicated in prior hypersensitivity to any penicillin; take careful beta-lactam allergy history ([FDA label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- Do not perform routine penicillin skin testing in patients without allergy history ([WHO RF/RHD — §4.7.2](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/fbd835e1-b9b7-414a-b6aa-9c279472bbdf/content))
- Keep emergency anaphylaxis kit (epinephrine, oxygen, IV steroids, airway equipment) immediately available ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16)) ([WHO RF/RHD — §4.7.3](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/fbd835e1-b9b7-414a-b6aa-9c279472bbdf/content))
- Consider oral penicillin instead in severe valvular RHD (mitral/aortic stenosis) due to vasovagal arrest risk ([WHO RF/RHD — §4.7.3](https://iris.who.int/server/api/core/bitstreams/fbd835e1-b9b7-414a-b6aa-9c279472bbdf/content))

### Monitoring
- Observe post-injection for immediate anaphylaxis, vasovagal syncope, or limb vascular compromise ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))
- Monitor syphilis patients for Jarisch-Herxheimer reaction and follow RPR/VDRL titres post-treatment ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16)) ([India NACP 2024 — Management](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13318318/))

### Kidney
- Excretion is markedly delayed in renal impairment and neonates; monitor renal function in elderly ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=012d46f1-d0a0-4676-a879-cd320297ab16))

### Common side effects

- Neutropenia, leukopenia, thrombocytopenia, decreased Hb/Hct, eosinophilia, positive direct Coombs test ([Pfizer India LPD — §4.8](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14800))

### Serious side effects

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Parenteral (IV/IM); IM peak concentrations occur at 15 min–2 h. - **Distribution:** Vd 18.0–27.6 L (sulbactam), 10.2–11.3 L (cefoperazone); high bile and tissue concentrations; does not displace bilirubin. - **Metabolism:** Minimal; no PK interaction between components. - **Elimination:** ~84% sulbactam and ~25% cefoperazone renal; remaining cefoperazone mainly biliary; both dialysable. - **Half-life:** ~1 h sulbactam (6.9–9.7 h anephric), ~1.7 h cefoperazone (2–4× longer in hepatic/biliary disease); children 0.91–1.42 h / 1.44–1.88 h.

### Pregnancy
- Both drugs cross placenta; no adequate human studies, no rat teratogenicity — use only if clearly needed ([Pfizer India LPD — §4.6](https://labeling.pfizer.com/ShowLabeling.aspx?id=14800))

## Drug: piperacillin-tazobactam
- **Name:** Piperacillin-tazobactam
- **Aliases:** `piperacillin sodium + tazobactam sodium`, `Tazact`, `Piptaz`
- **Class:** Antipseudomonal ureidopenicillin + beta-lactamase inhibitor
- **Uses:** Severe Gram-negative/polymicrobial infections, nosocomial pneumonia, intra-abdominal, pyelonephritis, necrotising fasciitis, febrile neutropenia.
- **Clearance:** Renal, Biliary

### Dose
- General severe infections (non-nosocomial pneumonia): India: 4.5 g IV q6–8h; FDA: 3.375 g IV q6h for 7–10 d ([ICMR 2019 — Tables 2.2, 4.4, 6.5, 9.3](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf)) ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))
- Nosocomial pneumonia: 4.5 g IV q6h (16 g piperacillin + 2 g tazobactam/day) plus aminoglycoside for 7–14 d ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))
- Avoid empiric use in septic shock until cephalosporin-resistant bacteraemia is excluded ([ICMR 2019 — Table 3.1](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf))
- Infuse IV over 30 minutes; tertiary-level essential medicine ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display)) ([NLEM 2022 — §6.2.1.13](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/Policy/policy_files/GNC509Q000158.pdf)) ([WHO eEML](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/320))

### Common side effects

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** IV only; peak immediately after 30-min infusion (4.5 g Cmax 298 mcg/mL piperacillin, 34 mcg/mL tazobactam). - **Distribution:** ~30% protein-bound (both); Vd 15–17 L; 50–100% plasma levels in lung, gut, gallbladder, bile; low CSF penetration without inflammation. - **Metabolism:** Minor active desethyl-piperacillin; single inactive tazobactam metabolite. - **Elimination:** Renal glomerular filtration and tubular secretion (68% piperacillin, 80% tazobactam unchanged in urine) plus biliary secretion; haemodialysis removes 30–40%. - **Half-life:** 0.7–1.2 h normal; at CrCl <20 mL/min, 2× piperacillin and 4× tazobactam.

- Aminoglycosides: inactivates aminoglycosides (especially tobramycin in haemodialysis); administer separately ([FDA label — §2.6, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))
- Probenecid: prolongs piperacillin half-life 21% and tazobactam 71%; avoid unless benefit outweighs risk ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))
- Heparin / oral anticoagulants: monitor coagulation parameters more frequently ([FDA label — §7.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))

- Lab tests: false-positive Platelia *Aspergillus* EIA and copper-reduction urine glucose ([FDA label — §7.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))

### Pregnancy
- Crosses placenta; insufficient human data; animal organogenesis studies show no structural abnormalities ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))

### Breastfeeding
- Piperacillin excreted in human milk (tazobactam unstudied); weigh maternal need against infant risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/fda/fdaDrugXsl.cfm?setid=9b682c66-61c6-4ade-baf4-9d68911a16b8&type=display))

## Possible issues
- **Cloxacillin (no active US FDA DailyMed label):** Cloxacillin is discontinued in the United States, so no active US FDA DailyMed label exists. Drug facts, PK, and dosing were verified from Health Canada official Product Monographs (oral and IV), ICMR AMRTG 2025, and LactMed (listed under `Paper` authority to fit the schema).
- **Cefoperazone–sulbactam (not US FDA-approved):** Not approved in the US and has no DailyMed label. Verified from Pfizer's official India Local Product Document (Magnex / Magnex Forte), Sulperazon LPD, and ICMR Treatment Guidelines (listed under `Paper` authority to fit the schema).
- **Cefoperazone–sulbactam and piperacillin–tazobactam paediatric `Dose` calculator lines omitted:** Both are fixed-dose beta-lactam + beta-lactamase inhibitor combinations (with 1:1 vs 1:2 vial ratios for cefoperazone–sulbactam), so paediatric dosing is provided as normal lines rather than single-drug `**Dose:**` calculator lines per Format A rules.
- **Ampicillin renal cut-offs:** The US FDA DailyMed labels do not give numeric CrCl cut-off tables for ampicillin; CrCl intervals (<50, <30, <15 mL/min) were sourced from StatPearls (NCBI Bookshelf) and haemodialysis guidance from the UK EMC SmPC.
- **Authority enum mapping:** Non-US regulatory monographs (Health Canada, UK EMC SmPC, Pfizer India LPD) and NCBI clinical references (StatPearls, LactMed) were mapped to `Paper` in the Sources table to stay strictly within the allowed `Authority` values.
