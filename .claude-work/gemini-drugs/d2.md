## Drug: meropenem
- **Name:** Meropenem
- **Aliases:** `Meronem`
- **Class:** Carbapenem (penem beta-lactam antibacterial)
- **Uses:** Severe complicated skin, intra-abdominal, paediatric meningitis, sepsis, and high-risk febrile neutropenia infections.
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- Complicated skin/skin-structure infections: 500 mg IV q8h; use 1 g IV q8h for *P. aeruginosa* ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Complicated intra-abdominal infections: 1 g IV q8h ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Pancreatic or pelvic/lower abdominal abscess: 1–2 g IV q8h with combination therapy ([ICMR STW — Intra-Abdominal Abscess](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1726644010_8_interventional_radiology_all.pdf))
- Infuse IV over 15–30 min; 1 g doses may be given as IV bolus over 3–5 min ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Tertiary-level essential medicine (Watch group); 500 mg and 1 g IV vials ([NLEM 2022 — §6.2.1.14](https://www.nitiforstates.gov.in/public-assets/Policy/policy_files/GNC509Q000158.pdf)) ([WHO eEML — Meropenem](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/329))

### Children
- ≥3 months, complicated skin infections (non-*P. aeruginosa*): 10 mg/kg IV q8h; max 500 mg/dose ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- ≥3 months, intra-abdominal or *P. aeruginosa* skin infections: 20 mg/kg IV q8h; max 1 g/dose ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- ≥3 months, bacterial meningitis: 40 mg/kg IV q8h; max 2 g/dose ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Weight >50 kg (≥3 mo): 500 mg q8h (cSSSI), 1 g q8h (cIAI/*P. aeruginosa*), 2 g q8h (meningitis) ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- <3 months, intra-abdominal (IV over 30 min): GA <32 wk & PNA <2 wk: 20 mg/kg q12h ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- <3 months, intra-abdominal: GA <32 wk & PNA ≥2 wk, or GA ≥32 wk & PNA <2 wk: 20 mg/kg q8h ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- <3 months, intra-abdominal: GA ≥32 wk & PNA ≥2 wk: 30 mg/kg IV q8h ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- **Dose:** Meropenem — cSSSI (≥3 months) | 10 mg/kg per dose q8h | max 500 mg | IV over 15–30 min ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- **Dose:** Meropenem — cIAI or P. aeruginosa cSSSI (≥3 months) | 20 mg/kg per dose q8h | max 1 g | IV over 15–30 min ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- **Dose:** Meropenem — bacterial meningitis (≥3 months) | 40 mg/kg per dose q8h | max 2 g | IV over 15–30 min ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Before starting
- Screen for beta-lactam hypersensitivity; contraindicated after beta-lactam anaphylaxis ([FDA label — §4, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Collect at least two sets of blood cultures before empiric sepsis therapy ([ICMR 2022 — Guidelines](https://bmhrc.ac.in/WriteReadData/News/202504070105301305292Treatmentguideantimicorbial.pdf))
- Check baseline CrCl, seizure history, CNS disorders, and concurrent valproic acid use ([FDA label — §2.2, §5.4, §5.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Monitoring
- Monitor renal function, platelets (in renal dysfunction), and neurological signs for seizures or myoclonus ([FDA label — §5.4, §5.9, §8.5](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- De-escalate once culture and susceptibility results return ([FDA label — §1.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6)) ([ICMR 2019 — Guidelines](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf))
- Watch for severe rash, rhabdomyolysis (muscle pain/dark urine), and diarrhoea up to >2 months post-therapy ([FDA label — §5.2, §5.3, §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Kidney
- CrCl >50 mL/min: standard dose (500 mg cSSSI; 1 g cIAI) q8h ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- CrCl 26–50 mL/min: standard dose q12h ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- CrCl 10–25 mL/min: half standard dose q12h ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- CrCl <10 mL/min: half standard dose q24h ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Haemodialysable, but inadequate label data for dialysis or paediatric renal impairment ([FDA label — §2.2, §2.3, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Liver
- No dose adjustment needed; hepatic impairment does not alter meropenem pharmacokinetics ([FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Common side effects
- Adults: diarrhoea (4.8%), nausea/vomiting (3.6%), injection-site inflammation (2.4%), headache (2.3%), rash (1.9%), constipation, pruritus ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Children ≥3 mo: diarrhoea (3.5–4.7%), rash/diaper moniliasis (1.6–3.1%), oral moniliasis (1.9%), glossitis ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Infants <3 mo: convulsions (5.0%), conjugated hyperbilirubinaemia (4.5%), vomiting (2.5%) ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Transient elevations in ALT, AST, alkaline phosphatase, bilirubin, creatinine, and eosinophils ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Serious side effects
- Anaphylaxis, angioedema, and SCAR (SJS, TEN, DRESS, erythema multiforme, AGEP) ([FDA label — §5.1, §5.2, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Rhabdomyolysis; stop immediately if muscle pain, dark urine, or elevated CK develops ([FDA label — §5.3, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Seizures (0.7% in non-CNS adult trials), delirium, and neuromotor impairment ([FDA label — §5.4, §5.10](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- *Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhoea and fatal colitis ([FDA label — §5.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Thrombocytopenia (in renal impairment), agranulocytosis, neutropenia, haemolytic anaemia, drug-induced liver injury ([FDA label — §5.9, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** IV only; 30-min infusion Cmax ~23 mcg/mL (500 mg), ~49 mcg/mL (1 g). - **Distribution:** ~2% protein bound; penetrates peritoneal fluid, bile, lung, skin, and inflamed CSF. - **Metabolism:** Minimal; one microbiologically inactive metabolite (~28% in urine). - **Elimination:** ~70% unchanged in urine within 12 h (filtration + tubular secretion); ~2% faecal; haemodialysable. - **Half-life:** ~1 h (adults); ~1.5 h (3 mo–2 yr); 1.58–3.82 h (<3 mo).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6)

### Interactions
- Valproic acid / divalproex: sharply lowers valproate levels, risking breakthrough seizures; avoid combination ([FDA label — §5.5, §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))
- Probenecid: inhibits renal tubular secretion, raising exposure 56% and half-life 38%; avoid ([FDA label — §7.1, §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Pregnancy
- Insufficient human data for birth-defect or miscarriage risk; no rat/monkey malformations at 2.3–2.4× MRHD ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

### Breastfeeding
- Excreted in human milk; no infant effect data — weigh maternal benefit against infant risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=31dd2160-7dbd-51fa-e054-00144ff88e88&version=6))

## Drug: imipenem-cilastatin
- **Name:** Imipenem-cilastatin
- **Aliases:** `imipenem + cilastatin`, `Imicrit`, `Cilanem`
- **Class:** Carbapenem + renal dehydropeptidase-I inhibitor
- **Uses:** Severe non-CNS polymicrobial or ESBL/multidrug-resistant bacterial infections; avoid in meningitis.
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- All doses are in imipenem equivalents (1:1 with cilastatin); maximum 4 g/day ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Susceptible pathogens (CrCl ≥90 mL/min): 500 mg IV q6h or 1000 mg IV q8h ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Intermediate-susceptibility pathogens (CrCl ≥90 mL/min): 1000 mg IV q6h ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Infuse ≤500 mg over 20–30 min and >500 mg over 40–60 min; slow rate if nausea occurs ([FDA label — §2.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Not indicated for meningitis; prefer meropenem for acute bacterial meningitis ([FDA label — §1.9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b)) ([WHO eEML — Rec 1215](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/1215))
- Watch-group alternative to meropenem for severe peritonitis, neutropenia, and MDR-TB (250/250, 500/500 mg vials) ([WHO eEML — Imipenem](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/600)) ([ICMR 2022 — Guidelines](https://bmhrc.ac.in/WriteReadData/News/202504070105301305292Treatmentguideantimicorbial.pdf))

### Children
- All doses are in imipenem equivalents for non-CNS infections only; maximum 4 g/day ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- ≥3 months: 15–25 mg/kg IV q6h in imipenem equivalents; max 4 g/day ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- 4 weeks–3 months (≥1,500 g): 25 mg/kg IV q6h in imipenem equivalents ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- 1–4 weeks (≥1,500 g): 25 mg/kg IV q8h in imipenem equivalents ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- <1 week (≥1,500 g): 25 mg/kg IV q12h in imipenem equivalents ([FDA label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Avoid in paediatric CNS infections (seizure risk) and children <30 kg with renal impairment ([FDA label — §1.9, §2.2, §8.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Before starting
- Screen for hypersensitivity to imipenem, cilastatin, penicillins, cephalosporins, or other beta-lactams ([FDA label — §4, §5.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Calculate steady-state CrCl; check CNS lesions, seizure history, valproic acid, or ganciclovir use ([FDA label — §2.3, §5.2, §5.3, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Collect at least two sets of blood cultures before empiric sepsis therapy ([ICMR 2022 — Guidelines](https://bmhrc.ac.in/WriteReadData/News/202504070105301305292Treatmentguideantimicorbial.pdf))

### Monitoring
- Monitor renal, hepatic, and haematopoietic function periodically; check CrCl regularly if ≤30 mL/min ([FDA label — §1.9, §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Watch for focal tremors, myoclonus, confusion, or seizures, especially in renal impairment or haemodialysis ([FDA label — §2.4, §5.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Tailor therapy to culture results; evaluate diarrhoea up to >2 months post-therapy ([FDA label — §1.10, §5.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b)) ([ICMR 2019 — Guidelines](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/custom_data/pdf/resource-guidelines/Treatment_Guidelines_2019_Final.pdf))

### Kidney
- Reduce dose when CrCl <90 mL/min (all doses in imipenem equivalents) ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- CrCl 60–89 mL/min: 400 mg q6h or 500 mg q6h (susceptible); 750 mg q8h (intermediate) ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- CrCl 30–59 mL/min: 300 mg q6h or 500 mg q8h (susceptible); 500 mg q6h (intermediate) ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- CrCl 15–29 mL/min: 200 mg q6h or 500 mg q12h (susceptible/intermediate); higher seizure risk ([FDA label — §2.3, §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- CrCl <15 mL/min: avoid unless haemodialysis starts within 48 h; inadequate data for peritoneal dialysis ([FDA label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Haemodialysis: use CrCl 15–29 mL/min dose after session only if benefit outweighs seizure risk ([FDA label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Liver
- No hepatic dose adjustment listed; assess hepatic function periodically during prolonged therapy ([FDA label — §1.9](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Common side effects
- Adults: phlebitis/thrombophlebitis (3.1%), nausea (2%), diarrhoea (1.8%), vomiting (1.5%), rash (0.9%), fever ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Children ≥3 mo: diarrhoea (3.9%), phlebitis (2.2%), rash (2.2%), vomiting, urine discoloration ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Neonates to 3 mo: convulsions (5.9%), diarrhoea (3%), oliguria/anuria (2.2%), rash, candidiasis, tachycardia ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Transient increases in ALT, AST, ALP, bilirubin, creatinine, eosinophils, and positive Coombs test ([FDA label — §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Serious side effects
- Seizures (0.4% adults; higher if CrCl ≤30 mL/min or overdosed), encephalopathy, confusion, myoclonus ([FDA label — §5.2, §6.1, §8.6](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Anaphylaxis, angioedema, Stevens-Johnson syndrome, toxic epidermal necrolysis, erythema multiforme ([FDA label — §5.1, §6.1, §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- *Clostridioides difficile*-associated diarrhoea, pseudomembranous colitis, and haemorrhagic colitis ([FDA label — §5.4, §6.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Fulminant hepatitis, hepatic failure, acute renal failure, pancytopenia, agranulocytosis, haemolytic anaemia ([FDA label — §6.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** IV only; 20-min infusion imipenem Cmax 21–58 mcg/mL (500 mg), 41–83 mcg/mL (1 g). - **Distribution:** Protein binding ~20% (imipenem), ~40% (cilastatin); penetrates lung, peritoneal fluid, bile, bone, skin, CSF. - **Metabolism:** Cilastatin inhibits renal dehydropeptidase-I, preventing renal inactivation of imipenem. - **Elimination:** ~70% of both imipenem and cilastatin recovered in urine within 10 h; both haemodialysable. - **Half-life:** ~1 h for both components (elderly: 91 ± 7 min imipenem, 69 ± 15 min cilastatin).
- **Citations:** [FDA label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b)

### Interactions
- Valproic acid / divalproex: drops valproate below therapeutic range, risking seizures; avoid combination ([FDA label — §5.3, §7.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Ganciclovir: generalised seizures reported with co-administration; avoid unless benefit outweighs risk ([FDA label — §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))
- Probenecid: increases imipenem plasma level and half-life; co-administration not recommended ([FDA label — §7.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Pregnancy
- Insufficient human data; increased embryonic loss in monkeys at ~0.6× human dose ([FDA label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

### Breastfeeding
- Insufficient human milk excretion or infant effect data; weigh maternal benefit against risk ([FDA label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=46f54df9-5afe-430c-aa19-0d84c2044b0b))

## Drug: ertapenem
- **Name:** Ertapenem
- **Aliases:** `Invanz`, `Lyortam`, `Gerta`
- **Class:** Carbapenem (1-beta-methyl carbapenem) antibacterial
- **Uses:** Complicated intra-abdominal, skin, urinary, pelvic infections, CAP, and colorectal prophylaxis (no *Pseudomonas* coverage).
- **Clearance:** Renal, Biliary

### Dose

### Breastfeeding

## Drug: vancomycin
- **Name:** Vancomycin
- **Aliases:** `vancomycin hydrochloride`, `Vanlid`, `Vancogen`, `Vancocin-CP`
- **Class:** Glycopeptide antibacterial
- **Uses:** Serious MRSA and Gram-positive infections (IV); *Clostridioides difficile* colitis and staphylococcal enterocolitis (oral).
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- India/IDSA IV: 15–20 mg/kg q8–12h (actual weight); FDA IV: 500 mg q6h or 1 g q12h ([ICMR STW 2024 — MRSA](https://www.icmr.gov.in/icmrobject/uploads/STWs/1725967368_bacterial_skin_infections.pdf)) ([IDSA/ASHP 2020 — Rec 9](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7358040/)) ([FDA IV label — §2.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))

- Fulminant *C. difficile*: 500 mg PO/NG QID plus IV metronidazole 500 mg q8h; add rectal if ileus ([IDSA CDI 2018 — Rec XXX](https://www.idsociety.org/practice-guideline/clostridium-difficile/)) ([NICE NG199 — §1.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/ng199/chapter/Recommendations))

- Reconstituted IV powder may be diluted for oral/NG use where capsules are unavailable ([FDA IV label — §2.8](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31)) ([WHO eEML — Vancomycin Oral](https://list.essentialmeds.org/recommendations/679))

### Children

- Children <18 yr (*C. difficile* or staphylococcal enterocolitis): 40 mg/kg/day PO in 3–4 doses, max 2 g/day ([FDA IV label — §2.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))

### Kidney
- Give initial IV dose ≥15 mg/kg in adults with any degree of renal impairment ([FDA IV label — §2.4](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))

### Common side effects

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Poorly absorbed orally (urinary recovery ≤0.76%; faecal >3,100 mg/kg), though measurable levels occur in active colitis. - **Distribution:** Vd 0.3–0.43 L/kg; ~55% protein bound; enters pleural, pericardial, ascitic, synovial fluids, and inflamed meninges. - **Metabolism:** No apparent metabolism. - **Elimination:** ~75% of IV dose excreted unchanged in urine by glomerular filtration within 24 h; renal CL 0.048 L/kg/h. - **Half-life:** 4–6 hours (normal renal function); 7.5 days in anephric patients.
- **Citations:** [FDA IV label — §12.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31)

- Other nephrotoxic or ototoxic drugs (aminoglycosides): additive toxicity; monitor renal function frequently ([FDA IV label — §5.2, §7.3](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))
- Intravenous anaesthetics: increased erythema and histamine flushing; infuse vancomycin prior to induction ([FDA IV label — §2.1, §7.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))
- Beta-lactam IV solutions: physical precipitation; flush IV lines thoroughly between administrations ([FDA IV label — §2.7](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))

### Pregnancy
- Second/third-trimester IV data show no infant hearing loss or nephrotoxicity; first-trimester data lacking ([FDA IV label — §8.1](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31))

### Breastfeeding
- Present in human milk after IV use (levels unquantified); oral infant exposure unlikely — weigh benefit/risk ([FDA IV label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c757e65b-ca6f-4c00-afe2-025962b43d31)) ([FDA Oral label — §8.2](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=3707ad2e-5c82-4111-a370-e518e5e7e4c5))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Parenteral (IV/IM); peak serum levels at 10 min after IV dose. - **Distribution:** ~50% protein bound; poor CSF penetration (~5% serum; 34–67% in meningitis). - **Metabolism:** Inactive prodrug (CMS) converts slowly in vivo to active colistin. - **Elimination:** CMS mainly renally excreted; high urinary active colistin (270 to 15 mcg/mL over 2–8 h). - **Half-life:** 2–3 h (CMS) after IV or IM in adults, children, and premature infants.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/lookup.cfm?setid=6d6e13e2-dc33-4b7b-84d0-66146a57c552), [Polymyxin Consensus 2019 — §II, §III, §XX](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7437259/)

### Pregnancy

## Drug: fosfomycin
- **Name:** Fosfomycin
- **Aliases:** `fosfomycin tromethamine`, `fosfomycin disodium`, `Monurol`, `Fomicyt`
- **Class:** Phosphonic acid antibacterial
- **Uses:** Oral single-dose for uncomplicated cystitis; IV combination therapy for MDR Gram-negative infections.
- **Clearance:** Renal

### Dose
- Oral (uncomplicated cystitis, women ≥18 yr): 3 g sachet dissolved in 90–120 mL cool water once ([FDA label — Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([ICMR 2022 — CA-UTI](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/chapter10-catheter-uti-antimicrobial-therapy.html))
- Never take dry powder or repeat daily oral doses for a single cystitis episode; avoid in pyelonephritis ([FDA label — Indications, Dosage](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))
- IV (≥12 yr, ≥40 kg, CrCl >80 mL/min): 12–24 g/day in 2–3 divided doses (max 8 g/dose) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV meningitis/MDR: 16–24 g/day in 3–4 doses; India: 4 g IV q6h (MDR 4–6 g q6h) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc)) ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5)) ([ICMR 2022 — MDR Dosage](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/cro-recommendations-dosage.html))
- Infuse IV over ≥15 min (2 g), ≥30 min (4 g), ≥60 min (8 g); avoid saline diluents ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2, §6.6](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- Use IV fosfomycin in combination regimens to prevent rapid resistance selection ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc)) ([ICMR 2022 — CRE](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/archive/2022/cro-recommendations-directed-therapy-carbapenem-enterobacterales.html))
- IV is Reserve-group complementary essential medicine; oral is Watch-group (not on EML) ([WHO eEML — Fosfomycin IV](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/343)) ([WHO eEML — Fosfomycin Oral](https://list.essentialmeds.org/medicines/869))

### Children
- Oral: safety and effectiveness not established in children ≤12 years ([FDA label — Pediatric Use](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))
- IV premature neonates (<40 wk PMA): 100 mg/kg/day in 2 divided doses; term (40–44 wk PMA): 200 mg/kg/day in 3 doses ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV infants 1–12 mo (≤10 kg): 200–300 mg/kg/day in 3 divided doses ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV children 1–11 yr (10–<40 kg): 200–400 mg/kg/day in 3–4 divided doses ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV CNS infections (children): 75–100 mg/kg/dose q6h ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5))
- No IV dosing recommendations exist for children with renal impairment ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- **Dose:** Fosfomycin IV — infants 1–12 mo (≤10 kg) | 200–300 mg/kg per day, in 3 divided doses | max — | IV ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- **Dose:** Fosfomycin IV — children 1–11 yr (10–<40 kg) | 200–400 mg/kg per day, in 3–4 divided doses | max — | IV ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- **Dose:** Fosfomycin IV — CNS infections (children) | 75–100 mg/kg per dose q6h | max — | IV ([ICMR AMRTG 2025 — §2.5](https://amrtg.icmr.org.in/section-info/2/5))

### Before starting
- Contraindicated in known hypersensitivity to fosfomycin or excipients ([FDA label — Contraindications](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.3](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV disodium salt contains 320 mg (14 mmol) sodium/g; assess heart failure, cirrhosis, hypertension, oedema risk ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- Obtain baseline cultures and calculate Cockcroft-Gault CrCl before starting IV therapy ([FDA label — Precautions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Monitoring
- IV: monitor serum sodium and potassium regularly for hypernatraemia, fluid overload, and hypokalaemia ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV: monitor leukocyte count regularly for neutropenia or agranulocytosis ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- Evaluate diarrhoea during or >2 months after therapy for *C. difficile* colitis ([FDA label — Warnings](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Kidney
- Oral: CrCl 7–54 mL/min prolongs t½ to 11–50 h and lowers urine recovery; no label dose change ([FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))
- IV CrCl 40–80 mL/min: no routine adjustment; use caution at higher doses ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV CrCl <40 mL/min: 70% at 40, 60% at 30, 40% at 20, 20% at 10 mL/min; double first loading dose (max 8 g) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- Chronic haemodialysis (q48h): 2 g IV after each session; post-dilution CVVHF: no adjustment ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Liver
- IV: no dose adjustment required in hepatic impairment ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.2, §5.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Common side effects
- Oral: diarrhoea (9.0%), vaginitis (5.5%), nausea (4.1%), headache (3.9%), dizziness (1.3%), asthenia, dyspepsia ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))
- IV: hypernatraemia, hypokalaemia, dysgeusia, erythematous skin eruption, injection-site phlebitis (1–10%) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.8](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Serious side effects
- Anaphylaxis, anaphylactic shock, angioedema, and *C. difficile* colitis (including toxic megacolon) ([FDA label — Warnings, Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4, §4.8](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- IV: severe hypernatraemia/fluid overload (seizures, coma), severe hypokalaemia (arrhythmias), agranulocytosis, neutropenia, thrombocytopenia ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.4, §4.8](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))
- Rare: hepatitis, cholestatic jaundice, hepatic necrosis, aplastic anaemia, unilateral optic neuritis ([FDA label — Adverse Reactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.8](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Pharmacokinetics
- **Absorption:** Oral bioavailability 37% fasting (30% fed); Cmax 26.1 mcg/mL in 2 h. - **Distribution:** Negligible protein binding; Vss 136 L (oral) / 0.30 L/kg (IV); CSF 20–50% in meningitis. - **Metabolism:** Not metabolised by liver; no enterohepatic circulation. - **Elimination:** Excreted unchanged renally (IV 80–90% in 12 h; oral 38% urine, 18% faeces); dialysable. - **Half-life:** Oral 5.7 h (11–50 h in renal impairment); IV ~2 h healthy, 3.6–3.8 h critically ill, ~4 h HD.
- **Citations:** [FDA label — Clinical Pharmacology](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7), [UK EMC SmPC — §5.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc)

### Interactions
- Metoclopramide (and other prokinetics): lowers oral fosfomycin serum levels and urinary excretion; avoid ([FDA label — Drug Interactions](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))
- Oral anticoagulants: monitor INR during therapy due to antibiotic-associated INR imbalance ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.5](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Pregnancy
- Crosses placenta; animal studies show no teratogenicity — use only if benefit outweighs risk ([FDA label — Pregnancy](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7)) ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.6](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc))

### Breastfeeding
- Low amounts (~8% of serum) enter milk; avoid as first choice, especially with premature/newborn infants ([UK EMC SmPC — §4.6, §5.2](https://www.medicines.org.uk/emc/product/100356/smpc)) ([FDA label — Nursing Mothers](https://dailymed.nlm.nih.gov/dailymed/drugInfo.cfm?setid=c3ba67e7-aaca-e682-277a-94e20b0abad7))

## Possible issues
- **Teicoplanin and IV fosfomycin (no US FDA DailyMed labels):** Teicoplanin and IV fosfomycin disodium are not US FDA-approved; regulatory dosing, safety, and PK were verified via UK EMC SmPCs (`dr-d2-teico-emc`, `dr-d2-fos-iv-emc`) and Sanofi India PI (`dr-d2-teico-sanofi-in`), mapped to `Paper` to stay within the allowed Authority enum.
- **Polymyxin B FDA vs modern PK renal conflict:** The US FDA label (`dr-d2-polyb-fda`) recommends reducing IV doses in renal impairment, whereas the 2019 International Consensus Guidelines (`dr-d2-idsa-poly`) and PopPK data (`dr-d2-polyb-pk`) show predominantly non-renal clearance (<5% urinary recovery) and advise NO renal or CRRT dose reduction; both are shown explicitly.
- **Gentamicin and amikacin once-daily vs divided dosing:** Indian guidelines (ICMR/NCDC) and WHO Pocket Book recommend extended-interval once-daily dosing, while legacy US FDA labels list divided q8–12h dosing and conventional peak/trough TDM cut-offs; both are contrasted side by side. Adult numeric gentamicin half-life is omitted because the FDA adult label does not state one.
- **Vancomycin TDM divergence:** ASHP/IDSA 2020 (`dr-d2-idsa-vanc`) recommends AUC/MIC 400–600 mg·h/L over trough-only monitoring for serious MRSA, whereas FDA and ICMR 2019 still reference trough-guided adjustment (relevant where Bayesian AUC software is unavailable in India).
- **Teicoplanin renal cut-offs and MRSA CAP dosing:** Sanofi India PI (`dr-d2-teico-sanofi-in`) uses CrCl 40–60 / <40 mL/min vs UK EMC SmPC CrCl 30–80 / <30 mL/min, and ICMR AMRTG 2025 §12.5 uses 400 mg q12h × 3 then 10 mg/kg OD vs UK 6 mg/kg; both are included.
- **Omitted optional sections:** `### Liver` is omitted for `gentamicin`, `amikacin`, `vancomycin`, `teicoplanin`, `colistin`, and `polymyxin-b` (no hepatic dose adjustment in primary sources), and `### Breastfeeding` is omitted for `polymyxin-b` (not addressed in FDA/ICMR/Consensus sources).
